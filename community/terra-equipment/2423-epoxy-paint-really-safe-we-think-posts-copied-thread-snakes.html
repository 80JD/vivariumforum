Just a quick note for future thoughts--merely covering the pine with moss or whatever will NOT prevent the phenols from outgasing into the atmosphere in the snake's enclosure. Unless you _completely_ covered the pine very thoroughly with silicon, the danger still exists, and if you missed some spots--which would be very easy to do, the danger would still exist. Also, for all I know, the phenols could outgas _through_ the silicon. So, it's just better not to use pine or cedar at all.

OK, Meghan, about the epoxy--a chemistry professor friend of mine said you need to do the epoxy outside or in a well-ventilated room where you can leave it to cure and don't put any live animal in it for several months because it outgasses for a long time.

Also, are you aware that epoxy has bisphenol A (BPA) in it? That's the chemical that has been put in some plastics (e.g., polycarbonates/#7) such as baby bottles that the government of Canada has now banned. A lot of people in the U.S. are trying to get the FDA to ban it as well--it interacts with hormones and reduces fertility, and it has been implicated in some cancers. My son made me throw away all my plastic containers with BPA in them because it leaches out into the food or water or other liquid.

In addition, epoxy is not UV-resistant and will break down in sunlight. If the lamps you plan to use in the enclosure will produce UV, epoxy isn't a good choice.

Anyway, my friend wonders why you don't look into another means of sealing the wood, such as marine varnish (spar varnish) or another polyurethane. Spar varnish dries more completely and is UV-resistant, too.

Whatever you consider, look into any possible health issues, degradation resistance, etc. Good luck!
