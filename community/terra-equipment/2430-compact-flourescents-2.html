I believe the spiral compacts are far more efficient than "normal" tube flourescents. At lot more light for the power used.

Long tube flourescents come in several diameters. t-12, t-8, and t5. T-12 is the old school standard shoplight diameter. T-8 is more efficient. T-5 is more efficient still. However, you NEED a fixture specifically for t-5, or buy a parts conversion kit to retrofit a fixture to use t-5 bulbs.

The t-12 and t-8 are cheap- But t-5 is expensive. Can find t-5's at aquarium stores. They do use less electricity than others, and have a great deal of light output compaired to the other tube flouro's.

Spirals are very inexpensive in comparison, and the small size makes fitting them on small/odd shaped tanks easy. Even for a larger tank using mutliple spirals, the cost is far less than a t-5 setup. I would expect t-5 uses a little less juice than similar spirals, and has more light output as well- however the cost and I have a corner tank, convinced me to use spirals. I feel I have plenty of light.

The other advantage to tube flouros, is if you setup a rack system with multiple 10 or 20 gal tanks. Put all the tanks next to each other, and the 48" light accross all of them. 1 light for 3 or 4 tanks. Instead of 1 light per tank.

It really depends on what your setup is.

YOu don't have to use clamp lamps for CFLs, they are just the easiest to find and the most common. You can easily build your own light hood (either buy fixtures and wire them in yourself, or use the fixtures from your clamp lamps!) that will look gorgeous and actually produce better light than the clamp lamps (especially if you paint the inside white). Personally, I prefer hoods (wither bought or built) that have fixtures for incandescents AND fixtures for tubes. Eventually I plan to have 4 incandescent fixtures and two tube fixtures for my CWD. I may or may not have the incandescent fixtures pointing downward (allowing me to use CHEs OR regular bulbs [or even CFLs]).

Anyway, I guess what I'm saying is that it depends on the needs of your animal (I need the tubes for UV lights), your wiring skills, and your skills at building a wooden box, lol.

Hmm, in my experience, white surfaces have reflected more light into the vives than metal (aluminum foil or metal clamp lamp dome). I am basing this on the vive looking brighter with the white being used for reflection. However, I find that when using them for heat as well as light, it is best to go with the metal, because it reflects heat better than the white. I haven't been able to make a foil box like the white I have used though, so my experience may not be the norm. I might have to see which is better when I build my light box.
