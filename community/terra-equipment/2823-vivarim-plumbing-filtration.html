Vivarim Plumbing and filtration.
Hello every one! My name is Joe, and I am a new Viv. Builder. I have a 55 gal long, which I house my CWD in, and would like some feed back about my plumbing idea.

I went to Hobby Lobby last week, and they didn't know what 'Egg Crate" is, so I bought some plexiglass instead. The plexy glass will sit on one and a half inch inch PVC tubing, to allow water to pass. (Big rocks will stop the Dragon from going under the substrate). So I will have a water pool, as well as a filter on the other end. The filter will then circulate the water from a tube into a stream, that will flow back into the pool after going down two water falls, and hitting rocks.

This provides the following:
  • Circulating water that releases Nitrates and Phosphates (sp)
  • Filtration through Osmosis, Coal, and light

I have no water or dirt in there right now, but will do this before the weekend is over. I have heard of every one using clay balls, but I thought that would cause smell and a nasty build up after a while.

Please let me know if you think this will work before I build it and mess everything up

as far as i see black light bulbs are the best sources for uva... not a good choice at all imo. also you should not have to supplement uva as it should be supplied by any full spectrum, daylight, sunlight, or growing bulbs. uvb should be supplemented for any diurnal species (awake during the day). the amount will vary depending on what climate they come from and their habits. for instance a bearded dragon is from the desert therefore they will need more uvb (this indicates a stronger bulb). frogs on the other hand rarely live to far from water and are usually prey to larger animals so stay out of the light more often... less uvb if any considering how many species are nocturnal.

UVC... this is the light commonly used by your local water source to filter out anything living in your water. you can buy little systems called uv sterilizers for an aquarium. these systems range from a 5 watt bulb for around $60 to "wow... and that kills everything in the water for the whole town?". it is highly recommended not to look at this light as the rays it produces kills just about anything living. the way we use it is we stick it in a housing to block the light from getting out. we seal that housing so it doesn't leak. then we run tubes and a pump to run water through it. when the water passes through the light for a long enough period it will kill algae. double or triple that time and it kills parasites. this light is only effective through water for a very short distance so sterilizers are always pretty thin.

i am currently running a kinda homemade version on my planted tank. my water is always sparkling clear . i have also not had anything spread through this tank even after moving fish from my other tank that i found a day later to be covered with ick!

coralife makes a series that always gets good reviews and their bulbs from what i've read are some of the best on the market. here is a link to a google shopping page to give you an idea: uv sterilizer - Google Product Search
