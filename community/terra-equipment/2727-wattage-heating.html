Ambient temperatures are going to make this number hard to nail down as does type of bulb etc. I have seen many snakes an lizards fried by a 100w bulb. This is just not where you should cut cost.

This is only my opinion. Others may have totally different opinions based on up to date info.

due to human error, shipping, electrical properties, manufacturers constantly changing/improving product line... this info you ask will be not useful at all or for long. plus when the bulbs get older they will tend to change their temps.

i agree w/both of you... it would be nice to know as it would help determine what bulb to buy... and relying on average temp for a certain model will eventually turn disastrous.

i'd say borrow a neighbors/friends thermometer to set your bulb regardless... and look to get one in the near future. it doesn't have to be super accurate/expensive... it just needs to go above necessary temp. i have used meat thermometers several times! mom wasn't so happy the first time, but she got over that almost 20 yrs ago.

if this thread works out and people start posting temps... i'd be happy to test what i have... it may take a while... but it could help others with initial purchases, and that is when your pets put the biggest strain on your pocket.
