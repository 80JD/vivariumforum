I have been using epoxy for over 25 years primarily as a glue but also for coating various submerged objects like wood, polystyrene, tree roots polyurethane foam etc.
I worked on a commercial & tourist attraction "koi farm" for many years & we used epoxy to make many decorative back drops in the glass fronted display tanks.
I am not a scientist & I cant say we never had any fish deaths but personally I would not concern myself about using epoxy in a viv or fish tank.
However epoxy is not the easiest product to use, you need ideal conditions & accurate mixing to get good results.
For the back drops I make, I normally use polyurethane products to coat the foam, G4* is my favorite. I also use polyester products like fiberglass resin & gell coats. Polyester mixed with a catalyst is very fast curring - 10 mins to 10 hours depending on amounts of catalyst so you get very quick results.
Epoxy is great for coating polystyrene because unlike many other coating it will not melt the polystyrene foam.

*G4 Pond Seal is a moisture-cured polyurethane, which forms a non-porous seal G4 uses the moisture in the air and substrate to cure or harden; it can be applied onto slightly damp substrates. However, it is important that the substrate is sufficiently dry to be porous so as to enable the first coat of G4 to bond and obtain a mechanical grip.
G4 can be applied in poor weather conditions, high humidity and at temperatures down to
0°C.
G4 seals out lime.
G4 is available in standard (browny translucent) — Mid Green — Black.*

been a long time since I posted here. The viv that I built is running great with no problems It was coated with 2 part tub and tile epoxy from klenks.
So far no fish or reptile has been harmed. I definetly would use it again make sure you have lots ventilation, it is powerfull and you'll get loopy. let it cure then seal the corners with fish tank silicon. let cure, wash, rinse, decorate and enjoy.
