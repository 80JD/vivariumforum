(1) You need to pick a pump that suits the volume of water you wish to turn over per hour, if you want to turn over the entire volume of water 10 times per hour a pump rated at 180gph is required, with that in mind you need to find a bulkhead what would handle this kind of outflow and not cause the pump to work to hard, A 1/2" to 3/4: BH should handle this kind of flow ( double check required ), but your return has to be the same size BH and pump but keep in mind not alot of people have had much luck with the 2 pump 2 valve method and tend to lean twords a standpipe ( gravity feed line ) and single return, reason being is that if one pump fails your in trouble. With a Durso your water only drains past the lip and no further..

( 2 ) At 5" water depth your BH should be as low as possable without getting to close to the solicone seal, you will need a solid 1" to avoid a brittle pane and cracking while drilling or fitting you BH, and remember you have to not only account for the entire volume of the water above the sump ( in this case the bottom lip of the BH )as well as the amount of water in the sump + the volume within the lines that go in and out as thay will drain back. Half of 18.7 is 9.35 gallons + the volume of each line we will juat say is 1/2 a G each totaling 10.35 gallons.remember the water will have to fill the lines before it reachs the sump and main tank, this 1/2g in and then 1/2g out can be tricky. i hope this makes sence.

( 3 ) Control valves are good if you take the time to set them just right and hope one avoids a clog, been there done that lol, You can go from a return thats pushing 20gph to 1/4ghp in no time if not kept in check get a life jacket for your pets heh

( 4 ) Biological - Ceramic Cylinders or Bio Balls. Chemical -Carbon. Mechanical -Filter Floss or Sponge. Depends on your sump design ad how many compartments you have or if just useing filters you already have but should go B-C-M anyhoo imho.

( 5 ) dosent matter as long as your not restricting the return pump and it puts in exactly what it taken out , well i guess it does matter just dont make it less then the feed line

( 6 ) close the valves and Git'er done, use a black marker on your sump to mark the max fill line when the entire unit is at full volume.

Before I "chime in" here I have a couple of suggestions:


If you've never done anything like this before you need to go really slowly before drilling any holes.

Really take time to think about what you want.

Create an ideal list of what you want to have in your sump (what sections you want and how the water will flow)

The whole purpose of a sump is to automate or render maintenance on the viv much easier.



Without going into any specifics here are some suggestions you might want to consider:

I would not make a hole in the side of the tank, I would do it in one of the bottom rear corners. Use a riser tube cut to the right height to control the water level height. This virtually eliminates clogging.

Use rigid tubing not flex hose. Hose will bend in time and block.

Use snap in connectors to facilitate connecting and disconnecting lines where necessary.

Use valves on each end of all your plumbing. You won't need to prime the pipes if the sump is stopped for any reason. There should be some small section of transparent tubing so you can see if the line is primed.

The sump should be able to hold all the water in your viv (or vivs). This way it can never overflow. Keep in mind a single sump can filter more than just one viv. (you may want to add vivs to this sump later on)

Make the sump as long as the tank if you have the room. Longer sumps mean more room for different compartments in the sump.

Plants may be used in sumps to filter ammonia. Try to set the sump where you will be able to provide flourescent T5 fixtures above it.


Honestly, have more suggestions but that's just for starters.

Aye, you want to be sure the sump can handle the entire volume below the overflow, once you have it set up and running the way you want grab a bucket (or in you case a cup would do seeing its not a full tank) and then simulate a power outage and see what happens to the water in the main tank and sump, once water no longer drains from the main tank and the sump is as full as its gunna get thats where you put your mark as a reference, then start up and get it running properly again and make a new mark at the water lvl in your sump at normal operation. This difference between the black lines will tell you how much water is divided between your main tank and lines, the second is what you use as a max fill line. GO slow with GG suggestion of a riser tube, this will need some tweeking for if you cut it to low you get to much water and a bothersome gargling sound and its functionality is dependant on the flow you require outbound. ** CAUTION **---> As for drilling your tanks bottom pane i would be really carefull with this as we only know how many gallons of water your going to have in there and not the total size of your tank or the make, if the base it made of tempered glass and you drill it KABOOM, of this you can be sure..
