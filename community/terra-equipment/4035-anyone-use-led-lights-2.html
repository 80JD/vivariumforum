There are UV LED's 250-385NM and others too. the big thing is that most LEDs are a focused light not like a cfl or t5 bulb that projects light in all directions. They are more like a spot light then a flood light. You can get varied angles of light based on the type of LED you get. You'll notice it's rated at viewing angle. Every day newer leds are hitting the market covering more of the light spectrum and brighter also. Big thing is if you build a UV LED light source you need to do two things. #1 have a base UV rating of a standard CFL/t5 bulb as a reference. #2 Build up with the # of LEDS you are using. In other words start it with a few leds and take a reading, then add LED's as need to approach the desired UV rating but never exceeding it. You could easily blind your pet or even yourself if your not careful. As for the LED drivers or even simply the right size resistor to put in-line with the led based on the power source, you can google it and there is many DIY sites based around LED's and such.
Hope this helps.

Well LED's are rated like light bulbs, in the referance of their output in MFD ect. So as to say I could get 1 LED that puts out the same if not more then 3 LEDs. The thing with LEDS you have concider is a few things, #1 The MFD (brightness), #2 The viewing angle in degrees, #3 Their Max operating forward voltage (They are diodes), if you run one at 1/2 it's rated FV it will only put out 1/2 the MFD but will last longer, on the other hand running it at full FV or near it, it will put out the rated MFD but shorten the life of the LEDs. Most Mfgr's run the LED's at or around 3/4 of their FV to make the LED last long and still put out alot of light.
