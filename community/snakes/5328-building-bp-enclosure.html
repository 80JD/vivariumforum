Not exactly a fair characterization!
You must understand, ball pythons spend most of their time in the wild hiding inside termite mounds, and rarely come out, other than to find mates, or perhaps water--most food comes to them, as they are ambush predators. If you want to build a naturalistic enclosure that looks like the inside of a termite mound, well, that's cool...but most of us use racks because they do the trick nicely. Dark, private, and enclosed. Sticks, rocks, live plants...nice, but not really ball python habitat. They live in open forest and savannah areas--dry brush and grasses most of the year, and they don't spend a lot of time out in the open.

The pretty vivarium with the nice pleasing branches and rocks and plants--that is mostly nice for you. They just want lots of cover to hide under, and a good tight-fitting hide.

A ball python living in a rack is a very happy ball python. One living in a tank..possibly not as much.

So why do people have ball pythons 'crammed' into 20 gallon tanks? Because many ball pythons despise large cages, and will refuse to eat if you put them in one. Too much open space freaks them out. Termite mounds, remember?

Having coaxed many ball python hatchlings into eating their first meal, I can testify to this. There are a certain percentage that, being in an enclosed dark bin that they take up 1/4 of the space in, with a water bowl and a hide, are still unhappy, and need their bin to be filled with crumpled newspaper before they will feel safe enough to eat.

Before you go hog wild on a huge, elaborate cage for your ball python...particularly since it is an albino and likely to have hampered eyesight as a result--find out if it is one of the many that dislike a big cage. It would be disappointing to set all that up for it, and then discover that your snake has no appreciation for it whatsoever, and refuses to eat until you put it into a tiny shoebox. Much the worse if you don't have a small cage handy, and have to fill your elaborate big cage full of crumpled newspaper, lol...

this seems to be an intermeshing of 2 worlds of thinking. i do not disagree with either world but i do like to have more than just a snake in a box. for the hardcore snake keeper with a collection tubs are absolutely doable. for the person who wants a display piece where he/she can observe their pet from time to time a big fancy cage is also absolutely doable. there's nothing wrong with wanting to enjoy your setup for more than a snake hidden in a box if that's what you prefer.

the absolute necessities imo would be your appropriately size hide or hides, a water bowl and whatever it takes cover wise to create security. in this sense the opaque bins will lend themselves to an ideal yet simplistic, easy to wash out setup. for someone looking for a bit more of an enjoyable setup a couple hides and adequate cover would be necessary. cover can be provided with some ground creeping vine like plants with large leafs, grass and/or some large leaf litter and by keeping the setup in a less traveled area. i mean if you think about it any animal in the wild searching for water would not be confined to a hide so certainly at some point in their lives they'd see some plants, leaf litter, branches and maybe an open area near the water, etc., etc.. surely no matter how large the cage in a persons living room was it would not compare to the size of nature on earth itself so there are ways to provide that security and still get what you want.

so, while bp's would need a pretty tight hide, a water bowl and to not be bothered by the constant traffic of passers by there is no reason to think it's absolutely necessary to keep them in something you could never just peer into.

one thing to keep in mind is that many bp keepers (even those with snake rack systems and opaque bins) will take their guys out and place them in a box to feed. it's a common practice and beings it's usually only once a week it's not something too intrusive to the daily goings on of life. i, personally don't see it as always necessary. especially for a species with such docile tendencies as a bp.

with the appropriate size and number of hides one could absolutely get away with as large a setup as one would want to make. surely the more distance a bp has from random people at all times the more secure it would feel. if one were to have a 20 gallon setup in the hallway between the bathroom and the living space with a hide, water bowl, etc. and host avon parties (have company) on a weekly basis of course the animal would be spooked from time to time. if that setup included a plant that hangs over the hide, a little space from the hide to the glass front and be located in the far corner of the living area or in a bedroom where company isn't usually welcomed of course the bp would feel more secure.

36" x 18" is a common footprint used for adult bp's. height is really a non issue.
