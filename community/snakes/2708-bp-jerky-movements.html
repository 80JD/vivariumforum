BP with jerky movements?
Now that my BP comes out more often and is actually comfortable groping me, I mean slithering all over me (sorry, he was just on his way down my top, lol...), I can see how he moves alot better. His movements seem to be somewhat jerky, and he is clumsy when trying to climb in his tank (maybe his branch isn't thick enough... I'll try to find a new one and see if that helps), he falls down more than I expect him to. Other than the jerky movements and some stuck shed on a few scales, he seems to be perfectly healthy. No evidence of RI, no stargazing or bubbles if you rub up the underside of his head (he will actually let me do that now).You guys that have BPs, do your guys move this way, or is it just mine? I'm not terribly worried, I just want to make sure there isn't something I need to keep an eye out for.

Thanks guys

I am sorry if this seems to come out wrong guys but facts are facts.
Rodent eating snakes get all the nutrition they need from being fed rodent prey of the appropriate size on a routine schedule. It does not matter if it is a Ball Python, Boa Constrictor, Corn Snake or Bull Snake, they are all the same as far as nutritional requirements go.
Rats are slightly more nutritious than Mice over all but Mice will supply everything your rodent eating snake needs if it is not large enough for rats, aka a hatchling.
Appropriate size is equal in circumference to the largest circumference of the snake. Slightly larger, 25%, is also fine but some snakes will regurge prey that exceeds this.
They need no other minerals, vitamins or supplements of any kind.
If you would like to see your snake take on an orange tint raise your own rodents and include carrots as a regular part of their diet. The Beta Carotene in the carrots will be absorbed by the snake in time giving them an orange tint. The tint will fade away when the snake is no longer being fed Beta Carotene enhanced rodents.

Sorry that I cannot address the jerky problem but a customer of mine who purchased a yearling Corn had the same problem with a BP. On consulting a number of BP breeders the best we could come up with was that it had some kind of neurological disorder that was not related to the Wobbles demonstrated in all Spider Balls at some stage of their life. If the BP in question is a Spider then it is now developing the wobbles a genetic neurological disorder of that morph.
From what I understand there are other genetic disorders associated with a few other morphs but not being a BP person I can’t remember which morphs have which problems.
