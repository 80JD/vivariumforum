There are several possible reasons for them not eating: 1) Winter season/cold weather, along with inadequate heat, 2) Housing BPs together in one enclosure is very stressful for them, and 3) a combination of 1 and 2.

1) Some BPs stop eating when winter comes, especially if their enclosure is near a window. Apparently the shorter days have something to do with it, or the colder temps.

2) BPs are MUCH happier alone in separate enclosures. It's possible that the one has become the alpha snake, so to speak, and the others are too stressed to eat.

3) If you have only one hot spot, all three of them can't be in/on it at the same time comfortably. That will mean that food digestion won't go well--they need the external heat source for good digestion.

Try separating them for a week or two, then try feeding them again; it might help.

Chu'Wuti has got it with (3). the most common thing with BPs lack of interest in feeding when housed together, For every snake you add to a tank that get this size need to be able to thermo regulate separately within the tank without being stressed by a tank mate already being in the prime basking/heating spot, You need manufacture a repeating gradient for each snake in the tank. I should of started with my friend picked up 6 babies from an abandoned apartment he was looking into renting, the short and unsweet of it is of the 6 sadly 3 passed away form what we later learned from a Vet was Gut Rot and severely degraded digestion and well on there way to MBD. After we learned this the Repeating Gradient idea was implemented and so far he had had the last 3 for maybe 12 years now...

I must add i am not discounting the Weather / Winter cycle animals go through, just figured id mention this sad tale as i have never had an issue with my snakes not eating no mater the season or time of day and have been keeping them for almost 15 years.
