as far as your snake eating the substrate the risk of that is a negligable one because your snake shouldnt be eating in the enclosure you keep him in. the problem with this is it tends to make the snake associate your hand coming into the enclosure with food so if you do it enough you may end up getting tagged alot when trying to handle your snake. so given that the substrate shouldnt be in the feeding tub and there for risk of ingestion of the substrate is minimal. as far as the comment on impaction impaction is possible for any substrate unless you are using newspaper or carpet and if you intend on making it a naturalistic habitat those wouldnt really support that intent so its a matter of decision do you want the naturalistic habit or zero chance for impaction. impaction is a standard risk when it comes to keeping reptiles as with Lizards a vast majority of their food carries a risk of impaction sure it is possible but low chance in most cases i would say that as far as with your kingsnake you should be ok with giving him the sand and even eco earth which is what i use for my reptiles it is a wonderful substrate

I am using sphagnum peat moss in my BP enclosure, it seems to be working out fine, I use it in all of my enclosures (it is very similar to the eco-earth, bed-a-beast type stuff, except it is not coco fiber). What I use is milled, not whole moss. I can get it inexpensively, and it can be baked for a little bit if you are worried about there possibly being pests inside. I have been using it for years, since I began keeping herps, I have had no problems yet. All of my plants do very well in it as well.
