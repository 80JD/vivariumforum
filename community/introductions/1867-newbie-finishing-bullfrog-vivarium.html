Hello and Welcome to the forum. I just finished up building a new Viv for some Red Bellied Toads that I'll be getting on Thursday. For a Bullfrog you need to provide water and land for him to hang out on. A picture of your current setup would help also if your able to do that.

As for your Humidity Levels I don't think that will matter with a bullfrog since they are semi-aquatic frogs. My Viv keeps humidity at about 70-90% at room temperatures also. I think if you have a large pool of water with a waterfall or filter it will cause that.

For plants I would think that either a swamp or pond setup would be great for your bullfrog.That's there natural habitat anyways. My setup for the FBT's Im getting consists of Gold Tip Moss, Horse Tail Rush and some ferms that like Humidity.

Im "trying" to grow Java Moss on the ground,but it's not fairing well for some reason. In my pool section I've got some marsh grasses and java moss growing well. I've added some branches I collected from the woods sticking across the pool section and into it to provide a way to get out easy. Also remember to make your pool easy to get into and out of for your frog.


Also what size of tank do you have? I'm still really new to the Vivarium keeping,but I hope I helped alittle.

Hi, welcome to the forum! It would also help if you can tell us what type of bullfrog you have, species name if possible. That way I could try to find some specific info for you on the set up you will need. I know back home in SC we called LOTS of frogs/toads bullfrogs...

That screen size you want is a fairly standard size, I'm guessing the tank is a 20G long? that is what I have for my BP, and both petco and petsmart had screen lids for it. Of course, you are probably going to kick yourself when you get it, if the ambient humidity needs to be high. If it doesn't and the water is fine to keep your bullfrog hydrated, then the screen top will be fine. You can also construct a screen lid yourself. All you need is appropriate screening, and something to make a frame. If you will be setting lights directly on the screen, I would go with metal screening, just be VERY sure there are no sharp points on the inside. I have an ESU hood light, mine has a fluorescent fixture and two incandescent fixtures. It has worked very well for me so far, since I need UV for my CWD, and heat lights. I'm not sure if the kit you are looking at is going to work out for you, it depends on the heat requirements of your bullfrog. two 100 watt lights are going to make it VERY hot in there. I currently am using a 60 watt and a 75 watt bulb to achieve an abient temp of 82 and a basking temp of 90. This is in a house that is NOT temperature controlled. Anything higher than that and I can get temps in the 98-105 range. The 100 watt nighttime light might work well. Honestly, I just use cheap lightbulbs from walmart for heat. For my frog tank, I use a compact fluorescent that does NOT produce UV (the UV producing ones can cause eye problems). I use that because they are fine with room temp, and it is better for the plants. It also doesn't suck out my humidity like an incandescent will. If you go ahead and get the hood, I would put the night light on one side, one of the day lights on another, and a compact fluorescent in the middle. That way your temps don't get too high, and you have a good light source for your plants. Plus, you'll have the second day bulb for when the first one goes out (herp specific bulbs are expensive). I have also used reveal bulbs in my tanks, and they seem to do better for the plants than a standard incandescent. If your temps drop too low with the bulbs in there like I suggested, you CAN turn the night bulb on in the day time in addition to the day bulb, it will help boost the temps. OUR SET-UP
Sorry no pix -My digital is full of pictures at the moment and won't be emptied for awhile . . . - consist of a 20 gallon long tank, half and half setup. The divider idea I got online from a site called Mike's Viquarium (Instructions Page) and the housing section from allaboutfrogs.com (http://allaboutfrogs.org/info/housing/). I simplified a bit also.

LAND SIDE:
Used egg crate on bottom followed by fiberglass mesh, then gravel, more mesh, then the substrate. What prompted us to build the vivarium is a desire to be able to feed BJ without taking her out and putting her in a feeding chamber once a week (too much stress for us and BJ). This was necessary because the crickets and worms would drown in the water when they'd go off the floating island which was not too big (when she was housed in her previous home). So the land mass will be her feeding "chamber". Substrate is coco fiber and worm castings -good medium, I believe for growing plants. So basically the idea is to drop in worms/crickets in dish on land mass for feeding -and leave them there with food (?) -we are really experimenting! We placed three worms in dish (and like a pot, if you watch, nothing happens: the frog won't eat when she is being watched -we found). And two disappeared. I looked through the substrate which is only about 2/3 inches deep but didn't find them so we think they were eaten. One was still alive for a couple more hours -then we went to bed and it was gone the next morning??? I need to check the soil again. (We are planning on setting up a worm compost bin as another project and I just read earthworms are good for frogs, too!)

DIVIDER: 5"/6" piece of glass slanted to mimic beach. With split glass tubing siliconed on exposed sharp edge. Rocks were siliconed also onto "beach". All siliconed at about a 45 degree angle to aquarium. I can't recall where I got this info but credit goes out to someone in cyber space -I did the research a year ago . . failing memory. . .

WATER SIDE:
While a tadpole BJ had an aquatic tank with a floating island in 4" of water and a Duetto filter (biological, carbon filter, very quiet and versatile) acting like a water fall on the island. We had a couple of plants with some large gravel on the bottom. We basically replicated this old model on the water side since it's worked so well -very low maintenance (the filter is awesome)-only ambient light. Bullfrogs love being in the water peeping/peeking out. We added one water lettuce and its doing very well! So we'll add more of these soon. We are looking into adding a couple of feeder fish (bullfrogs eat fish also) to see what happens . . . I read Vitamin D can be found in fish -and bones can provide calcium. (but I just found out that dusting the insects or gut-loading them with a good vits powder would work out also . . . and we've been doing that with the crickets.)

Screen: This is typical. I went to Petco and I found the screen I needed (not the ESU screen/light combo). Even after I called and they told me they had none!! I bought it. But not sure if we'll use it, yet. We have a glass cover so if it ends up she needs more humidity, then we'll keep the glass cover (we air it out). We are trying to decide.

CWD stands for Chinese Water Dragon. Sorry, I got so used to typing the acronym that I rarely type it all out first anymore.

I think your frog should probably do fine with the humidity in her tank, since she will spend alot of time in the water. Room temp is also probably fine (as long as it isn't in the 60's). You're right, it does seem like yo will only need a light for the plants (I did a bit of research on your frog, btw), I would suggest a clamp lamp (can be purchased in the tool section of wal-mart for less than $10) and a compact fluorescent. These provide better light for your plants than a standard incandescent, and they will not suck the moisture out of your tank or make it too hot. If you would rather go with a strip light, you can get a fluorescent plant bulb, and a strip that will accommodate it. This may be a better idea, and you wont have to worry about what to clamp the light to. I currently have a fluorescent marketed for fish and plants on my aquarium, and it is a nice color, and the plants and fish are doing very well. You can either get a strip light meant for an aquarium (you should be able to find one the right side to go all the way across your tank), or you can purchase one from a hardware store, or even wal-mart. If you get one from wal-mart or a hardware store, if it comes with a plastic covering, take it off, it will block some of the spectrum that the plants will need. I checked out that page you got ideas from, and it was very nice. Might have to steal some ideas from him myself!
