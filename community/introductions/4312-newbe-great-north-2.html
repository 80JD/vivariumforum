estimative index is based on maximum possible uptake from aquarium plants. i tend to advise to start off with half ei and work from there. also i am a real big advocate of aquariumfertilizer.com for those that don't want to search out fleet enema, stump remover and such locally.

i, personally have low light systems. i started with ei and adjusted from there. my dosing regimen is now much more conservative yet it's sufficient for my setups. it now more resembles a pps pro dosing schedule.

another thing to consider epiphytes typically don't get a lot of nutrients. they're typically sustained by rain water run off which is very nutrient poor. dosing their water may cause a problem.

i am by far no expert on terrestrial plants by any means but my thinking is for your epiphytes you may want to check the ph and hardness of the water supplying them. you would want it soft and not too acidic i would think.

the one very beneficial aspect to ei though is target levels. if you are able to test you can just meet that with your dosing and you should be good for most plants.

those are my thoughts on ei for a paludarium in relation to epiphytes.



as far as drip irrigation and keeping it simple i completely agree. KISS (keep it simple stupid) applies to electronics on every level in my opinion.

my point was no plant needs ei levels unless it's got extremely high lighting and injected with co2. to add to that i'm not sure but you may be able to do harm to plants that are used to low nutrient levels by for instance adding too much nitrogen compounds.

just some food for thought. you should start a thread chronicling your experiment when you start. i wouldn't mind finding out myself how it works out.



adding to the peat and organic materials you still have magnesium and calcium which are measured as gh. gh is removed to virtually 0 by an r/o system and is important for both flora and fauna. then there's your other ferts. waste products and food should provide a decent amount of nitrogen and phosphorous probably but then you have potassium and micros including iron that will be stripped and therefore lacking.

now keep in mind you can still use r/o water it's just best to re-introduce those minerals. i can lead you in the right direction for minerals if you decide to go that route.

i think the auto doser is pretty cool myself. your welcome.

Soaker tubing's definitely one way- there are also drip nozzles that might be better for smaller spaces. The soaker tubing's for running through gardens and the like.

You're totally right about being able to keep the misting nozzles and acrylic/glass clean with the RO water. Perhaps (and maybe this was the plan all along) having two routes from your RO source- one directly to the misting system and another to the canister filter or wherever you'll be introducing water to the viv- might be a good way to do it.
