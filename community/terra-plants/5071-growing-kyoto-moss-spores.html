You should look up the type of conditions that moss grows in. You'll need to know whether it needs acidic or alkaline soil. Most likely it will be acidic. If the substrate you are using isn't right for the moss, it won't grow, at least not quickly. For example, if the soil is not acidic enough, the coating on the spores will not wear away and allow the spores to grow into moss.

I recently purchased a book on mosses (the link to the site was posted here on the forum), and I'm very happy to have done so. This fellow grows his mosses on what he calls a moss mat system. It is basically vinyl sheeting, a layer of felt, the moss (in your case, spores), then a layer of tulle (fine mesh that can be found at fabric stores). He says that this eliminates the need for soil testing/amending in order to grow beautiful mosses. The pictures of his mosses in the book are certainly lush and beautiful. You could also do this with open cell foam. I recommend starting a second batch, seeding it onto some felt, foam, or other surface that will keep it moist. I've had great luck with moss on wood as well as foam in my newt tank, so I know that this can be beneficial from experience.
