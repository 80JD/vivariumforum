Well, if you are going to go with the Kentucky blue grass...

I just sprinkled the seeds over the substrate (if your FBTs are in the tank while you are getting it to grow, sprinkle some more seeds every couple of days, since they will get moved around by the FBTs). If you do that, expect to see some of it getting into your water area, because the toads will drag it in on themselves. IMO, you could grow Kentucky blue on LECA if you wanted to, or even in water, or with no light, lol... I had some that ended up UNDER my pond in the CWD tank that was growing, pretty well too, with hardly any water (just what tiny bit overflowed the pond) no air, no upward room to grow, on gravel, with no light (bottom of pond was completely lined with rocks). SO, it is pretty hardy. I have found it growing INSIDE my filter, as well as submerged in the pond. I have also had it growing on my carpet at the house, when it had only been exposed to water once when I sprinkled it in the pot (cat walked in the pot, then on the carpet, and a few blades sprouted). The best thing about this grass, IMO, is that it does not spread like other lawn grasses do. Each seed sprouts a single plant, which is a single blade of grass. It is a very soft grass, and has no razory edges like some grasses do, which makes it safer for the FBTs. It grows fairly tall without trimming (3-5 inches), but the toads will either trample it down or build nests in it (very cool). If it gets to looking crappy to you, just take a pair of scissors and trim it flat. Look at it as a tiny piece of lawn, lol. (Maybe you could build a tiny rotary lawn mower... FBT size, hehehe. JK!!!) I have found that this grass did not require fertilization in my tanks, but if closely exposed to high levels of light (less than 2ft from light source in my CWD tank, which has a UV fluorescent, two incandescent bulbs for heat during the day [wattage varrying from 40 to 100] and two 75 watt black incandescent bulbs at night) that it will scorch and whither. You wont likely have that much light on your FBTs, so you should be fine. It grew with no problems in my frog/toad tank. I'm currently researching organic gardening methods that can be used safely in vivariums, so if you feel the need to fertilize the grass, I can get back to you with safe alternatives to commercial fertilizers.
