I agree with frogsong on most of these with a few exceptions. I will use scintific names for clarity sake.

1 Agreed
2. Agreed - Hypoestes phyllostachya
3. Agreed - Smithiana species or hybrid
4. Agreed - Schlumbergera hybrid
5. Most likely an Aloe
6. Not a Peperomia but a Crassula ovata, Jade Plant
7. Agreed
8. Alocasia hybrid
9. One of the plants known as Baby's Tears. Could be Pilea depressa. Not a Peperomia
10. Selaginella martensii I think
11.Hedera cultivar and as frogsong said deadly to herbivours. I lost 2 3 Iguanas to it.

I have to disagree that hard water spots damage the leaves. They make them look bad but seldom damage them. Hard water is bad for some plants and not others. In general epiphytes do not like water with lots of dissolved minerals so I suggest using rainwater or RO water. When you by a fertilizer get one formulated for these as many ferts have buffers in the and this may make the PH of the water either too low or too high when using rainwater otr RO water. Both are low in PH, 6.0 6.5. Most terrestrials do not mind hard water if flushed periodically with rainwater. But, you do get the spots if you allow them to rest and dry on the leaves. Mosses hate hard water.
