Quote:
Originally Posted by newsalt
I am new and not real familiar with ecoweb but from what I have read and the discussions I have come across it seems ( to me ) that ecoweb is made from recycled plastics is not much more than a glorified scrubby ( different thickness of fiber and density of mesh ) where felt wold absorb and hold water ecoweb would retain water droplets in its structure but would not stay wet like felt which is actualy made from wool fibers and fibers themself would absorb and hold moisture.
Please correct me if I am wrong or off base here but it seems the felt is used to retain maximum water retention and ecoweb is designed to let most of the moisture to pass through and retain small amounts of water droplets caught in its mesh making for a dryer substrate.
You are not off base. ECOWEB is used more for a growing surface for epiphytes. From what I have seen no mosses spontaneously get started on it since it's too open. Thats why you need some something in addition to get the mosses growing on. I use sphagnum but you could use other materials such as peat. Bottom line is that for growing purposes it's not the best medium for moss. But, as you can see in the pictures I posted it will grow them. The good thing about ECOWEB/EPIWEB is that you can form almost anything with it given a fair amount of practice and once a moss mix slurry is added it can be pretty impressive. I have not tried any of the scrubbing pads so I can't speak to them.

I have seen a ton of amazing photos of people that use Eco/Epiweb. You can even buy a tropical moss mixture from their website that they give a 100% growth success rate using their Ecoweb II system.

The only problems that I have herd about this product is that unless it is completely covered in moss the capilary action will be limited. Also, if the openings in the fibers arent filled in, they are the perfect size for frogs to get their legs cought. I have even herd that frogs have had their legs broken and as a result have even been killed from it. I'm sure this is a long shot but if anyone uses this product (as I am planning in my next tank) make sure it is WELL grown-in before adding animals.
