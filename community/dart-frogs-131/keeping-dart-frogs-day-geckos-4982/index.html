it's best to house them separately for some time and do fecals, swabs, lab testing, chytrid treatment, get to know their habits, quirks, personalities, preferred peripherals (temps, etc.) and judge for yourself if YOUR SPECIFIC animals would do well with each other. making sure they're both completely healthy and happy for MINIMUM a couple months would help out severely. also keep in mind you'll have to culture your own foods for these species keeping containers of flies (and maggots) handy for feeding time. if you are not familiar with this practice it's best to give yourself a couple months before you even buy your animals to get cultures of at least 3 feeders going strong and healthy reliably before you even purchase your animals.

this will be quite a daunting and time consuming tedious task for someone new to either species maybe even if you're familiar with breeding other feeders and housing other herps already.

think about it. if you'd still like to continue after you've done enough research into everything they'd need start at the beginning (breeding feeders). once you've conquered that much and you have 2 setups and 2 ready for quarantine setups then take the next step (buy them and start quarantine, treatment, testing, etc.). once everything is worked out and you're confident there's no issues and think they'll be happy together then do so... but keep in mind the whole process will take months MINIMUM and you'll want your tanks already setup with plants growing in throughout the whole ordeal idealistically.

i know it's a lot to swallow, but there's a lot to consider and think about... good luck and welcome to the site.

also keep in mind i'm one of very few people who are not going to come off as if mixing species will deteriorate the earth at it's crust. we try to keep an open mind around here... but things happen.

good luck... god's speed and welcome to the site.

I have a viv. that is pretty decent size that has a jackson chemelion, a crested gecko, eastern newt, and one dart frog. This seems to be running jus' fine as they all share the space equally and the two lizards will actually sleep together at that early morning time when one is falling asleep and the other is waking up. I have noticed with any multi-animal situation that the key factor is food. Keep your bugs and such around so that none are hungry, once you lose that balance and competition starts up then you have teritorial issues and fighting. As always tho these types of setups require much attention and the ability to seperate animals if need be.
