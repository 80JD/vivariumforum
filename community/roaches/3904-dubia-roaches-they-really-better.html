Dubia roaches - are they really better?
I wanted my title to catch some attention from those who have had experience using Blaptica dubia as a feeder insect. I have been keeping B. dubia for about 3 months now. I still haven't had any reproductions yet, my males and females are still too immature. But I have been buying what nymphs I need to feed my Hispaniolian White-lipped Anole (Anolis coelestinus). She loves them.

But I am wondering if dubia roaches really do have a higher 'meat to shell ratio' than crickets. Does this claim apply to all stages of the roaches life, incl. nymphs, or only the adults, or near-adults? From what I can see of my nymphs, they don't seem to me to be very "meaty". In fact they look so flat in comparison with crickets of similar size, that I wonder if the claim has a real basis in fact.

The size of dubia nymphs that I use for feeding are in the small size category, say 1/2 inch or a little more. My anole can handle those up to about 3/4 inch ok but nothing larger than that.

So, is the higher 'meat to shell ratio' of dubia roaches really true? For all stages of the roach's life, incl. nymphs?

From what I understand, roaches in general are better than crickets not so much for their 'meat to shell ratio', but for their calcium to phosphorus ratio. Roaches have more calcium than crickets, and much less phosphorus. I switched to feeding and breeding blatta lateralis to my herps instead of crickets for several reasons, including better nutritional value. It is difficult to find info for specific roach species, but I did find one paper that outlines the nutritional values for many common feeder insects, including common crickets and the american cockroach. I don't know how dubia and lateralis differ from the american roach, but its a starting point anyway.

Besides having a higher meat to shell ratio, they also have much longer intestinal tracts which means they hold more gut load and for a longer period of time than crickets. They also have a much longer life span. So you don't end up with a ton of dead ones all the time like you do with crickets. And at this point, with the cricket virus happening, the other obvious benefit is that roaches aren't affected. I work with dubia and lateralis and have for years now. I'll never go back to breeding crickets.
