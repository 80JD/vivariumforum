SMALL lizards are likely on the menu for a WTF in the wild, but NOT full grown anoles. If you feel that you MUST feed the frog anoles, at least buy baby ones. Now that said, I don't know if you are aware that almost all of the anoles in the pet trade are wild caught and most likely riddled with parasites... But they are, and as such you should consider carefully whether you want to house them long enough to get them clean before offering them as a snack.

I don't agree with feeding anoles to something that wouldn't be eating them all the time in the wild, but I did grow up loving them because they were everywhere and endlessly interesting. Personally, I feel that a feeder, whether it be insect or otherwise, should have a good life before it is eaten, and I take that into consideration with my breeding stock. My rats are pets and feeders, and all of my baby rats are pet quality animals. I even snuggle them before I put them to sleep. I certainly don't cuddle crickets, but I make sure they have what they seem to like best and that they are kept within their most comfortable parameters. Even my supers and mealies are kept in the mediums they seem to prefer over others and fed good food. I know this may seem like a real "tree hugger" attitude, but if they are going to give me their lives, I may as well make it a good one before it's their time. I feel the same way about livestock, if an animal is going to give up it's life to sustain another, they should at least be comfortable until the end. I hope that makes sense to everyone. I feel like I'm over explaining it, but it's hard to get the words right to convey what I mean.

Anyway, in the end it comes down to what you want to do. It's most likely safe to feed a full grown White's little lizards, as long as they aren't bigger than a pinkie (or at least no buy much, and not counting the tail), just make sure whatever lizard you are offering isn't spikey or toxic. Also, don't ever feed him other amphibians, especially newts or salamanders, there are too many potentially fatal considerations to take into account where that's concerned. But please, don't make the animal suffer, and be prepared to care for it if it is NOT eaten. Feel free to leave it in the tank with the frog until it is, but care for it as you would a pet until the day comes.

an American grey tree frog? Those guys arn't big enough to safely eat an anole in my opinion- The adults around where I live are a little bigger than a half-dollar. I like to stick closer to the fish trade rule: if a fishes eye is the size of its stomach, don't feed more than the size of the fish's eye. Granted, herps are a little different, but they can over stuff themselves and perish. Better to stay on the safe side.
