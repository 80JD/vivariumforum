The dart frogs will likely eat them and solve the problem for you. My darts are always hunting tiny insects and larvae between feedings. I sometimes see tiny mite-like critters and tiny worm-like larvae on the glass that even my largest darts will to back flips to catch. So, unless they're harming your plants, and since you intend to put darts in the viv, I wouldn't do anything other than the CO2 bomb.

Also, CO2 gas is heavier than air, so before you put darts in the viv, make sure it is fully ventilated. Otherwise a layer could persist at the bottom of the viv and kill the frogs.

There are two groups of springtails, based on body shape. Some are elongated and would have bodies shaped like a piece of rice. Others are more rounded (referred to as globular springtails). There are thousands of species in both groups and they feed on fungus.

Is your viv only plants or do you have any animals? Dart frogs would make quick work of them.

Newly set up vivaria typically have a lot of fungus, which is probably what caused the springtail population explosion. They'll eat it up and then die back to a stable population. It's funny because I just spent $30 to have several types of springtails shipped to me so that I can get them started in a couple of vivs since I recently acquired some thumbnail dart frogs. I never needed them before because I've always had larger species. And, without trying, you have accomplished that for free!
