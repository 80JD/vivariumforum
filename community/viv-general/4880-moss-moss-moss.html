Java moss will grow outside of water... you just have to keep it wet and well lit. It will look different from how it looks underwater, but given the right conditions, it should take off and take over any wet piece of wood or substrate. Same goes with riccia, but a lot of folks have had bad luck with it as it grows slower than java moss outside of water.

Brian at Brianstropicals.com uses both java and riccia and talks about what he likes/dislikes about them. He has pics of both in his tanks, which will give you an idea as to the growth habit of each of them: brianstropicals.com/building

riccia stays very short which is great for foreground and waterfall areas. Java moss can grow rather tall for a moss and has a jungly appearence. Brian suggusts keeping bigger dart frogs in java moss tanks so that the moss gets trampled down in place. He specializes in darts btw- and his basement has over 50 dart tanks! (but he also breeds zebra plecos and other fish- check out his site, he sells the young and even has unrelated breeders set up for other hobby people like himself.)

I haven't done Riccia out of water yet, but I've had all sorts of different experiences with Java moss, and I find that a lot of factors can affect how it will grow. I've seen it go really long, pale and somewhat spindly, but I've also had it stay short and a nice deep green. I think it has to do with the amount of light it receives, and also how much water it's getting.
