Not to be rude, but your set up is very dangerous for your chameleon! Chameleons need at least 2 sides and the top of their cage screened, more height than floor space, and you cant have water in the bottom! Chameleons need screened cages to keep the air flowing, a screen lid is not enough! Without proper ventilation, a chameleon will develop a respiratory infection(a veiled chameleon needs 60-70% humidity, which should be provided by daily misting). If your chameleon falls in the bottom, it will drown, they can't swim. Chameleons are arboreal animals, meaning they spend their lives above ground. Also, chameleons should not be housed with any other animal! This is extremely stressful to them! Chameleons also need many places to climb while still being hidden in the plants. My suggestion would be to just build a new, screened tank with no substrate(you can use coconut fiber but they can still ingest it when eating, which can cause impaction), many more small vines and many plants, and not other animals. You don't have to scrap that tank, you can put tree frogs(such as Vietnamese mossy frogs) in it, but depending on the humidity needs, you may have to take off the extra topper. Again, sorry if I sound rude, but these issues are very important and need to be taken care of immediately!

This looks great! Someday I hope to do something like this for my Panther Chameleon.

I too had the same thoughts about the open water situation. Till I seen the pictures of just how BIG the chameleon is. Lol! There might be a slight risk of drowning, but seems unlikely as long as someone is home to supervise. (if you hear a sudden splash, it may be your chameleon drowning!). But just to be safe, I'd probably move the cham into the said screen enclosure if no one is going to be home for an extended period of time.

As for the stress from the other animal, it's not like the turtle is zipping around the tank and thus freaking out the chameleon. I imagine the slow swimming movements of the turtle go virtually unnoticed by the cham. Especially if the cham hangs out in the upper egg crate section most of the time for basking, which looks to be at least a few feet from the water.

You can use glass enclosures as long as they are WELL VENTILATED, which seems to be the case here again with the egg crate section.

I'd say that as long as your chameleon is executing natural behaviors and is eating and shedding normally, then you shouldn't have a problem.

Just my opinion. Nice job wit the viv, and keep up the good work!
