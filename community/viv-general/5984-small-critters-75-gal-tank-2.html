One thing to bare in mind about nocturnal species is that (a) some of them can adapt to becoming active during daytime (my leopard geckos actually move about quite a bit during the day, because that's when I feed them); (b) many nocturnal species will become active just after lights-out, so if you set a timer to turn the bulb off at a time of day that you'd like to watch them, you will see them move a lot. You could even have moonlight bulbs or some other dim lighting for part/all of the "nighttime" in their schedule; (c) while this is still being debated on rare occasion, it is generally believed that nocturnal species do not need UV bulbs, as they have evolved to be active after the sun is down, and therefore do not rely on UV to process calcium, so you would not need to provide UV for a nocturnal species.

Day geckos are diurnal, so that might bring the vote back toward them.

Edit: Also, leopard geckos and cresteds are well known for being handleable (or at least easily hand-tamed), so I'm not sure why he wouldn't let you hold them, especially if you were there as a prospective customer. While over-handling might stress them out, it's kind of par-for-the-course that, during their stay in a pet shop, species will get held and inspected by those who are considering a purchase. To me, that sounds a little like a used car salesman saying "I can't show you the exact Saab I'm selling, but I can show you a picture of a Saab online." It's a little sketchy.
