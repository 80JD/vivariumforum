Im just new here so take what I say very loosely lol

I was thinking the same thing. What made me want to go with the filter was a combination of a few things. First I didnt want to make a drain , and no matter what substrate you have, with animals, I think at some point it would have to be cleaned and not just drained. I want to leave my viv alone, and have the lowest maintenance possible. With the canister filter, a few things are accomplished. You wont need a drain, your water can be cleaned, filtered, and even switched out for new with ease. The only draw back is having to put the canister somewhere,but, I still find that easier then creating a cover inside your viv to access the pump and making a draining area.

So my opinion, go with the filter to make life easier and keep the viv untouched as much possible. We will see what the pros have to say, but after thinking this through, the canister in my eyes was the most sensible. Once I get into this hobby a bit more, maybe some better options might open up for me, but being new, this seemed like the best

Im going with the Zoo med 501. Cheap price, small..and with only 3" on water on my floor, I think it will definitely do the trick.

Good luck on your build, Post pics!!!!

The canister filter is going to keep your water much cleaner. Will come down to whether or not you want to keep everything internal or external.
Only problem with internal pumps from my experience is that they can tend to get clogged relatively quickly and become a pain to always get back into were the pump is to clean it out.

I would think sump would be good if your tank is already drilled. If you don't mind running tubes into the viv something like a Rena canister filter with a valve coming off the outlet to adjust flow would be great.
