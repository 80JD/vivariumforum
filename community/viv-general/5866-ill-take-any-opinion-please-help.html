I think what beck was getting at is that you should pick up a cheap ten gallon and some supplies to practice some of the techniques, like false bottoms, backgrounds, waterfalls, etc, so you can decide how to do the larger tank with a little experience under your belt. Believe me, if you do your first in a tank that big, you're going to regret not having practiced on something smaller.

Mixing species is a no-no without experience, take that from an experienced mixer.

You could do a wonderful tank for a pair of water dragons in a tank that size, if you add a topper. Or, you could do a colony of some sort of frogs or smallish lizards. If you are interested in desert species, you could do a desert tank for a couple of beardies (what exactly are the dimensions of the tank?) or some leopard geckos. It just depends on if you want something lush and green or not. Decide kindof the look you want, then pick an animal, then work out how to get the tank done.

We're all happy to help out, we just want to make sure you don't make the mistakes that others have made.

if you still want variety i'd suggest something that's bred to bring out different traits like maybe crested geckos. i think bronze auratus (i could be completely wrong on this since i'm not a dart guy) just happens at this point when 2 regulars are bred or vice versa. i think if you stock with them you might have a good chance some throws would be regular giving you a little variety. so there's ways to add color without risking too much.

i would definitely check out some sales on different forums to see what you can get ahold of and at what price. as well check out some vendors online and local sources for supplies you'll need. a lush tank doesn't happen overnight without putting a lot into it at first whether that be your time or money... or both. outfitting a lush 100 gal. could easily run you a thousand or 2 if you're not careful and don't capitalize on good deals.
