Acrylic Vivarium - Moss, Rocks, and Awesome!
Hey folks!

I am new to the forum as well as vivariums. I am a former reef fanatic who then switched to planted aquariums, and now on to terrestrial planted aquariums (vivariums).

Enclosure:
I purchased a large acrylic enclosure from Ricky's Reptile Enclosures (originally designed and inteded to be a fantastic enclosure for ball pythons) that has very little ventilation and unfortunately sports an extruded acrylic door that is 1/4" thick. Needless to say it has done a little bit of warping on the edges; cast acrylic might have been a better choice. Either way it will still do the job.
The enclosure is 24" x 20" x 18" and gives plenty of room to add depth and dimension to the hardscape. The lack of height is something I actually wanted in the vivarium. In my experience, if the enclosure’s height is greater than its width, you will lose the illusion of depth and dimension in the enclosure. If the enclosure’s depth (front to back) is less than your height, you wall also often lose that illusion of a deep and expansive habitat. In short, Length should be the longest dimension, depth (front to back) should be the second longest, and height should be the shortest. That doesn’t mean I think short enclosures are the most attractive, I love a good 36” tall enclosure! I simply believe following that guide will prevent the dreaded “wall of plants” or “closet vivarium” look.

Lighting:
For now, a simple Marineland Single Bright LED fixture is providing enough light for my mosses and shade plants.

Misting:
I am misting manually with a dollar store bought pressurized plant mister. I bought two – one for my snake rack, and another for the enclosure. I didn’t really need to buy two separate misting bottles, but they are dollar store quality and I wanted a backup in case one broke. Due to the little ventilation the enclosure has, it holds humidity very well. I only mist once a day to maintain 80% humidity or better.

Substrate:
For drainage I am using Eheim Substrat Pro. I had a ton of it lying around and it is porous enough to support beneficial bacteria, and roughly the same size as expanded clay. The main substrate is comprised of a mixture of coconut fiber, Fir peat moss, sand, and Sphagnum moss.

Flora:
I currently have 4 different species of moss in the vivarium. For now there are a few strands of Creeping Jenny (Lysimachia nummularia) and a bunch of wild ginger (Asarum canadense). I am going to try and stay away from atypical vivarium plants in this enclosure; I am going for a different look. I will be doing a 2nd enclosure at some point for Mantellas which will certainly have bromeliads etc.

Fauna:
None yet, but the plan is to get a small colony of Phyllobates terribilis.




Any questions or comments are welcome.
