Cool beans!

So are those pots made from clay? You need to be able to poke drainage holes in the bottom, or your pots/substrate will become water logged.
And make sure to boil that mopani several times. I only boiled my piece once in a big pot of water, and it is still leaching tannins like mad.
And when you are attaching the ecoearth to the silicone, make sure to bake it completely bone dry, or it wont stick at all. Do it either in the sun for several days, or oven at around 225-250

If you take a look at my very 1st build, I do a seperate water/land feature exactly how you just said.

Good thinking for the drainage tube out each pot. It may be difficult to have the pots angled at the right angle as you use the GS, since they will be heavy. Try and make few GS blobs on the side 1st, then use them as supports for the pots/driftwood as you add the fresh GS.

The drainage rock area has to have its own hose out the top of the tank, or out the side of the glass. I ran mine up one of the back corners, then out one of the power cord holes in the exo-terra top. Just use something on the end of the hose that will prevent it from sucking up a rock and clogging it...Like some window screen.

The biggest advantage of having a seperate land/water feature, is that no tannins will leach from the soil into it. I always get questions how I keep my water feature so clear, and thats how. Just make sure your barrier wont let water into the substrate side. test test test.

if you dry your eco-earth out in the sun... put a window screen over it. you don't have to make one. just pop one out of a window to keep your coco from blowing everywhere... then rinse it and put it back. once or twice a day it's best to turn the coco so it dries faster. otherwise you can just dry it in the oven at 170*F or better.
