[...] I have no idea how marty's setup is ... um... set up. :P

What kind of silicone is suitable for vivariums?
---> GE 100% silicone I or II (II does not stink and cures faster, but there is an old debate running around that I is better because it doesn't have bioseal (but it shrinks). Now I believe that that debate is outdated, especially since people have been using II and not having any issues. I think most everyone here uses GE silicone II - black preferably.

[...] not my area of know-how.

Is riccia expensive?
---> no, it isn't very expensive imo- look some up on ebay. You can sometimes get some nice wood pieces already covered in the stuff.

How do you know what plants/rocks go with what frogs?
---> Well, if you have a jungle-loving frog, go with vines and big ferns and lots of moss, etc. If you have a toad or other such burrowing amphibian, then you should probably plant hardy plants and put them in individual pots in the soil, that way the critters can't dig them up as easily. If you have native critters, use native plants (always my favorite go

Basically if you look up the frog species and figure out where in the world it originates, then google pictures of that place and find plants that mimic that habitat as close as possible. There are also sites that list plants from the frog habitats. A google search while your bored will dig up some interesting finds on them.

Sorry, but that's about all I can confortably add... not too comfortable in the other areas yet.

grout is a kind of mortar. there are other mortar products that will withstand water too. a sealer will look unnatural unless it's shine is buffed out or sand has been added to the final coats or it's under water.

the air system recirculates the humid air through the viv and the hood keeping the lamps cooler, more efficient and giving them a longer shelf life.

you will want separate timers for the lights and the mister. some misters i believe come with their own timers.

be careful putting several heat sources on the reptitemp 500r. it's a nice unit (i'm using one right now) but you'll want the temp probe on the hottest spot if you do that to prevent burns. it only has 2 outlets anyway (not like you can't use splitters).

a wood canopy can be sealed too. if the inhabitants can't get through it then you could just use eggcrate as a divider section so nobody jumps out when you open the lid. but yeah glass or plexi is best.

if it's a tropical lizard you want uvb may be necessary too. if pc lighting (like marty's) or t5ho you won't need heat lamps to provide a day temp... especially if your circulation cools your lamps by sending that heat into the tank.

purge the water refers to rinsing the alkaline ph out of many mortars. (the ones packaged as mortar or concrete).

the water flows through the epiweb on the bottom. there is a pump behind the epiweb. it is a filter... and the plants help.

heat lamps are rarely the only source of heat in a larger system imo. they're not the most dependable source either.

a sealer is not required for any mortar if it's not going to get wet. it is not required for SOME products if it will get wet.

you can buy a hood, build a hood or have someone build it for you. it is a good way to house a lot of things, yes.

there is a bunch of ways to get heat into an enclosure.

if one were to use a wood canopy as opposed to covering your tank with glass you could seal the inside from moisture to prevent issues.

the inhabitants can sometimes get through eggcrate, not wood.

for most frogs you won't need uvb so you may not want to concern yourself with that at this point. pc= power compact. t5 refers to 5/8" thick fluorescent bulbs. t5ho is the same but high output. pc and any "ho" bulbs as well as a couple other types put out a good bit of heat.

frogs live in just about every geological niche on the planet so species would determine the type of setup.

good to hear you're appreciating and enjoying the community we have here.

please look over the stickies and as much as you can in the links i provided. you'll come to understand more and more as you do. as of right now there's thousands of questions you could ask and still have issues or do something a little off... it happens. take advantage of the information we have here as best as you can, pick a species first and we'll be happy to guide you into understanding and through your builds. i really do think looking over as much of the info we have will do you better then asking lots of questions. some of these answers are a lot more complicated than what you're going to get and seemingly conflicting advice is going to be confusing. for example... i completely agree with EVERYTHING vivweaver posted... yet at the same time all i know you want is "marties viv"... that's not a lot to go by and unless you want everything exact including plant and frog species you may have to choose another direction at one point or another. 2 examples of that already are that you wont find epiweb and habacrete. you will not find the epiweb in the states anymore and the habacrete maybe too. even if you do people have had problems with the company lately that sells habacrete.
