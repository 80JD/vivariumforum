Ok, first, because it bugs me, the term is not herBs, it is herPs, with a P, herps. Like herpes without the extra E, lol...

IMO, and I have been keeping mixed tanks for a long time, your tank is FAR too small for the species that you are housing and the number of them you have chosen to house. The WTF really should have a taller vive than that, and it's too big to share such a small space with the other inhabitants. The RETFs are a BAD idea to mix with anything, much less FBTs... RETFs are very sensitive, and should be kept in species only tanks, the most you should mix with them is some fish, snails, and/or slugs.

As far as the amount of foliage goes, you're doing good in that regard. You need lots of foliage in a mixed tank, and you have plenty, and alot of it sturdy enough to hold the frogs' weight.

Now, sure, you think that it's a success, even though the WTF tried to eat a lizard. Sure, you say it's because you took the hide away, and after all, it was only once, right? What happened once will happen again, but next time the lizard might not be able to escape. Are you aware that WTF EAT lizards? That small anoles are often fed to them as treats?

If it were just the WTF, the lizards, and the FBTs, while still too small, it's a little more doable, but once you added the RETFs, you became FAR overstocked. The measure of whether something is overstocked doesn't go by numbers. There's no "animal/gallon" rule. You have to factor in the size of the animal, the space in the tank it will occupy, how far it moves when it moves (since frogs jump, it's important to consider their average jumping span), how much territorial space each animal needs, etc.

When animals are forced atop one another to get to food, sure, it's kinda cute, but it's not good. You see it as a sign that the animals are friends, and I understand that, my frogs used to sit on top of one another as well, in fact my squirrel frogs preferred to sit on my golden tree frog, but they had more than enough room to get away from one another if they wanted to. It's not a sign of friendship, especially with territorial frogs like RETFs, it's just a sign of overcrowding.


I'm going to say it: you should have done your research more thoroughly before adding more animals to an already overstocked tank. It was irresponsible. You aren't going to like what I am saying, I know that. You might find it rude. I'm not being rude though, I'm just being honest. I'm not going to coddle you, I'm going to tell it like it is, and it IS overstocked. You need to set the other tank back up or sell some animals. It is YOUR responsibility to provide these animals with the space they need, regardless of what your inlaws think. You should have at least one QT tank set up at all times anyway.

I suggest, in order to save space since it is an issue, that you mount a shelf above the current tank to hold the other one and set that one up for either the WTF or the RETFs.

You said it would cost too much to build a second vive, what did you do with everything that was in the old one? Did you move it to the new enclosure?

When mixing species, you have to give them MUCH more room than you would give them in a species only tank. My mixed tank (mixed no longer as my golden passed recently of old age) is a 40g breeder tank with a topper on that more than doubles the size of the tank. The mixed tank I will be setting up shortly will be in a 28g bowfront tank, and will house two native chorus frogs, a native salamander, and my native newt. All of these animals would come into contact with one another in the wild, and all are small and non-territorial. The salamander burrows, the newt stays in the water almost exclusively, and the frogs use the whole tank, but mostly the ground and BG. This is acceptable because I have considered each animal's niche, level of toxicity, and eating habits. I have kept all of these animals for over a year, separately. I have had my newt for ~3 yrs before deciding to risk mixing her with other herps due to her toxicity level. You can't just go buy animals and toss them in and hope for the best. You have to get to know each animal first. How will you know if the RETFs become sick? Frogs rarely show stress, as it would lead to a quick death in the wild. Usually, by the time you see signs of stress, the animal is knocking on death's door. You haven't kept these animals separately, have you? Did you even QT them?

I really not trying to be negative, I just don't think you have done this responsibly, and think that pointing these things out might help you have REAL success with mixing.
