The price wasn't too bad, I think it was something like $6 per bag. And I wouldn't say the additional weight was much of a deciding factor either, the real reason I might not recommend this approach would probably be the difficulty of applying the mortar mix and getting the right water ratio. And the messy aftermath.

The other reason I'm slightly turned off by this approach is the difficulty of finding an underwater substrate to match the color of the mortar mix. If I were to do it again I would probably use a dark brown 100% silicone caulking, smear it all over the G.S., then stick on bits of the underwater substrate, coco fiber, and the terrestrial substrate. For the underwater portion I would glue on just the underwater substrate (since the coco fiber and terrestrial substrate would slowly decay over time underwater).

This way there are consistent patterns and colors throughout the entire tank, replicative of a biotope in nature.

I believe the entire project took about 6 cans of G.S., however I had to get used to how much I could pile up in one spot and a lot was wasted in the learning process.
