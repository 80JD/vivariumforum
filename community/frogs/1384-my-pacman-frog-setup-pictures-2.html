Now now children, either behave yourselves or try and add little wit and ingenuity in your flames, make things a little less tedious for those of us that waste our time reading them.

Your setup looks good to me, milkman, very similar to my first pacman tank. As others have pointed out, though, you need to be aware that your new pal will trash it - dig up the plants, rearrage the substrate etc. Personaly I find simple to be the order of the day with pacmans. You might also be suprised at how much they eat and how quickly they can grow.

My prefered setup(I used to breed pacmans, I speak from experience) is much like yours but a little plainer - half and half land and water, constructed similarly but with false bottom and pump. Water changes with your current setup will become a bit of a chore as they can dirty the water quite quickly. Water level should be detmined by the size of the specimen - at rest its head should be above water. A setup as I describe neednt be complicated - a small submerged filter, egg crate a couple of inchs of the bottom sectioned down the middle with bogwood, a mesh bag of hydroleca(so it cant dig it up) on one side with substrate on top and some large pebbles on the other. Easy to clean, good filtration and water volume and the depth is easily adjustable.

Lightening the substrate with bark chippings and leaves to make it a little easier for it to dig and stop it getting to compacted is advisable. A large flat piece of bogwood, cork flat or tile/ceramic piping laid on he surface may make a better hide(it will burrow underneath).

Edit: this may also be a good time to consider culturing your own live foods depending on local availability/finances, they'll eat anything in quantity. Roachs, earth worms and waxworms are easy if your new to it.
