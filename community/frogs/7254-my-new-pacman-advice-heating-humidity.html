instead of a 10 gallon aquarium, a sterlite tupperware 5 gallons equivalent or larger is recommended. why? because it holds the heat and humidity much longer. simply get a long one (you can get a long tank with less height so it's actually more roomy than a 10 gallon although it takes up less space, since they dont jump) and drill airholes in the sides near the top. be warned though, it IS harder to get the zoo-med heatpads to stick to the sides, however not impossible (i did it with my pacman). good luck

ps. make sure you are using an appropriate sized heat pad for your terrarium size.

Your set-up looks ideal. You could throw some moss on top, as that would allow him to "burrow" as well as retain more moisture. Your lighting and heating are fine. The most important thing you have to worry about with this species is frequent water changes (and possibly substrate changes) due the high amount of ammonia in their waste that gets reabsorbed into its skin.
