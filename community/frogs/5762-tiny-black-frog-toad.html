Tiny black frog? Toad?
Hi, I'm new here. My 5-year old son LOVES frogs. And they are growing on me, too. Anyway, he brought home a green tree frog a year ago, and we have had it in a small 2.5 g aquarium for the past year. I finally decided to give him a better home, and am in the process of building a vivarium for him with an exo-terra 12x12x18 terrarium. But my question is actually not related to that - well at least not yet. The green tree frog is from SC. We now live in southeastern PA. A few weeks ago we discovered that we had thousands of tadpoles on top of our pool cover. We rescued as many as we could and released them into a small pond. We kept 4 as a little "project" for my son and put them into a kid's tadpole habitat kit. We've been feeding them flaked fish food. 2 of them turned into little tiny frogs yesterday. And I mean TINY! Even smaller than a fly. Sadly, I found one dead in the water section this morning. The other one is sitting on the land portion. I don't want him to end up dead, too. So here come the questions. How do I know if they are frogs or toads? Is it possible that the first one drowned? The second one looked to be struggling in the water this morning, so I very carefully helped him up onto the land portion. Should I move him to another container so he won't drown? I did some research, and I'm going to head to the pet store shortly to get some fruit flies... unless you all have another suggestion. We feed our tree frog little crickets, but this guy is only as big as a cricket! I don't know if I can find food small enough for the little guy! We still have two tadpoles swimming in the water portion and they have back legs now. I really want to help these 3 guys survive. And now my final question is, do you think I will eventually be able to put these guys in the same terrarium as the little green tree frog? I guess it will depend on what species they turn out to be and I have not had any luck figuring that out yet. Your help will be greatly appreciated!! Thank you in advance.

here's a list from pennsylvania...

1. Fowler's Toad-(Anaxyrus fowleri) formerly (Bufo fowleri)(P)

2. AmericanToad-(Anaxyrus americanus) formerly (Bufo americanus)(P)

3. Eastern Spadefoot Toad (Scaphiopus holbrooki)(P)

4. Bullfrog ((Lithobates catesbeianus) formerly (Rana catesbeiana)(P)2010

5. Eastern Gray Treefrog (Hyla versicolor)(P)

6. Wood frog (Lithobates sylvaticus) formerly(Rana sylvatica)(P)

7. Southern Leopard Frog (Lithobates sphenocephalus utricularius.)formerly Coastal Plain Leopard Frog (Rana sphenocephalalus utricularius),(P) 2010

8. Spring Peeper(Pseudacris crucifer) formerly Northern Spring Peeper (Pseudacris crucifer crucifer)(P)

9. Green Frog (Lithobates clamitans melanotus) formerly Northern Green Frog(Rana clamitans melanota)(P)

10. New Jersey Chorus Frog (Pseudacris kalmi) formerly(Pseudacris ferairum kalmi) 2010

11. Mountain Chorus Frog (Pseudacris brachyphona)

12. Pickerel Frog (Lithobates palustris) formerly (Rana palustris)(P)

13. Upland Chorus Frog (Pseudacris feriarum) formerly (Pseudacris feriarum feriarum)

14. Northern Leopard Frog-(Lithobates pipiens) formerly(Rana pipiens)

15. Northern Cricket Frog- (Acris crepitans) formerly Eastern Cricket Frog (Acris crepitans crepitans)(P)

16. Cope's Gray Treefrog (Hyla chrysoscelis)

17. Midland Chorus Frog (Pseudacris triseriata)formerly Western Chorus Frog


found here... Pennsylvania Wildlife - Showing Reptiles and Amphibians of PA List Page

if you look here it seems that all the frogs in our area that might have black legs are endangered or protected species... Pennsylvania Wildlife - Photo Gallery
