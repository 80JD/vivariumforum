Paludariums are really cool, but the best ones are the types with a somewhat equal amount of vertical height with the water and air, up to having twice the air height as water depth. the best type of frogs would be different types of tree frogs as they can utilize the entire air space with trees branches and vines while in the water if your looking for low maintinence, try out guppies and stick to the usual stocking formula.

the best type has an all water base with the second best having a moat like deal where you can have some land frogs provided they are able to easily exit the water should they venture or fall in. not to mention feeding with a water moat or base can get tricky too.

i would have no more than say 5-6 white tree frogs or a dozen american tree frogs in there. that would also be a good estimate for different types. I tend to do things differently from the "accepted norm" and have had stellar results. you could even get away with having fir bellied toads in the bottom and some type(s) of tree frogs using the canopy space

I wish I could answer more about reed frogs, but my range of specialties do not include them.

That sounds fine. I would suggest 3-5 reed frogs for an 18X18X24(45X45X60) exo. but ONLY reed frogs. mixing different species is not a good idea, as 1 of them will eventually be lunch.


Also I can't really find much out about the reed frogs, so would appreciate any help.
-How much do they 'swim'? do they swim or just occasionally lounge around in the water? Do they prefer shallow or deep water?
They don't really swim a lot, but they'll go in occasionally. shallow water

-Do they like to have lots of flat land, or would branches and the background be enough, (If I was to perhaps think about doing the floor nearly all water)
I would do somewhere along the line of 2/3 water or 1/2 water, as the land area will give the reed frogs a place to hunt for wingless fruit flies, crickets, etc...
-Is it OK to keep all the different types of Reed frogs together? There are many different species, yes? Should they be kept only with their own species? What abou breeding them? I assume you have to have the exact same reed frog species to be able to do that.
Mixing species is very taboo in the dart/reed/mantella hobby, they should be kept with the same species, especially if breeding is planned.

-Lastly (for now), Do you ever come across CB frogs? they always seem to be WC, and are CB much more expensive and much much harder to come across?
CB are fairly common. they range from $25-$60 depending on the species. WC are always riskier (chytrid, parasites, etc...) but both are common. just be sure to ask the breeder.


Thankyou
Em

Also, reed frogs from pet store WILL be WC, I would check a local reptile expo or join some more frog forums, for CB.
