I think if you actually calculated the cost of Pleh's background, and compared it to the typical method of foam covered in silicone/co-co, or other types of construction, they would be similar. He only has to pay 10$ a square foot for the stuff. It may even be cheaper since that is the only cost he had.

Using our methods you have to factor in the cost of everything he didnt use....Egg crate, expanding foam, silicone, coco fiber, land substrate, drainage rocks/lyca, window mesh, pvc piping, titebond glue, concrete, binder, paint.....ect ect ect. Whatever method floats you boat, will differ in cost.

I prefer using expanding foam covered in something, better then the bare foam method since it gives a more natural and uneven texture/color. Also, Pleh's background completely removes the need of a drainage layer since it is a paludarium. I think for palus it is a great technique, for vivaria, not as much.

Claymore, I think this is probably worth pursuing- there are other materials to Epiweb which are much cheaper that would work well as a drainage layer. I believe some people on Dendroboard have used Matala for this with good success.

I was thinking of doing something like a hydroponic ebb and flow system with Epiweb or similar placed horizontally, where the water would flood up into the material and water the roots for a while and then drain back out to let them breathe and dry out a little.

No plant technically needs soil (at least, that's my thinking)- hydroponics proves that, at least. Lots of plants would take to this method if you planned it right.
