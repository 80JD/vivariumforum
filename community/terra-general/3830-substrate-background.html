That's correct. You can't use wet co-co fiber (sp?) and have an adhesive hold it. So once it dries, you can add it with a second layer of silicone. Notice in this picture how dry it is:

1) there are one or two forms of spray foam which are still questionable about their toxicity when submerged.
2) if the gs does not cure completely due to a defect or layers that are too thick most forms will still be toxic as they will readily leach voc's.
3) the silicone is not only used for a glue, but also as a sealant at this point. this helps for several reasons one good example is that wet gs can fail and the bond between it and the glass can be broken due to it's properties when it's wet.

i will add that stuffing some in the gs while it is curing is not a bad idea to camouflage the gs before applying silicone and substrate. this way when the substrate breaks down the gs might not be so unsightly.

i must also add that when done correctly if the can is good gs usually is not toxic and sometimes (especially under the right conditions) will last considerably long under water without failure. this means applying thin enough to dry, giving it ample cure time, not making tall front heavy structures and some other things that may be specific to your build.

coco husk should be chunkier than coco fiber.
the husk is the entire outside of a coconut whereas the other should just be the fibers. eco-earth should just be the fibers. you can also get coco fibers from hydroponics places as well as some of the better gardening stores. finding eco-earth should prove easier though unless you search online.
