Your toads are always hidden because thay are always afraid of being eaten, if not by you then the Anoles or what thay represent. Give a small animal a place to hide and so it shall, especially with those on the lower end of the food chain. Recreating the enviroment to expose more of your toads will only lead to undue stress and possable illness related to a lack of security. I would leave it as is and remove the Anoles. Try a feeding ring or a far away section of the tank to feed the FBTs, so you can see them at feeding time at least before thay dart back to safety.

Now as i write this my friend asked me " how the hell is a FBT going to be afraid of an anole", and i told him .The toad may not be afraid of them but think about this. If you are in, lets say the wilds of Africa, and you stumble upon a baby Zebra, of course your not afraid of this tiny majestic beast, i mean come on, its a baby zebra, insted your attention should turn to scan the grassy backdrop and the possability of a pride of lions lying in wait. Its not the Zebra you should fear as the toads may not fear the anole's, but what kind of trouble those pesky lizards will gain the attention of.

Mind you i have no degrees in repitle psychology or even claim to know what a toad thinks. these are just the things that come to mind as i read your post.Ive never owned a FBT or an anole for that matter. I just design my tanks with the most hides i can afford to place, and a minimum of 3. 1 hot zone 1 mid range and one on the cool side, as well as an additional hide in each location for every addition i make to the stock. I used to keep one anole in with my FBT. I definitely do see the toad more now that I do not have the anole. I never noticed any stress to my toad, he was always out and about... However, I agree that your toads are likely stressed. The tank I used to keep the anole, FBT, golden tree frog, and squirrel frogs in was bigger than yours, and had plenty of room for the animals to stake territory. The anole had a territorial area off of the ground, up in the tall sturdy plants I had for him, and the branches that ran through the plants. He rarely went onto the ground. The FBT had territory around the pond. The tree frogs all stayed up high just like the anole, except when the squirrel frogs were sharing the hide with the FBT (they liked to sit on his head, lol). I agree that you should move the anoles to a different tank. If you want to keep it mixed, I recommend no smaller than a 4'x18"x2' enclosure (I do not know if a glass tank comes in that size, but go with a larger tank rather than a smaller one, and a high version of a tank over a shorter one). If you separate them, you should go with a shorter tank with more floor space for the FBTs, and a taller tank for the anoles (or keep them in the tank you are using now). I went to a reptile expo in Oct., and there was a vendor that had glass enclosures that I felt would be absolutely perfect for FBTs. They were low, square enclosures with a sliding lid built into the top frame. Don't get me wrong, FBTs like to climb from time to time, but they will usually only do it if they are trying to escape, if you provide enough approprate hiding spots for them, they rarely climb.

Using GS foam on the BG is not permanent. It can always be removed. It is actually not hard at all to scrape off of glass. Anything attached with silicone will be a little harder to remove, but still not overly difficult. Just use a metal scraper and start from the top and go to the bottom, the use a razor blade to scrape off any excess. You can sharpen the metal scraper if necessary with a whet stone (comes in really handy to have a scraper with a razor edge... I sharpened mine until I could shave my leg with it, lol... Then I proceeded to nearly amputate a finger... )

Beth, can you post a pic of your current setup? If I can get a better idea of what it looks like, I can help you better, as far as the anoles and FBTs being together is concerned. Please don't take this the wrong way, but the problem with keeping a mixed tank is that you are a beginner and you don't know how to look for the subtle signs that will tell you something is wrong with one of your inhabitants. Mixing is only recommended for people that have enough experience with the animals they are keeping to know if something is wrong with one of them. FBTs are toxic, so mixing them with something that is going to share their water source can be dangerous. Captive bred FBTs are much less toxic than wild caught specimens, but how can you know for sure yours are captive bred? Like I said, I mix FBTs with other species, but to start with it was because of inexperience. At this point I still have my FBTs with my golden tree frog, but I have had them for a long time now and know what to look for. Plus, the golden rarely goes into the water. The species that you currently have is a much better pairing for a beginner. If you MUST have a mixed tank, then keep the ones you have but get a bigger tank.

Generally, a foam BG is covered in silicone and coco fiber/peat moss or grout/thinset/concrete (like habbacrete) to make it look like rocks.

I collect "wild" plants for my vives, it is fine as long as you treat them for parasites and make sure they come from an area where no one uses pesticides. The easiest way to do this is to treat the plants with CO2, then keep them somewhere warm for a week or two and then do it again. Doing the treatment twice ensures that there are no eggs that will hatch into your vivarium. If you setup a larger tank, you can get it set up the way you want with plants from outside, do a CO2 treatment on the whole tank, then wait and do it again. That way you don't have to treat plants individually.

Wow, that tank is beautiful. I don't think you should change it at all!

Your anole staying brown all the time and strutting is a sign of stress. He feels like he has to dominate something, which is why he struts and displays his dewlap. I had less plants in my tank when I had the anole, and mine was always green. They are brown when they are stressed, too cold, or sick. They are sometimes referred to as american chameleons, but they don't change color based on the background color like chameleons do.

I think you should keep the anole in that tank and put the toads in something new. Keep it just the way it is, it is beautiful. If you want something that will spend more time on the ground, I hear that alot of people mix long tailed grass lizards with anoles without any problems. Please do alot of research first, though. I haven't done this so I cannot advise. Blue-tailed (or five-lined) skinks occupy some of the same areas as anoles in nature, so you might have success with mixing those lizards. The best amphibians to mix with green anoles are green tree frogs, barking tree frogs, and squirrel tree frogs (my favorite). I think your best bet for mixing would be to get two female anoles and two or three green tree frogs. You need to have two female anoles to one male anole otherwise he will bother the single female too much during breeding season and she will get stressed.

Some of the things I have read say that anoles are brown when they are relaxed. I promise this is not so. They should be a gorgeous, bright green all the time. How long have you had him? Maybe he just isn't settled yet. Here is a pic of my Brutus (may he rest in peace... ) and one of my squirrel frogs (Herman, I think):

He looked like this all the time. He was very healthy, full grown when I caught him, and lived with me for several years. I miss him, he was fun to watch (when he wasn't being lazy, lol). As you can see in the picture, the anole got along with the squirrel frogs just fine. These guys will inhabit the same bushes and trees in the wild, so living in close proximity is ok for them. The anoles are diurnal and the squirrel frogs are nocturnal, so mixing these gives you something to watch all day and all night. Squirrel frogs are hard to find in the pet trade, though, but GTFs are very similar and will also inhabit the same bushes in nature. Barking tree frogs are usually found closer to water, but you can mix them as long as you have a good water dish for them.

Really, I can't stress this enough... I don't think you should change that tank. It is too beautiful. It's great for anoles and some tree frogs. The only thing I would change at all is maybe plant a broad leafed plant close to the front on the left (maybe a dieffenbachia) and add a UV strip light (just switch one for one of the bulbs you already have). Definitely put another clamp lamp with a low wattage bulb in the back left to raise the temps a bit, they could be a little higher. Maybe try a 40watt bulb, cool temp should be 75, warm side 80, basking area should be 85-90. Here is a link to a great care sheet on them.

I hope this helps.
