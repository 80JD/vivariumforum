I really liked the concept of the false bottom and after I built my first vivarium.. I went all out and after I was done with it and thought it was perfect I found all kinds of things that I had to go back and change.. after a while you kinda start learing what works and what doesnt work.. its all trial and error.. granted asking questions on here helps from you making the same mistakes others have made.. I built my tank before I ever layed eyes on my darts and I didnt account for how small they would be because they were Juvis.. and my tank was a living pitfall for them lol.. luckily I had the additional 29 gallon that I setup as a holding tank until they got a lil larger and it gave me time to make the repairs needed for my 40 gallon...

I dont like how much space the false bottom will take up on a tank.. by the time you add your fb, your drainage layer, your soil, substrate, and moss across the top of your viv floor you have lost 5 to 7 inches if not more.. and on a vertical tank its not a big deal but on my 40 gallon now it looks as tall as like a 10 gallon.... which is not a big deal because my auratus dont really need height as much as ground space..

but it was a good learning exp for getting me ready to jump on my 100 gallon soon..

and trust me we all want to see pictures the more pictures the better

Quote:
Originally Posted by froggsong
Freecycle can sometimes be a treasure trove of vive supplies. I recommend joining and checking it frequently when you are looking for something or starting to plan a project.
I totally agree with checking out Freecycle.org. Our local group uses Yahoo! Groups, but I know other communities that use other web services. It can be tricky to locate one for your area, but using The Freecycle Network as a starting point can help a lot. I have received 4 out of 5 of my tanks through our local Freecycle. The 10-gallon tank that I currently use for my crickets (soon to move to a 20g long tank) came with a screen lid. Just remember that you'll most likely have to clean them out first. Believe it or not, my hex tank not only had the 3-piece glass lid (which I probably won't be using), but also included a stand...AND a pump already installed.

The last I knew, the most recent place I picked tanks up from still had more (some with lids too) to get rid of. If I had my own vehicle, I would've taken more than just the three I managed to get - as well as sheets of glass to use as dividers and such. The largest tank they had did have a crack near the top, but it could've been repaired or covered easily. Unfortunately, my ride didn't have enough room for it.

None of the tanks I got were in the best shape. But if your budget is anything like mine, you can't be too choosy.
