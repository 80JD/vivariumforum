First tank, 75gal w/ false bottom
So this is our first tank, harder than you can guess to find the right pieces here in town.


Here is the tank, a standard 75gal aquarium without hood, came with a rod iron stand when bought second hand.


We could not find anyone here in town that carried hydro clay pellets for the bottom of the tank to help with drainage so we decided to create a false bottom using eggcrate and pvc pipe. Also using eggrate to foam over for the backdrop.


This is the silicone we are using to glue stuff and the foam that will become the background.


Siliconed the spacers to the bottom to keep them from moving.


We cut the black eggcrate to size and made room for the water area and the ramp for easy access. We will eventually put a rock water fall in the water zone using a canister filter on the outside of the tank.


We attached most wood pieces to eggcrate using a screw and washer, we are planning to use the plastic dixie cups to put some bigger plants in. Next step is to foam everything in place and let it cure for 8h.


We foamed the cups thoroughly to hide them from sight and to make them stick good.


We got everything foamed tight, the wood will serve as both platforms and planting spots. We will cover the foam in silicone and add some coco-fiber to make it look like rock/dirt.
(Next time we will put something under the background before foaming to make it come off the "door" easier.)


Foam has hardened so decided to try it out in the tank for looks and size estimates. Turned out better than expected. Still need to trim bottom and top to make it a better fit and then figure out the best way to anchor it.

We decided to go with a drop in background to make it easier to hide hoses and cords.

Next we have to decide on substrate and try to make the stream and waterfall work good.

Well my fears were accurate. My background looks like it's balding in spots. Surprisingly it is not as bad as I thought it was going to be but I have a lot of repairs to do as can be seen in this pic

This is a great example on what happens if you don't use enough silicone or you let it skin over too much. Oh and if you use GE Silicone I do not, I repeat do NOT lean too close to inspect if you got a good coverage, the fumes almost knocked me out at least once and singed my nose hairs several times.

Oh I would also not recommend clear silicone, it makes it very hard to see what areas you have already gotten.

The rock area turned out much better but I might go back for a retouch of that area too just to make it little more uniformed. This tank will end up as a centerpiece for my living room when I'm done so I figured it can't hurt to be a little picky on it right?

IMO, you should go back over the bald spots with black silicone. When the coco fiber starts to fall off, it wont be as noticeable. From my own experience, type II GE silicone works better for BGs, it holds the coco fiber better, and doesn't shrink (causing some of the peat to fall off, and leaving exposed areas).

#1. Penn-Plax Cascade 500 Canister filter, it pumps 115 GPH and seems to offer excellent three stage filtration (If you believe the MFG)
