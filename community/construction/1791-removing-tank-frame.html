Removing Tank Frame
Hey Everyone,
I have gone nuts over the past few weeks trying to figure a way around this but thinking about it last night it is the only logical way to have my vivarium come out right!

So I am attempting a fairly complicated landscape in my 1st vivarium(my first mistake, but once I set my mind there's no fighting it!) and I have run into a wall(otherwise known as my center brace), I have already divided the background eggcrate into 2 peices that fit perfectly which I insert on an angle but no matter what I do I cannot attach the wanted "accessories" to these walls and still get them back into the tank due to that center brace.

So my next option and my real question is what do you all think about removing the entire top frame doing my work and then putting it back at the end of the build.

Has anybody done this with success, I cant find any sort of how-to on the net for installing or removing the frame. I know it cant be more then cutting some silicone but I don't want to bust it.

I am desperate for help here, my build is at a stand still and I am getting really frustrated..... Please Help!!!

the pressure on the sides of the tank is relative to the height of the water. at 5" of water you are below the halfway point(vertical) and still applying all of your pressure to the bottom frame, not the top one..
if i remember this discussion right from once before, structurally in a full tank, the front piece of glass has the same pressure from top to bottom( the pressure on the front pane at 16" deep is the same as 1" deep on the side walls). the pressure the manufacturer accounted for is 1/3 the volume of the actual tank. not the pressure 1/3 of the way up a full tank, because it's the same 2/3 the way up.

IMO if it was to separate at all, it would be from the bottom of the tank and *peel (for lack of a better word) upward(this is where your pressure is). maybe the engineer can interject, but i don't think you are applying any measurable amount of pressure to the seam at the top if the tank is only 1/4 full. the pressure on the silicone side seams dissipates as you move away from the water table.

Hey Waterkeeper, what's up Good to hear from you again, bud.

My two cents on this is I'm pretty sure what Jason and you are saying is the same thing. He meant that the Center Brace only starts to serve it's practucal function as a brace after the tank is greater than 1/3 full. (Because like you said there's barely any pressure at the top with this amount of water) As for the pressure, it's true the pressure is the same on the top of the pane as the bottom (The pressure is the same as long as the top edge is on center with the botom edge.) except the bottom pane is attached to the bottom (floor of the tank) piece and so it's reinforced all along the bottom edge as opposed to the top not being attached (except to the side pieces). That makes the top part structurally weaker and more likely to break, not the bottom. If one wanted to be really precise, I guess it could be argued that in a filled tank with no Center Brace and the top part of the front and back panes bowed, then there would be slightly more pressure there than on the bottom of the pane, because the shape begins to be conical, but I digress

I've acutally built aquariums in the past. I built the two sumps I'm using here myself. (Glass shop cut the pieces for me)

In a filled 48" tank without a Center Brace (that attaches the front and back panes at the top) you can really notice how the front and back panes bow out at the top. I've seen that on some older tanks that didn't have CB's. It's not so noticeable when you look at the tank from the front but when you put a glass sliding top on it, you can see how it's not square when it's full. It bows quite a lot - maybe 1/2". You can see the stress on the pane.

Without that CB there, when the tank is filled the bowing on the front and back panes creates stress points in the glass. The glass is not perfectly uniform in that there are weaker points in it than others. Although the thickness of the glass can handle the bowing from the water pressure, if the right spot (meaning one of the weak spots) is pushed on or tapped etc.. (meaning some external force applied) then the glass can crack or break. That's why all new tanks usually come with a CB. Second reason is esthetics. The top isn't quite sqaure and glass covers don't close properly due to bowing.
