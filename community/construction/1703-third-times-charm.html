I've been fairly impatient with my first two and want to really take my time on this one and make it real nice. So if you have any advice for any or all of my questions that would be great.

Depending on the humidity you need, having some screen in the door actually works better for ventilation than a screen top. I found when I had my frogs ten gallon on end that way, that even though the entire door was screen (I used the screen top I had for it when it was horizontal, I bent the bottom edge of the screen frame so that I could slid it up and down), I had much better humidity levels, as well as better temps, because the moisture and heat weren't rising out of the top.

As for not having a substrate, I'll have to take a look at the setup you are talking about. In my experience with the frogs I have, they are arboreal during the day but stay on the land at night when they hunt. Depending on what types of frogs you get, you'll need some land in order to feed them, I would think. You could make the landings that I made in my frog/toad tank, my frogs seem to love them. They are just made with GS and covered in peat moss, so there is no substrate to worry about with them unless you carve them out to add a plant (in which case any waste that lands there will just be fertilizer).

Also, a big point, be very careful if you are laying the GS down directly on the glass that it does not expand the crack that is already there. It will go through the tiniest cracks, so if you weren't planning to cover the surface in silicone first, at least cover that part. You don't want to let it cure and then when you look have the back entirely broken. I'm sure you know that already, I just wanted to make sure it was something you has thought about!

1: By now everyone already knows I favor drilled tanks, driven by sumps so I'd say definetly drill. Everything gets automated from the sump and there's very little maintenance afterwards. Too bad you didn't have a spare 48" tank because the cracked one could be used as your sump. For the water return question did you mean the type of pump you want in the sump? Or the water feature itself?

2: If you were thinking of drilling the end of the tank (your top) to ventilate (did you want to put fans on the holes?) then maybe leaving the top intact and drilling at the side front (your back wall at the top) might give you better ventilation and the vent hole(s) could come through and potentially be camouflaged by the GS. If you do put a sump in then automatic misting gets a lot easier and you can plan where to drill for that also. (or run the misting lines through the GS backing. Problem with a tank on it's end is that the footprint of the tank (and also the top) are pretty small so putting misting tubes or fans on the top will just allow even less light to get in the viv. Also I'm not sure about getting light down into the tank. Definitely see about Metal Halide fixtures is my two cents.

3: No substrate really depends on how you can care for your viv. Both my vivs (the two "showtanks" I have in my dinning room) don't have typical substrate. Basically I have sand and gravel in the bottom. Fish in the bottom and frogs on top. For example it could be a good setup for mossy frogs or even FBTs. What type of frogs did you have in mind?

One last thing.... I would make sure the tank is sealed before going on, since the front got cracked. You should have at least 1/4" thick glass on there so even though it's cracked it may still be able to take any stress. IMHO definitely seal with silicone before anything. I'd be very careful leak testing because a cracked tank can burst, especially one that size. An option to consider would be putting in another pane of glass (on the inside) in front of the cracked piece.

Dripping water into the viv whether it's from a water line or from a waterfall feature basically serves the same purpose. But misting and water dripping from something are different ways of humidifying. Misting is way faster. Tiny droplets are forced into the airspace of the viv instead of the water itself slowly evaporating.

Misting on it's own raises the humidity and cools the tank off slightly. If you used the fans to circulate air only (no fresh air) while misting, you would cool the tank even more. So these two elements working in tandem can be used to control your climate. If you're able to also have some opening to regulate a fresh air intake, that would give you even more control over the climate in the viv. So I would say "both" to answer your question. Both types are definitely desireable. Put both in if you can manage it. If can only choose one or the other and you're asking which should you choose between, then I'd recommend recirculating (no fresh air). As for placing the fans Waterkeeper and I were talking about that a little while back..... let me see if I can find the thread for you......

I can't speak for all FBTs but mine really like shallow water. They are at home in the water and actually will go dive down to the bottom and hide there awhile if startled suddenly. So in the wild these toads live in deeper ponds. Basically up to you.

For sure misting will also provide a water source for the plants in the viv. Usually misters run on a timer. So depends how frequently it's set to come on. If it stays on longer more water goes into the viv.

I think it's also fair to mention that not all misters are created equal. Some nozzles provide a much finer mist than others, obviously they cost more. So the finer the mist then the longer it stays in the air. When the misting nozzles don't produce such a fine mist, they'll put more water into the viv, so yeah it can act like a sprinkler, watering the plants.

I guess no one can tell you both dripline and a misting system in your viv is wrong. You could potentially put both in but choose how and when you use each of them. Doesn't mean you'd have to use both at the same time, or all the time for that fact.

Ideal humidity for RETFs ranges between 70% - 85% daytime and falls a little at night. Ideal humidity for a desert reptile would probably be much less. Depends what you want to put in there. For a FBT maybe around 65% but they will tend to stay very close to, if not right in, the water. So a misting system may not really be so necessary for these particular frogs but might still be necessary depending on the live plants you want to put in with them.
