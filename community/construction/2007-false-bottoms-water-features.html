Generally with a false bottom, the pool *IS* the bottom.

The stream runs down to the water underneath at the bottom of the tank, and the pool is just a part of that. If the water level is too deep, gravel/stones in the pool to make it more shallow. You can place the pump anywhere in the false bottom that is easy to access, and the hose carries water to the top of the falls, runs down the fall- down the stream- to the pond which is just an area lower than the substrate so you can see the water of the false bottom.. The weedblock/screen/mesh/sand/gravel/aquarium gravel/whatever--- will let water pass through from the pond to the rest of the false bottom.

It sounds to me like you're confusing the difference and benefits of a false bottom with a recirculating stand alone waterfall / pond.

There are other ways of doing it with a "closed system" like the prefab plastic waterfalls you can buy from zoo med which has a fall and pond but the water never leaves the system into the vive itself.

Lastly- If you want a pool big enough for fish and keep fish in the pond without getting under the "land" section... Most people silicone a piece of glass accross the bottom of the tank to make a water tight pool for the fish, and land on the other side of the glass partition- This sounds more like what you should be looking into. The downside of this method (not that I'm an expert on it- just read alot!) Is the water to land transition is usually rather abrupt... Imagine the glass is 3 inches high separating the tank into 2 parts... one side water, one side land.... there is a 3 inch drop from land to the bottom of the water- You can slope it with rock or gravel, or better yet, angle the glass you use for the partition...

Others can give you better specifics on this subject- I just do false bottoms with a pond- not the others I mentioned which may be more suitable for you.

Quote:
Originally Posted by Pittylova
...Also, is there any way to do a pool that has the open front for viewing that will be secure enough to the front glass so the fish will be ok and not make their way into the FB? Ultimately I'd like to cut off one of the sides of the bin to make it viewable through the glass...
My 2 cents: think of a FB as a ledge for a land portion... except that its the same footprint as the tank floor.

If you reduced the surface area of the FB and made it - lets say half of the tank (lengthwise) - and you stuck the (now halved) FB at the rear of the tank, then you could have a land portion at the back and fill the bottom of the tank with water up to the ledge. This way your fish would have the maximum amount of water because they could swim under the (halved) FB.

Then the front edge of the FB (probably eggcrate) would be exposed and this might not be a "natural" look. You just edge the eggcrate with a piece of branch or branches.

If you want a waterfall, place the pump or filter somewhere in the water section under the FB where you can have access to it later and have the output end so that it comes out of the top of the waterfeature. Pumps cam be placed vertically or water can be directed via tubing.
