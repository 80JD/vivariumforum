Man... I need a digi cam to show you my new tank still under construction.....

I GS'd it and covered in fine coco husk It's a corner tank, so I have 2 sides to spray.

1 side was a big can of gs triple expanding. I did the entire side at once since I punctured the can with a nail (nozzle didnt work) -I'm not recommending that btw.... but its what I did.... I covered it pretty thick- thicker than people recommend- but I didnt have a choice... couldnt stop the spray.

Other side was 2 cans of touch n foam landscape/pond. I like the GS better. I got way more coverage and depth. I was able to spray the foam in with "branches" of foam higher than the rest of the background. Some areas were thick enough, that after fully curing, I took a knife to it and cut the branches away from the rest of the background. Giving more depth to a backgroujnd really helps make it look more real. These foam "branches" really help with that.

The point is, you should put foam on in relatively thin layers, and let it cure- but you can go back over it in places to build it up thicker. let cure again, and spray some more....

I did one side in one day THICK- and another minor touch up-
the other side took 3 days. The first side with the GS can is much thicker too- and I think looks better...

Dont plan to just spray foam once and call it good. It will take at LEAST 1 touch up as well, but you can keep going at it to build up features like I did.

I covered mine with coco husk. I got the chunky kind from exo terra. I sifted it through a strainer, and got the small fibers out, and plucked some longer fibers out... and separated the large chunks.

I siliconed the background and used the short fibers for most of the background, and the long fibers for my "branches"- the different textures bring out the branches even more. I dont think people use dirt- usually coco fiber or peat or sand. Dirt probably will not stick well.

It boils down to the fact that there are some hardfast rules for success, as long as you follow the specific procedures... (foam is cured- your sand/coco/peat whatever is COMPLETELY dry before gluing, etc) follow those set procedures, but the rest is all creativity- do what you want- make it look however YOU want. However many layers of foam that takes.... Just let the foam fully cure before you silicone.
