wood can be very hard to leach. i have read about people leaching theirs for up to a month. unfortunately i think this is the good news. if the color of your water does not resemble tea that would be a good clue it's not your wood.

honestly, here comes the bad news, i think your water might be exposed to your gs somewhere. i think this is where your bubbles are coming from. your best bet might be to seal it wherever the water is getting in. it's a shame, but you may have to rebuild and cover your gs more thoroughly.

edit: warm water still might help and putting it directly in sunlight (in a window) might create bacterial growth (good thing). if you can sustain bacterial growth you may be ok... i wouldn't trust it though, especially if gs is your culprit.

We should test it with each type of GS, including the pond type. I can't afford to do it until I get started on another tank though... Maybe we can set up some parameters and then whoever wants to participate can follow them with however many types of GS they can do, and when everyone is done (in case we can't do it at the same time), we can compile the results.

We would definitely have to have the water agitated somehow, maybe with a bubble stone (it would be more effective, I think).

Four buckets, one with just water and a bubble stone (to be sure there isn't something in the water making it foam), one with uncovered GS and a bubble stone, one with partially covered GS and a bubble stone, and one with completely covered GS and (you guess it!) a bubble stone. If the two with exposed GS foam and the others don't we'll know it was the GS. If the two with silicone on them foam, we'll know it is the silicone.

Buckets should all be the same size, with the same amount of water inside. The bubble stones should all be the same size and run by separate pumps of the same size (each type could be done separately, one at a time, if there is an issue with having enough air pumps). The water should come from the same source for all buckets (if each sample is done separately, enough water should be set aside the first day so that it is the same water for each experiment). The eggcrate that the GS is sprayed on should be the same dimensions, and the GS should be as close in size as is possible. The time elapsed before foam starts should be recorded.

This experiment will likely lead to more so that we can figure out how to fix this problem, but we'll cross that bridge when we come to it.
