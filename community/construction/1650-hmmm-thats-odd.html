In MY experience, if you carve it it shrinks, if you don't it stays the same. Alot of the stuff that I carved for my frog/toad tank experiment shrank in the areas where I carved it, but not in the areas that I did not. The part that is smooth when after curing doesn't shrink, is what I mean. I think maybe when you carve it, since it opens the bubbles created by the expansion, the air pockets inside sort of collapse and it makes it shrink. I could be totally wrong though. Even the stuff that I heavily coated with silicone immediately after carving shrank like that. I actually like how it looks though, the places it shrank most were on the landings, and it made them look more natural.

I know the can says it will cure in eight hours but you really should wait a full day or more before carving or trimming it. I've used this stuff a lot for casting in latex molds. While the outside may be fully cured and hard in just eight hours, the inside is still "wet" like a marshmallow and prone to collapse. This is what causes it to shrink.
I prepare each mold with a non-stick surface to keep it from adhering to my molds and theirs almost always some shrinkage if I take it out too soon.
My guess would be it didn't stick well enough to your glass and collapsed some inside (from moving/carving/messing with it) before fully curing on the inside.
Did you apply silicone to the glass before spraying the GS to help in adhesion?
Hope that helps!
