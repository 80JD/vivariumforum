Rough Skinned Newt 10g Paludarium rebuild thread...
I decided to rebuild my newt's paludarium so she could get on and off the land portion more easily, and so it would look better than it did previously.

Here is a pic of the way it used to look:

This pic is a little old, I couldn't find one of what it looked like after I raised the land portion so I could add more water.

I can't get the pics of the construction of the land mass off of my camera right this minute because I can't find the cord, so I'll just have to write out what I did and show you pics of what it looks like IN the tank so far.

I measured a piece of eggcrate to the size of the tank, and then cut it in the front so that it did not cover the entire surface of the water. I also cut an opening in the back left corner for the water filter. Next, I sowed the mesh on with fishing line (very tedious, but it is very secure). GS doesn't float as high as I wanted this to float, so I also sortof sowed some pieces of polystyrene to the bottom. I did this by cutting out tight fitting holes in the eggcrate, wedging the polystyrene into the holes, and then using a needle to sow it to the eggcrate with fishing line so that they would not come loose. After that, I laid the GS on the top of the landmass and let it skin over. At that point, I molded the GS to the shape I wanted, hoping to avoid having to carve it at all. I wanted a stream going from the water filter across the tank to pour into the water, so I molded that so that the foam was mostly flat, with a lip on either side. I did have to carve it some, but only because I underestimated the flow of the water and I had to keep the stream from overflowing into the area where I will be adding dirt and plants. Originally, I intended for the entire area other than the stream bed to support plants, but my husband came up with a very good idea. I cut out the eggcrate and screen for the area in the back right corner so that I can plant a tall plant in the water, and have it extend out above the land so that she can climb up the plant to get onto the land. I will be using dieffenbachia and pothos in lieu of aquatic plants, because they grow well completely submerged, and also partially submerged. After I cut that portion out, it did not float as high as I wanted, since I had to move some of the polystyrene supports, so I decided to lay down a layer of GS on the bottom. I also did that so that you can no longer see the polystyrene. I let that cure for about 15-20 mins and then molded it and trimmed it with scissors. After that, I carved the stream bed a little better, and carved away some of the excess GS over the part where the plants will be. Now it floats considerably higher, and there is a pocket of air under where the substrate will be, this way I do not end up with a waterlogged area for my plants.

I will be covering the landmass with black/white silicone mixed to look like grey rock like pufferfan did in the crab tank, I think it would work alot better in this tank, plus it will allow me to cover the stream bed without loosing any of its depth.

What do you guys think so far? Any ideas/suggestions/problems you see?
