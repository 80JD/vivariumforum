You'd have to c a r e f u l l y remove the silicone from the glass where the frame is, then gently lift it off, if you feel any resistance, find the spot and cut it. You can do that by running a razor blade against the glass under the edge of the frame, on the inside and outside.

if you own a dremel or a grinder they will make short work of that support. removing the trim can be a pain in the a** to say the least. anyone who says carefully, in my opinion, really means stressfully with beads of sweat dripping throughout your entire house and a bucket full of excedrin to finish the job. if the tank is old or small enough, or if the trim moves easily you might be able to remove it with a razor blade without wanting to hit something. each tank is different, if yours comes off easily with a razor blade, go for it. if not break out the power tools and save yourself the energy, time, aggravation, discouragement, frustration, sweat and razor blades.

when they apply these, they just run a bead of silicone around the rim of the glass, and press on the frame, so most of your silicone is going to be in the hardest part to get to.
I think if you can get it off in 1 piece(I managed 1 out of about 17 tries, older tanks are always easier) it will make the best fitting lid.
just remember these frames have become increasingly harder to buy separate from the tank so don't break it and think it will be an easy replacement.
