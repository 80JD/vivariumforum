Is the coco husk you are talking about the bed-a-beast type? I know you said it is chunky, did you mean sortof like mulch? I use milled sphagnum in my tanks (I feel like I said that a thousand times! lmao Maybe I'll add it to my signature... ), and it drains fairly well, and if you generally only water by misting, it will let you know when you need to do a more through watering because the tank humidity will drop (it sucks moisture from the air if it is too dry, in my experience). From what I have read, the only benefits with coco husk over peat moss is that the coco husk wont erode as badly, and the coco husk retains moisture better and will release more into the air. I would NEVER recommend adding charcoal to the substrate for ONE reason (and I don't know why other people do), once it has absorbed everything it CAN, it will begin to release the toxins back into your substrate and water table. One of the reasons I use the peat moss is because it does so well for plants (and the cost, which is very low), it is the same stuff they use in those expandable seed starting disks. If the coco husk is doing well for you and you still have some and want to use it, you can just put a thin layer of some other substrate on top of it to hide it. Otherwise, I would just go with something like peat moss (it has just always worked SO well for me), and maybe mix in a couple of handfuls of medium sized gravel, just to give the roots something to grow around and help keep the soil aerated a bit. Depending on the type of plants you put in, you might want to mix in a little sand as well (some plants do better in sandier soil, like roses, if I remember correctly... I know you aren't going to plant roses in your vive, but other plants are like that too, and it was the only one I could pull out of the top of my head, lol). Definitely add a few earthworms (wigglers or nightcrawler, it doesn't matter which), and try to find some springtails. If you don't add the springtails, they will likely get in there eventually anyway, but it is beneficial to add them from the start. Other than mixing in the gravel and maybe a little sand from time to time depending on what I am planting, I don't mix any other soils in my peat moss. I do sometimes mix plant clippings into the soil when I replant, it helps to fertilize a bit. I hope that helps you at all... If you need any clarification or have any other questions, just ask...

coconut would work perfectly for moss. But i don't think it would be great for plants...I am not a specialist there, but i believe you will prefer a high humidity in the air, not in your substrate.

Postal, that is pretty much what i was saying. When froggsong talks about sphagnum, he gives you a better alternative i believe. Sphagnum will absorb humidity in the substrat. But when it get saturated, water will drip from it, and since it is the layer on the eggcrate, the water will fall back into the reservoir. With coconut as a layer there, it will absorb a lot more than what sphagnum can. Wich could end up with way to much humidity in the soil by itself....My 2 cents.
