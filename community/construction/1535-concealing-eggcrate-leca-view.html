Concealing Eggcrate/LECA From View
Okay, I'm preparing to do a complete tank rebuild my 10 gallon as practice before I begin work on my 120g but I did have a question about the eggcrate and LECA...
What's the best way to conceal these layers of eggcrate/screen/LECA/screen from view???
I plan on building a water portion, much like I have now, and was wondering about any tips on creating a realistic shoreline.
I read that one way is to glue rocks equal to the height of your soil, concealing the eggcrate from front view, but what about the side view?
Also, can you silicone the coco fiber to these rocks for a more natural "pond" look? Would the water wick from this to the main soil and would it be able to stay attached being constantly wet?
As always, any help and all suggestions are always welcome.

No one really worries about concealing the substrate layers and to be honest it's not really worth it especially if you're planning on a water portion. I don't recommend it for a few reasons but I think maybe the best way might be to smear a thin layer of black silicone on the inside of the tank where you want to hide the substrate. You can use a single edged razor blade to cut and scrape away the excess once it's dried. Obviously, take caution not to remove any silicone that's sealing the tank glass sides and bottom when removing excess black silicone.

If you want to make a realistic shoreline, what I did was go get a fake rocks at the local pet store and use them to shore up the substrate of the land section. The way I did it is I used a one inch layer of river stones 1/4" - 3/8" dia. Except at where I planned the water section at the front side of the tank (where most of the viewing took place from) - I didn't put any stones there.

Then I covered that with a good inch of sand. So at the back it was about 2" high but sloped down to 1" at the front side but only sand, no rocks under. It looks much better this way.

Then another layer of riverstones where I planned to have the land section. Then I placed the fake rocks on top of the outer edge limit of the riverstones. So the fake rocks act like a bulkhead for the land substrate. It's like a 4th side across the tank holding back the substrate from the water section.

Then more sand in the land section. Another layer of riverstones and finally the last layer of sand to bring the land section height equal to the height of the fake rocks. I have a good 7 inches of rocks and sand there.

Now if you leave the sand there it will get washed into the water section eventually. Also if you want to have a waterfall in the land section, the water coming from the waterfall will wash the sand away also. What I did to remedy that was that while I was filling up the land section with sand and riverstones behind the fake rocks I also piled flat slate pieces one on top of the other in the back corner. When the land section was filled to the height of the fake rocks the flat slate pieces were level with that also. I found thin pieces of slate and covered the land section with them. So finally the whole land section was covered in thin slate with the sand visible between the pieces. Then I kept piling the flat pieces of slate up in the corner untill the waterfall was the desired height - about 14 - 15 inches high.

This way when the water flows down the slate to the land section, it doesn't wash away the sand.

This can also be done with good quality drift wood instead of fake rocks (or both) I actually used both.

Keep in mind if you fill up the water section to the height of the fake rocks which in my viv is just (about 1/4") below the level of the land area, then the whole land area will also be waterlogged. I know this is not for everyone meaning not everyone likes this idea because they want to have plants in thier viv that will root in soil not waterlogged sand. (And what I did to solve THAT problem is another post entirely )

To further keep the land section together I planted lucky bamboo as I went along in the land section. The stems come up through the edges between the flat slate pieces covering the top of the sand in the land section. The root systems hold everything together. There's maybe 7 or 8 bamboo in there.

Whew what a novel. Anyways that's it....

Anyway... all that sand sounds like it would make a tank very heavy...

I took a look at your ten gallon, and it looks very nice. Those frogs in there are the ones native to South Carolina, right? If so, you need more plants and places for them to hide in there. I grew up playing with these guys, so I know what kinds of places they like to live. If you have enough good tall bushy plants in there, they wont jump on the glass as much, in my experience. (I have some very similar frogs that are from the same area). When you redo your tank, try to put in some tall plants with broad leaves that have growth from the top to the bottom of the plant. Maybe some sturdy grasses and some thin branches, going across in different directions (they like to have lots of stuff to jump on, especially when hunting. Actually, some thin branches with some pothos or ivy climbing on them would work very nicely (though you'll probably have to prune it fairly often). Having more plants will make the frogs more comfortable, and also help keep the humidity up. They like a good hide too, even a piece of pipe or a broken brick (they will hide in those in the wild, so you don't have to worry about the brick being to rough). While a water feature is a must (a stream is perfect for these guys), a larger pool isn't necessary, because they don't generally swim much, or at least not that I've seen. They only need water deep enough to sit in. So I'd focus on a nice stream bed and a small shallow pool. And of course daily misting. If you do decide to keep the deeper pond, I'd plant some smallish water grasses in there, they will really like that. I know this is off topic, but I thought I should share since I grew up where those guys were native. Of course, if those aren't the frogs I think they are, then disregard the entire thing (although most of what I said can apply to most frogs).

Good luck with the rebuild. I hope I helped at least a little.
