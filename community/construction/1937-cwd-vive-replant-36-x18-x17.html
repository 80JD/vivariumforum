It IS very short, but it was what I had when I bought him. I am in the process of building him a bigger enclosure (though not the final size he'll need, don't have room for that atm), but I just started a new job, and I haven't had much time to work on it. I posted the dimensions of the tank in the thread title though, lol... It is either 18 inches high, or 18 inches from front to back, can't remember which... I think it is 17 high though... One inch isn't that big of a difference, I just can't remember atm which way is which. There is also no FB, so don't think I am taking up precious inches with wasted space (this tank has always done VERY well without a FB, or even a drainage layer. It gets misted daily, but not watered to the point that the substrate is soggy, so I don't feel a real need for it. All of the plants do VERY well). Here's a couple of pics of him in his new setup:






As far as upward room for him to climb is concerned, there is a lower level of ground in the back (it slopes up to the front, fairly steeply), and he has plenty of branches to climb on (though not as many as before, it was too cluttered). I wanted to give him more useable floor space now that he is bigger, mostly so he would have more room to hunt, now that he finally does. Some of the plants on the pics obscure the view of the ground, so it doesn't look like there is as much as there is. He loves to sleep nestled under one plant or another, so I like to make sure he has plenty of spots to choose from (can't get him to use a hide at all). His branches also go all the way to the top of the tank, so he has a very good temp gradient up and down, and not AS much of one from side to side (though the side the pond is on is cooler than other other side). Alot of the plants in there will also support his weight and stand up to a trampling, so he really has alot more room than you would think he does.

I water them with a watering can stuck down into the substrate a couple times a month, to give them a really good watering (I stick the spout into the substrate because I use uncovered milled sphagnum peat moss, and it will erode if water is poured on it), sometimes I will just pour water into the open space where my pond usually sits when I take it out to clean it. Other than that, it gets misted (fairly heavily) a couple times a day, and that keeps the plants hydrated. I'm actually a little surprised it has worked out this way for so long, but it has worked out really well.

My dragon seems much happier now that the ground in his tank is level again... I was beginning to get worried about him.
