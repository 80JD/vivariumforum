120g Viv Project Need a Little Help
I have been lurking for sometime, and have loved what others are doing with their vivs. I have been inspired to create a more realistic habitat for our two CWDs (Chinese Water Dragons). I recently acquired a 120g Vertical tank 24"x24"x48" for a song compared to the cost new! Before anyone starts in on me I realize this isn't large enough for fully mature CWDs, ours are juveniles, and we should be moving somewhere more permanent in a year or so, so this is better then their current living quarters, a 36"x16"x16" tank. When we do move I will be building them a larger home until then this should help take advantage of their semi arboreal nature.




As I said I have been lurking and doing my research and have almost all my materials ready, except I have yet to figure out the plumbing for the filter and water feature. I think my best bet is a sump but I have little knowledge of these systems and have a couple of questions. How large does the sump have to be. I will be using it as intake for our ZooMed 501 canister filter as well as the submersible heater. The water volume I am anticipating would be 15g this is based on a false bottom with a water height of 6", I would like the pool to be deeper however there is a screen 7" from the bottom of the tank on one side, which I will be replacing with plexi doors, so I am limited to that depth.

I was planning on building a three sided glass box (four sided if you include the bottom) and attaching it to the lower back of the tank. Something along the lines of 12"x4"x7" would this be suitable, please correct me if not. Here is a pic along the same lines of what I would like to do:

Good idea Tiamat! Frosty, that really sucks that it didn't turn out the way you wanted it to... However, in my experience, the GS only shrinks when you do not let it cure fully before carving it. If you have a thinner layer, it needs to cure at least 24 hours before carving to avoid shrinkage, and if you have a THICK layer, it needs closer to 48 (possibly longer if it is VERY thick). The cure time on the can isn't as accurate as it should be, but you have to keep in mind that it isn't meant to be applied the way we are using it. I hope that helps both you and the OP, for now and in the future.
