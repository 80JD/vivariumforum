as far as the pump goes this is what i have in my tank Fluval 2 Internal plus filter 104 GPH
it is able to be used as an in gravel filter and works wonderfully has a great flow for a water fall as for in my tank since i havnt set my FB tank yet i ran the discharge to a piece of cork bark with a hole carved in it and it provides a nice waterfall look in a FB tank you would have more freedom since you could design your GS around the discharge hose to create your water fall where ever and however you want weather you want it going directly into your water or creating a small pool and a river and draining into your water you could create the pool and river by using a small bowl or something similar thereto and covering it in GS to form your pool basin then where ever you would want your river coming off of the pool use 2" PVC cut in half length wise and cast a mold with GS and trail it of into your water obviously it would have to go down a slight slope cover your pond and river molds in a layer of silicone just like you would with your BG then place sand or small pebbles on it for the looks =)

filters work by straining fine particles and providing area for bacteria to grow. The bacteria eat organic material after it has already rotted and introduced ammonia into the water column. Bacteria break down ammonia into nitrites and nitrates. Things like shrimp dont really clean anything, they just help the breakdown process helping to turn larger portions of organic material into smaller ones that will rot faster and turn into ammonia faster. The final product in the chain of the nitrogen cycle is nitrates. Toxins, minerals, salts ect. do not evaporate and there for must be removed, either manually by changing a percentage of the water, usually 20 to 50 percent every one or two weeks depending on the bio-load, or by introducing a large amount of plants. It was originally thought that plants could only use nitrates as fuel but it has now been discovered in the aquarium hobby that fast growing plants like duckweed, water sprite,elodea, water lettuce, giant sylvania ect can use the ammonia directly from the water column and have no need for it to be broken down. These are all very fast growing high light plants that grow emergent with no need for there roots to be in a substrate. Combining these with more permanent plants rooted under the water and in the soil above you can have a very efficient filter. It is still recommended that you use something to create a water flow so the toxins or nutrients can be used efficiently by the plants.

If a terrarium is in balance with the correct ratio of plants to animals then it should only be broken down when the soil has completely degraded. Eco earth and coconut fibers, orchid bark, and there are other brand names that actually take a lot longer, are good for terrarium use because of the high ratio of cellulose, it takes longer to decompose. This process can be prolonged by adding newer substrate on occasion and allowing the leaf litter to accumulate much in the way of mulching and adding compost to a garden every year. But because it is a closed system it can not sustain itself forever. I would imagine that a well maintained balanced system with a water table could go on for no more then 5 years before all the planting substrate would be complete mush. I would suggest replacing all or part of the substrates ina system such as this every one to two years.
