No offence, but that actually makes zero sense to me lol :P ...Maybe I'm not understanding what you are wanting for the end result...The paint would wash off since the clay is in a constant moist/soft state. I think the only way to add colour, is to actually mix seperate clay batches using different types of substrate, or to just rub the clay with the substrates after it has been applied to the glass.

As far as my searching and oogling at other people's vivs go, I havent come across a build that has tried what you are suggesting. Not to say it cant be done though.

What would your intentions be by coating the clay other then creating inconsistencies (like in nature). I think it would be difficult to have any part be removable, since its just to sticky and requires the humidity.

I know you could definitely make various different colour clay mixtures. A lot of peat moss will result in a background similar to my own, co-co fiber will give you a nice brown, leaving the clay "pure" will give you a rockie grey colour, or using red art clay will give you a red background. Im sure you could even go as far as coating the clay in sand to give it a rockie look, but it may degrade quite quickly and the frogs will most likely pick a lot of it up on their skin.

The main bonus of using the clay background, is so all of it can be covered in plants Thats what I love to see anyways. The greener the better imo.

There is no wait time, or "carving" for the clay method...Just mix it up and slap it on the glass. You could essentially place your frogs in the tank on the same day, since there is nothing to let cure and no harmful vapours. The clay should never dry out, and it shouldnt become super hard either. If I wanted to, I could mold the background into another design since the clay is practically the same consistency as the day I added it to the tank.

You should start a thread and post pictures of what you are explaining Im more of a visual guy and could probably give you better insight and advice.
