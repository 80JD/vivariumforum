you need a sink screen in the output end of your filter imo. after the media and before the threaded hose barb. that will make your media last much longer before it clogs. if you look at a sink screen sideways it looks like a "(" figure the ends will go towards the outlet/threaded end of the hose barb and the center will aim towards your media effectively keeping it out of the smaller diameter hose. this will keep the water from being forced through a narrower area in the media where it will clog much faster.

if you could figure a way to feed the water through the core of the media and have it move from center outward into your hose and then carried into the outlet it should really help with the design flaws of such a design.

for examples of what i'm talking about think about whole house and r/o filter cartridges for example. another good example would be the magnum 350 and i believe the h.o.t. mag 250 both by marineland.

intake filters for powerheads work on the same premise also to keep from clogging often.

Thanks for the tips beck. If this doesnt work out, I'll think of redesigning it. I will definitely take your advice about a sink screen insert for now to avoid clogging the outlet hose. Good thinking.

I turned it on and had it running throughout the night. My water cleared right up, and flow is still smooth and even through both output tubes to each stream. No where did I say this is a permanent and 100% reliable fix, but for under 10$ I figured I'd give it a try to reduce my maintenance for now. And like I said, the water in the false bottom does not flow quickly, so particles settle. If I dont need to move the tank for syphoning purposes, they shouldnt even have to be filtered. They can just chillax on the bottom glass :P

I may build a large filter box surrounding the pump also as a preliminary larger filter. Then use this for any remaining particles that get through.

And as for the ease of cleaning and replacing the filtering media, it is very simple. The black end sticks into the pump, and the other 1/8" tube goes into a quick connect T. Each brass fitting just slides into the large tube, so all joint are easily able to pull apart quickly. Id much rather take the assembly out and replace the media elsewhere, then to have to drag the tank across the house, and spend 20-30 minutes syphoning, refilling, and repeating until the particles are removed. All while disturbing the darts.

I'll let you guys know how this little experiment goes over the next week. If it lasts longer then 3 days while keeping the water clean, I'm ahead of where I was before
