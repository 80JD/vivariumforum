Thanks a lot.

And using this clay mixture, you can not have the clay constantly wet. It will disintegrate very quickly. Some people have had success using a mixture of 75% Red Art clay, and 25% calcium bentonite. In any case, do a month long test run on whatever mixture you want to use.

As for the clay, as long as it is kept humid and moist it will be fine. The top inch of my background is beginning to crack, however it is because my old glass top used to come loose and let dry air inside, and because the clay is only 1/4" thick. A couple tips would be to allow for 1-2" of bare glass above the clay, and to ensure the clay is at least 3/4" thick in all places, especially the top. The bare glass will allow for condensation to accumulate, then drip down on the clay to keep it moist.

The moss took 2 weeks to acclimate. Now it can withstand a dry spell, but it is all growing like weeds, especially on the water sections.

And that is a great point about the water seeping up from the substrate. I have noticed that the lower sections of the clay are getting softer. If you notice in my second build, I kept the clay 2-3" above the false bottom. I didnt mention in the video because I forgot, but that is the main reason why I didnt build it all the way down.

The only way to have a successful vivarium like this is to have lighting on timers. Most people are home at the same time everyday, but sometimes I dont make it home to maintain everything so that is what the timers are for. I did have someone come and mist every few days, but thats it.

I have the lights on for 12 hours. And for these twin tanks on the top shelf, I only use a single 48" fixture, with 2 GE Daylight T8 tubes (6500k). The fixture and tubes cost just under 50$, and do great, so dont go looking for overpriced lighting at pet/aquarium stores...Been there, done that, and they suck anyways.

In my future large display tank, I plan to either have a controler that will gradually turn the light on/off to mimic the sun. If cost wont permit that, I will have the brightest lights come on for 8-9 hours, and have less powerfull lights for the start and end of each day.
