Quote:
Originally Posted by Greg H.
See that is one of the reasons why I don't like the idea of using clay for background ( heavy, need to maintain or repair/replace it, susceptible to mechanical degradation ), and why I tend to opt for a porous cement background.

It doesn't matter if it gets wet or dries - it's still going to offer good mechanical stability for years, and is going to be lighter ( either wet or dry ) than clay.
Im not posting this thread to show that clay is the one and only way to make a background, just as an alternative....But I'll tell you one thing, there is a reason for this method being the most popular new methods of today for dart vivariums. Once you try it for yourself after having experience with other methods, the pros greatly outweigh to cons and you wont want to do anything else.

Edit: Also, I have experience with the Titebond method. This is very similar to Concret/Binder background method because it creates a rock hard background....Im almost certain that the Titebond method, even though expensive, has an easier and quicker application process then concrete and can last just as long.....I prefer clay by a long shot to this. Plus, this background cost me less then 10$!

Originally Posted by Grimm

Please do not get me wrong, I'm not trying to say that one method is better than another for all set-ups.

Just stating that for various reasons my preference was for another method ( LOL ...... then again I prefer natives and moving water over dart's as well ), and the cost for my last set-up was about the same - $6 for a 80 lb bag of cement and $5 for a compressed bag of aspen animal bedding and 10 lbs of free newspaper for the background in my 80 gal tank ( and had plenty left over ).

I did consider doing something similar to the clay method ( as well as painted GS and polystyrene foam ), but after taking everything into consideration, moving water, weight, mechanical strength of the material, animals in the Viv ( some larger tiger salamanders ), looks and the fact that I would most likely be moving the tank my self from time to time, I finally opted for the method I used.

As for popularity of the method, I never was one for doing something that was different, just because it was different, or because everyone else was doing it. I like the idea of a clay based substrate, and it has enough merit for me to try it in my next set-up ( as long as I can work out the water feature to go along with it to my satisfaction ), but I am not comfortable with the use of clay for a background material, especially where it has to be kept damp/wet to look decent - I just can not get past the poor mechanical properties and weight of wet clay but that aside, I have no doubt that a clay background will work in some set-ups, especially when one is trying to do it in a minimum amount of time and the weight is not going to be an issue.
