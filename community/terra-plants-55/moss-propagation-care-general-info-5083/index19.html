Quote:
Originally Posted by Greg H.
If minerals in water are detrimental to ~95% of moss varieties, why not just seek out the other 5%?

Surely this might be easier ( and cheaper ) in the long run than trying to deal with hard water issues?
This issue is really so variable as tap water is not the same around the country or even in the same region. Plus whatever additives are put into the water by city water departments will have a bearing on the impact such water makes on a colony of moss. Another variable that compounds really trying to generalize accurately is the nature of the substrate the moss is growing on.
Presuming that all city water is pH buffered to 7.0 then pH is not an issue for the mosses. What really is an issue is if the water drains off rapidly and is washed off the mosses by regular daily watering, perhaps followed with a flush of distilled or r/o water.
Many commercial nurseries that water frequently will be hosts to unwanted liverworts and mosses in their plant containers irregardless of the fact that only city water is used. For a confined space such as a terrarium distilled or r/o water are requred for aquatic or terrestrial plantings but not for sites where water movement is constant.
R/O water is really the best to use for any purpose and for any substrate, including paper or artificial felt.

Greg H,
Not too sure if there really is an issue at all. Everything depends on how you construct your terrarium and what you build up your substrate to be. If it is a closed system then everything including water would be entirely contained and recycled within the system. Such systems are not within the scope of most terrarium-ists. So the problem of water that will feed a variety of plant life without endangering any of the plant life is a question of irrigation, recycling, and substrate characteristics. What makes it really doable to combine mosses with more nitrogen dependent vascular plants is the use of the felt system. The substrate can be constructed to house essential nutrients and then covered where desired by a felt pad to house mosses. If percolation and run-off are directed into a collection tank or feeder pool then the excess water can be removed from the system. For natural systems r/o water should be ok as it is only slightly acidic and possibly less than that is currently falling in the natural world. But for a natural system the type of system that is being used may mean that less watering is needed at all except for those habitats constructed to replicate areas where there is high precipitation and in such a case r/o should pose little risk for plants that are already exposed to such levels of acidity in the normal rain cycles.
I would think that this issue is not as vital as is the construction of the terrarium and the substrate.

Greg H.
I would stick to felt as coir and tree fern seem to be a more demanding substrate for moss to grow on. The use of a plastic layer for the felt system is for outdoor applications. My own feelings on using felt based moss colonies is to grow them outside in order to get a colony established and looking good and then cut to size those portions for the spots in the terrarium. In case there is any problem with the moss colony in the terrarium then you would have more to work with already established outside. While wicking may occur between the felt and the substrate I cannot state what kind of impact such would make as regards the carry-up of Ca or other ions. If such occurs and moss growth is curtailed then the felt could be glued to any type of nonpermeable base and then located into the terrarium.
Misting is more than adequate when they are confined to a terrarium though an occasional wash down would be beneficial. The mosses I grow are outside and are subjected to humidity levels that range from 50% or less in PM to 80% or less in early AM with the possibility of wind. Misting is my usual method of countering dessication with maybe weekly watering, all using R/O water.

You can grow moss anywhere IF you have the time and resources to do so.
