Hi hagis30, and welcome to the forum. It really makes me happy when I see parent-child duos here- it's inspiring and gives me a shred of hope for the future.

As regards your questions, I'm sure you'll get some more definite answers than mine, but I believe that substrate microfauna is completely harmless and is often used with dart frogs as both a food source and an aid to breaking down decaying waste/plant matter, as are the worms (I'm sorry I can't help you with which variety). I think with both of them the only thing you have to make sure of is that they continue to have enough to feed on, which is ordinarily leaf litter that needs to be added to the top of the substrate periodically.

As I said, someone else should be able to better inform you of the subtleties of keeping these little guys- post a specific question elsewhere on the forum if you don't get the info you need here in your introduction.

As far as worms go, you want "red wiggler" worms. They break down leaf litter, and you only want about 5 per cubic foot (if I have to give an actual measure, lol) to start, unless you notice them being eaten. The red wigglers are fine as food, and won't hurt your tank. They'll be quite beneficial, aerating your soil and keeping it fertile.

Springtails and isopods (woodlice, roly polies, etc), are great additions to a tank. They also break down leaf litter and any other decomposing matter. Isopods will likely be a snack from time to time when they venture into the open, but that's ok. They aren't high in chitin, like alot of bugs, they are crustaceans so their shells are high in calcium. They are a good supplemental feeder for this reason, natural sources of calcium are ALWAYS preferred to artificial sources. You can throw in a handful (not literally) of springtails, and throw in a fistful (literally) of isopods, watching to see how many are eaten right away. The isopods will sometimes nibble plant roots if they don't have adequate leaf litter/decomposing stuff to eat. Make sure that they soil is moist enough, they breathe with modified gills.
