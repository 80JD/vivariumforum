A Leopard Gecko is very easy to care for.

-A 10g tank is good for 1 gecko, and is a bit cramped for 2
-You don't need a lid for the tank unless you have a climbable background, or if your afraid crickets will hop out, but I've never seen a cricket with that good of hops.
-You'll need a source of heat, either a heat lamp or heat pad will work.
-2 hides, one of both sides (warm side and cool side)
-Water, food, and supplement dish

-Lastly some love.

I that should be all for bare minimum.

bare minimum requirements:
- tank
- UTH (under-tank heater) * i highly recommend getting this. leopard geckos need belly heat to properly digest their food. you can get away with using an over-head light heating a tile floor, but the UTH is much more reliable & consistent.
- digital thermometer with probe to monitor floor temps.
- rheostat, thermostat, or dimmer for UTH. * again, i highly recommend this. you need a way to control the amount of heat or you could end up burning your pet.
- daylight bulb on timer or turned on/off at consistent times giving consistent day/night schedule
- 3 hides (one on warm side, one on cool side, & a humid hide on the warm side).
- water dish
- calcium powder/food dish
- calcium powder w/ vitamin D3
- pure calcium powder
- some sort of substrate (can be as simple as paper towels, or repti-carpet, tile, slate, shelf liner, grout floor)

additional optional components:
- night time viewing bulb (red)
- reptovite reptile multi-vitamin
- tank decor
- tank cleaning products (wipe out)

as you said that you have read many care sheets i'm assuming you know the basics i.e. offering a temperature gradient by only heating half the viv. a side note about the rheostat, thermostat, or dimmer is a thermostat, though being minutely accurate & having things like night-time temp drops, are very expensive ($100 or more) & usually mostly for ppl who have many geckos. rheostats are more affordable (~$25) but are far less accurate & are basically just a dimmer. if you have any wiring knowledge you could wire your own dimmer (i.e. rotary push dimmer) for far less money. also, you could be as cheap & utilitarian as possible by using paper towels as substrate, and turned over empty pastic containers for hides:
