What's the grain size like on that, jpstod? Is it fine and dusty or more coarse? It's definitely got a nice look to it.

@ claymore:
Impaction is a risk with ANY substrate. Those of us that choose to use more natural materials to achieve something closer to what the animal would be living in in nature just have to be sure we understand ALL the factors that cause impaction. Substrate is far from the only culprit and not the most common cause (chitin from bugs like mealworms and even crickets is the most common cause of impaction, NOT substrate). Most often, the folks that are so against sandy substrates because they have had impactions in the past are also those that feed lots of mealworms or mostly crickets, etc. Calcium absorption is also a factor in cases of impaction. Proper absorption of calcium aids in digestion, helping to move anything through the digestive tract that can't be used by the body. Also, alot of keepers that don't know better purchase calci-sand, since it is highly recommended in the pet stores. The animals eat it because they aren't getting enough calcium and get impacted. I guess what I'm saying is that substrate isn't the only factor, and with someone who keeps so many herps and is as knowledgeable as jpstod, it isn't necessary to point out that the sand is a problem, especially since it isn't always a problem.
