Some people hook up a cool mist humidifier. Many also have a misting system to automatically mist the enclosure. Though there are a few that replicate rain, most just change the amount of misting time to replicate a rainy season.

Broms are native to most pdfs. How much the frog will use the brom depends on the type of frog, and individual tastes of the frog. But, it is generally recommended to at least have a couple of broms in the enclosure. Not required in all types, but recommended.

There is now a link posted to a supplier in the UK. And there is a forum in the UK about pdfs, though I do not know the name. It could be very helpful for finding suppliers.

The main advice is to do lots of research and planning so that both you and your frogs will be happy with the outcome of the viv.

I was not trying to run you off from here. I just thought the UK forum would be more helpful for suppliers, as I know nothing about suppliers there.

Water features definitely help keep humidity higher. I have two in my 40 gallon tank. Dart frogs can drown, they are not the best swimmers. That is why it is important to provide easy access out of water features or ponds. This can be done by using gravel, etc. to give a slope so that they are not trying to climb straight up and out.

It is impossible to help with lighting without knowing what size enclosure you will be using. Metal halide is usually only used with tall enclosures. And 6500k would be better for the plants then 20000k.

Everything you mentioned in your plans are very doable. There is not a problem with using a mister and a water feature. I only hand mist mine, usually every other day, and humidity stays plenty high. Always over 80% at ground level.
