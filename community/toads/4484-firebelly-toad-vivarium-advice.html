I'm just stopping in briefly, so I don't have alot of time for a lengthy post, but a great plant for the setup you are considering is pothos. It has large sturdy leaves that will support the toads weight, and it can grow in almost any conditions, including emersed and even submerged.

It's going to be difficult to do a setup the way I think you want without having the water turn somewhat brown. The brown color is tannic acid leaching from the soil into the water, and the only way to keep it 100% clear is to divide the pond and land entirely. The other way to keep it mostly clear is to have the substrate high enough over the level of the pond that it can't leech into the water as much. Personally, I like the tannic water, I think it looks more natural, and it doesn't bother the toads at all.

hmmm, I guess one way you could keep it shallow but keep the substrate from sitting in the water is to use flat stones to raise the substrate up, instead of gravel, which will wick up more water.

Well, Deadriel, we've already established that Toes is a male because he tries to breed with Twinkle. Here is how you can tell male from female just by looking at them:

Male
-Wide shoulders compared to hips.
-Large forearm compared to upper arm. The skin of their arms is rough.
-Short, stubby fingers.

Female
-Narrow shoulders compared to wide hips.
-Long, thin forearm and upper arm. The skin of their arms is smooth.
-Long, narrow fingers.
-Their legs tend to be thin.

Both of your toads have all of the characteristics of a male. Also, any time you see a toad calling or barking, it will be a male. The females have a different sound than the males, but that would be impossible to explain...

Deadriel, you do not want to get any plants that are delicate. Don't get anything that's easy to break or has small flowers on it. I learned this the hard way... The toads like to jump all over everything, and when they jump and climb all over the plants, they'll tear leaves and flowers off. And if the plant moves or has something moving on it, then they will bite it and will not let go until they rip something off.

If you must have delicate plants, put them where the toads can't reach them. I have one creeping fig on a mud wall that I built that the toads can't get to and another creeping fig that they can reach... The one they can reach doesn't look very good... I also have a baby fern that the toads have ruined, I'm hoping that it will come back.

Two good plants to use would be green pothos and philodendron, they're big and leafy, so the toads can't tear them up. Also, the toads seem to like sitting on and under them. You should probably put plants in the deepest part of the water for the toads to hang on to. Mine like to wrap their arms around the leaves or stalks and float there lazily all day. Also it gives the females something to safely hang onto when they're being grabbed by the males. The males are usually strong enough to get away from the other males though.
