i know its too late but let me give some advice on drilling glass. If you were to do it like you originally intended i would have a shop do the holes in the lid. If you buy the glass from them then let them drill it then if they crack it its on them. I've drilled holes in tanks for drains and misting nozzles myself, it's pretty easy and i've never cracked one yet (knock on wood) but there is always the chance. My glass shop drills just about any size up to a 1" hole for about $5 a hole but of course they didn't have a 5/8" bit i needed for my misting nozzles, i was going to have them do it since i needed 3 holes and if they cracked it then they would redo it, but now i have to do it myself.

As far as bigger holes, glass-holes.com sells bits pretty fair priced, but I'd be nervouse myself drilling a big tank especially the bottom since tanks over 40 gallon tend to use tempered glass for the bottom pane, and well drill bit + tempered glass = instant break.

If you ever in the future decide to drill glass look on youtube there are plenty of videos. My key advice is Medium speed, low torque, and take your time. let the weight of the drill do the work, DO NOT push on the drill at all. Its a slow process. Keep the bit cool and hope for the best =). if the glass is going to crack its goign to do it at the end when you are breaking through. try to keep the bit level so it doesn't catch on the glass when breaking through, thats what will crack it. If you know you are close to breaking through don't stop the drill. keep going till you get through it. And always drill from the inside out if possible because you'll most likely get some chip out when you break through and you want that to be on the outside of the tank
