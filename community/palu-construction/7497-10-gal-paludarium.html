10 gal paludarium
OK, so I am new here. I am an avid fish keeper and reptile keeper as is my fiancee. I had an extra 10 gallon tank sitting around and decided to make a paludarium. When I posted it on one of the aquarium sites, one of the members instructed me to go here and check this site out. So I did. Now I want to keep frogs as well. Not in this 10 gallon of course, but in a bigger enclosure. (I have a 55 gallon tank sitting in the garage begging to be used.) Anyway, here is my attempt at a paludarium. At the moment, I am not planning on any animals in it save for some fish or shrimp. What I really want to accomplish with it is this: I would like to try my hand at making a fake background, making a false bottom, mixing a soil, and keeping plants alive. I don't really have a plant list yet. They will probably be a mix of terrestrial and aquatic plants. I really would like this to be a show piece, but it is, first and foremost, an experiment. Enough of my waffle, here it is.



Equipment is this:

Tetra internal filter
Incandescent hood with 1 full spectrum 10 watt mini CFL, soon to be 2
I have a piece of drift wood with some small pieces of Java Fern on it to see how it does.

The flower pots will not stay in there, and I am going to be dividing the tank to add a terrestrial part along with a DIY background.

Here is somewhat of a plant list:

Lobelia cardinalis
Bivittatus "ruby"
Creeping Fig
Moon Valley Pilea
Polystichum tsus-simense

Let me know if these will work, and if I should add any or omit any. I am thinking I am going to need more plants, but not sure yet. I want to see what I can do with this before I jump into any frogs.
I would love some plant suggestions. Even if it is 100 different species, I want to know what you would suggest so I can do research on them. Thanks all.

P.S. - For future reference, I want to keep Dendrobates azureus and adelphobates galactonotus Red.

P.P.S - I am also planning on Christmas Moss, Java Moss, and Peacock Moss for this tank.

Hey there!

First thing, if you haven't already also check out Dendroboard.com they have all the frog info you will need as well as a LOT of building info.


just a few bits of advice i can give you from having built many failed tanks and some successful ones now.....you will always try to do to much at once. its just a rule esp when starting out....things take up a lot more space than it seems so my biggest suggestion to you is to start SIMPLE!!

make a tank with NO water features at all and see if you can keep the plants alive (likely you will overwater/flood it). also, it depends on what kind of plants change syour soil needs etc etc


then try to add maybe a stream and move on....


last note along with the stuff takes too much room, i have personally tried and failed at least on 6 occasions to make a 10-gallon tank with a NOTICEABLE feature. you can squeeze in a stream or something but it becomes mostly a trickle and gets overgrown pretty fast anyway, the tanks are just too small.

just my 2cents gl

It may not be what you originally envisioned, but if nothing else it looks like it is becoming a learning experience for you. LOL Nice thing with a tank this simple is that if you start to change your mind about what you want to do with it (and it sounds like you have) then changes are easy to make.

Is that an actual sponge you are using? If so I would change that. Sponges tend to become breeding grounds for all sorts of bacteria and fungi -- often ones that are detrimental. Instead, especially since it looks to be particially submerged, I would suggest using either some ecoweb or even a piece of rot resistant wood to attach the java moss to.

Lobelia cardinalis would be a "No" as far as advisability of use. It Is beautiful but you are talking about a plant that grows to be over 3ft tall. Not something that will work well in a 10gal tank.


Polystichum tsus-simense would probably not be a wise choice either. While it is one of the smaller growing ferns, it still grows to be about 12in tall and wide which in a small tank like a 10gal means it is going to become very cramped and will muscle out other plants in the terr. And ferns, IME, tend to become 'thugs' in a terr -- taking over all available space -- as it is. With this fern being a fast grower that would likely be an even bigger problem.

Because I'm lazy, I'll just do a link -- there are some smaller growing plants here amongst the bunch: https://www.vivariumforum.com/communi...hp?do=ad&id=90

Hope I didn't 'pop' too many dream bubbles.
