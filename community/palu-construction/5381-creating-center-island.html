Creating a Center Island
Hello and welcome everyone. Since this is my first post (I'll do an introduction later if there is a section) I figure I would start it off with a good one...

How in the world can you make a Water Proof Center Island for a Paludarium? I was considering getting some Lexan, a Hand Held Torch (admit it, these things are fun no matter the application) and doing some creative bending and then sealing to the bottom of the Aquarium.

I wish a "land portion" center in the tank which can enable me to do some planting of plants along with the usual Air Plants.

Has anyone done this? I there a good construction journal of how to do this land section or I am going to have it wing it, and create one of my own. I have the idea, just hoping someone else has done it and can give me a few good pointers.

Just be aware, silicone and plexi/lexan don't really mix well. Silicone just doesn't stick to either very well. You might do better to have some pieces of glass cut to your measurements and use that, as it's less likely to eventually leak.

Have you considered a false bottom? Or is that going to take up too much vertical room for your tank?

I've done a tank like this before- it was basically just an egg crate platform with PVC piping below for support (one of which also held a fogger- nice in principle, but that part of it didn't really work out), a load of wood draped over the edges sticking into the water, and a whole lot of angled plastic pots held together with GS foam which were filled with substrate and plants. I don't have photos of it, unfortunately, so hopefully my description makes sense. It was easy as pie, and the plus side was that I could take the whole thing out of the tank when it was necessary.

have you considered BIG WOOD?

a nice stump or uprooted small tree or bush would be a great way to do it and accentuate the palu at the same time. if you're looking to add species that would like the higher ph or harder water you could seal the wood to keep it from leaching. a few epiphytes and some mosses to cover the less sightly areas or wherever it's necessary to cut your branches and you're good to go. it would also not take away as much from total water volume lending you a cleaner environment with less maintenance. also the space under it could be utilized for hides for less boiterous/photogenic species. you could also take a page out of hydrophites book and add plants like bacopa monnieri to any branches that sit at the water level to create a floating, creeping ground cover of sorts.

lots of options really, get creative, think nature... like a river bank or seemingly floating island in the center of a shallow pond.

rocks piled up in the center would also look more natural too.

if you find you don't have enough height with either option you could use an eggcrate false bottom to give it a little more height and just hide it with more decor.

sounds like an interesting project... do post pics of at least the finished project, if not a build log too.

good luck. wish i could be there to get my hands dirty on this one. sounds like it could get pretty creative.
