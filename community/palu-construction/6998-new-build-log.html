ditch the heat rock... they're dangerous... unless you're talking actual rocks that will have a heat source over them.

ditch the silver eggcrate and get black or white. afaik... nobody makes a silver that's not just painted. that leads to flakes and the paint itself is slightly toxic and not meant for consumption. frogs/amphibs will be consuming any water and it's contents when they're sitting in it. bottom line... that silver will flake off and when it does it is a threat.

for a dumpy, i might be inclined to do the tightbond as opposed to the clay. seems they're little fatties to me and they might indent the clay possibly breaking it off. grimm should have a better idea whether it would hold up or not.

tightbond should last almost indefinitely. tough stuff, like epoxy paint...

foggers can be diy'd to save money and have a more resilient end product. i would suggest doing it that way and planning for it in your building stages if you're going to.

misters... i love my mistking. i have a somewhat noisy house and it's in my bedroom where i never hear it (it doesn't go off at night while i sleep), so the med. pump is silent as far as i'm concerned. if your situation is different and you can't stand faint noises, you might want the econo-system. it's more than you seem to need atm, anyway. love my zip-drip, but can't say it's a necessity for everyone. i wouldn't go without it now that i have one, though. premium, drip-less nozzles... are great... and about as fully adjustable as you'd want them to be practical. cutting tool... makes working with the tubing and connectors literally a snap. greatest little tool i have, almost. using it it almost seems useless, but let me tell you... i installed an r/o unit under a very tight cabinet for my parents this past week and boy do i wish i had it with me!

THE TIMER... OH, THE TIMER... this is the single most important thing in the whole system IMHO. this timer is great. i got 2 and one has issues, but Marty is a great guy and surely will handle any issues you may or may not have.

alternatively, there are other ways to make misting/rain/whatever... but at this point i see no reason to hassle with it. especially once you consider the alternatives, time, money... the whole shebang. one way to diy is to use a windshield wiper pump... not bad, but then you have to source all the other parts and diy the whole job yourself anyway. not much savings once you consider the time and where you'll source all your parts (gas, shipping prices, etc.). another way is to plumb in a garden sprayer and put a solenoid valve on a timer and keep the sprayer pumped up all the time. the problem with this is filling the reservoir more often, seals blowing from constant pressure, overfilling, etc.. it will still cost more in the longrun, imo. other ways are to use aquarium/hydroponic water pumps and different fittings. there's cheap, easy to install irrigation heads available at most hydro shops (online or local). there's hose style fittings that can be utilized with an inverted sprinkler in larger (really big, unless you have a drain line plumbed from your viv to your main drain set at your water level) setup with semi-large pumps. so-on and so-forth... but then you're paying for a large pump, maintaining it and still have to do all the work, locate all the parts, and design your system to fit your needs.... and none of the above options basically come with their own handy dandy manual/e-manual.

as for a recipe for tightbond... grimm did it... and in his thread (which i believe is sticky) recipes were discussed. just keep in mind it is a glue. take the amount of material you think you need, add 20%-40%, grind or tear it up, mix it with glue and water until you get a slurry you think will stick and form well for your purposes... unless you just want a recipe to make things easier.

i think you have a great start. unfortunately there are a couple things you did not know. that's o.k., though... we'll work through it.

take it easy

Hi Beck! Its been a while :P

And yup, becks pretty much got you covered.

Silicone/coco falls apart quick. You will no doubt have bald spots with a dumpy climbing around on it within a year. If you do the titebond method, try and watch my vids and read a few of my threads. There arent too many people that have done it other then myself. Once dried it will be hard, and may have some sharp points. Just take some sand paper and try and smooth things out. It also helps to run a spoon over the mixture while it is still wet to smooth it out beforehand.

Once you actually make the titebond mixture it wont take you long to figure out if you need more water, glue or peat. A very thin milky film on the top will signify you have enough glue. And a dry mixture that cant be painted needs more water. Once applied to the background, if water starts to run out of the mixture, use less water next time. Its not science, more like a sloppy mess of an experiment.

Clay is not the best option for a dumpy. The clay will form a biofilm after the first month or so to keep it protected, but a dumpy is fat and will most likely end up tracking clay residue all over the tank. Darts are small, so they can walk over the biofilm without picking up any of the clay below it. (biofilm is just a thin layer of bio matter that helps protect the clay. Microbials, algea, and moss make up this layer)
