homemade filter and water qual for palu
Well i just got back from a trip to San Diego, for a bit of work and touring the zoos and aquariums around there. In short an amazing time. So I thought i would show the filter I made. Well its a Pseudo-filter, Its not big enough to filter to much due to lack of filter media, however it will be a conventent place to drop some carbon, or other media to adjust the water. I usually have very good luck with minimal filters and getting the cycling balence "just right" for very little maintenance, Kinda my specialty actually. The only way the water in the tank WONT go to hell is if the following is kept in mind.

TO GET GOOD WATER IN A PALU:
1. the live stock is put in SLOWLY, to fast and it will crash right away. U have to be very patient to get everything working right.
2. Live plants aquatic plants are a must. They really help stabilize the water. Plus the look really great!
Also, the terrestrial plants are great if they have fine root structure which will hang into the water. These plants will a) require less watering, b) the roots help filter the water a bit.
3. Watch your alkalinity, If you dont know what that is look it up. Arguably its more important than pH as it tells u what your pH is GOING to do in the future. This is the only way to keep water parameters with a minimal amount of fluctuation
******4. ALSO, Realize that one thing is very true with this kind of Paludarium******
Your water probably wont be crystal clear, maybe ever. This is a function of watering the terrestrial soil. The soil will leech some tanins and other nutrients into the water. This is OK. Actually i prefer it. Ever been to the amazon? the water is very murky. We often assume that water in the aquarium is only good if it looks like a chlorinated swimming pool. I personally like it a bit murky or the water stained a bit. I feel it better mimics their natural water chemistry. But be aware of what fish your stocking and what they can tolerate.
5. Dont put fish in right away, water your terrestrial plants while cycling the water the water will be pretty weird looking right away so water the plants, change a good bit of the water and repeat a few times till the water is looking good. If your terrestrial plants are rooting (as said above) and getting water themselves u will have to water much less.


The Filter:
So the little filter/ media basket thing I built. It also functions well as a water catch for the "waterfall" It lays on the bottom of the land area with soil around it to cover sides and "filter".

Notice water goes into the gravel area then trickles into the inlet then into the media, right now i threw in a few bio-balls for whatever, but ill probably put some filter foam too.

Construction is pretty easy just cardboard "mold", crack some acrylic pieces to fit, place them in the mold (basically to hold its shape when its drying and silicon away. Heres a pic of a cardboard for the base, very hightech, tape, and cardboard.

Can darts swim? ; You will find that is a question usually answered with a definite no.

I think any time you mix them with deep water you are taking a chance, but I do know that they can float and move about in the water for short periods of time, but they definitely are not built for swimming. A duckling can drown if trapped in the water for a surprisingly short amount of time.

I would have many floating plants and wood escape routs and shallow sloped edges so the frog had access to terrafirma if he did manage to fall in.
