So I am fairly new to this as I have never done anything like this before. I am getting a 90 gallon tank that measures 3ft long x 2ft wide x 2ft tall. I want to make it so that the front half or a little bit less is water and the back is land. On the right side I want (about 6 inches) I want the water to go all the way to the back. Since I will be using a false bottom and the excess water will drain into the pond water, what is the best way to set this up so that I don't get soil from the land side to go into the water underneath it?

saw your post about the two elements....I'd been mulling over a similiar setup with fire bellied toads and Endlers live bearers in the water portion. Have done some background work into the logistics and decided the only/best way to keep the water clean, land from becoming saturated...was to have glass panels cut to form the separation of land/water. It would have to be glass to be siliconed to the glass tank as my extensive research... shows that silicone will bond for a long period of time to glass. Silicone does not make a good, long-term bond to glass using acrylic as the separation ingredient... Also found out that there are not too many frogs that would like the water element, but that the FBTs would..any of them, however, need some way to escape easily....I'm working out the details in my mind before I get the glass cut...but am sorta close to attempting it. Unlike you, I'm not going to do a false bottom, using a good base for the terrestrial plants with a tube running under the strata to suck out excessive water...the open end buried in some sort of material that would resist clogging. Keep us posted...
