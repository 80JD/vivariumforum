Newbi Built Indy tank - 90x12x30
Here goes...

I am new to the forum and to the hobby. I am currently building a paludarium tank that is sort of themed - Raiders of the Lost Ark!

I have uploaded some picture of the set up.

Currently, I have a canister filter, water heater, light timer, air bubbler, a sump tank with pump and a fog machine.

The fogger is triggered by a humidity sensor and vents out through the snake's mouth.

I have two planting areas that have been set up with drains prevent over-saturation.

The tank is viewable on both long sides and one narrow side. All scenic elements have been carved from foam, painted and covered in epoxy. I have water flowing from behind the statue, down a few steps and into a channel that meanders through the "dry" area.

Next I plan on adding jungle sounds that time with specific events in the tank - such as lights on, lights off, feeding, and watering. Ultimately, I will have a couple of dart frogs and I want to see if I can condition them to associate the sounds with the events (sound track b = food) etc...

Anyway - let me know what you guys think!

I made the stream and idol nook from styro foam sheets. For the stream, I did a rough shape, then hit it with a heat gun to make the channel. Once it I got the slope like I wanted, I filled in any gaps with wood filler. I then painted it with spray faux stone paint - the kind of stuff that make a textures and splatters different colors. Once that dryed, I coated the whole thing with epoxy/resin to seal it all and make it a little tougher.

The planted areas are plexiglass trays I made that drop into crescent shaped areas around the stream. Since I have just planted it, it looks very precise in shape right now. I am hoping once stuff starts to really grow in, it will take on a bit more organic look.

Attached is a picture of a test fit i did with the foam for the idol. I made it so the water comes down behind the gold idol and the fog come from the mouth of the snake. I carved little relief images across the header based on some mayan glyphs.
