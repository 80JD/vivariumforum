I believe GE II silicone is the one that has the mold/mildew inhibitor? I know that is toxic to fish, I am not sure what it would do in the water for these toads but I have always used GE I 100% silicone for all my fish tanks and stuff i made for in them.

Jlennon, I think you're on the right track with this build- keep us posted. You might consider building outside of the tank onto a sheet of rigid material that you can just slide into the tank when it's ready- you'll have to keep the toads out of the tank while you wait for any adhesive you use at that last step to dry, but it's probably the best way to do a good job with minimal disturbance to the inhabitants. You shouldn't need any waterproofing at all- once the foam is cured it will be able to take the water, and silicone is waterproof in and of itself- that's what it's for.

for bricks i might be inclined to use foam boards/panels instead. i'd also probably use concrete/grout color to get the red of the brick and maybe some grey/brown/green to give the look of age/wear. to seal that i might use some west systems epoxy paint/clear pond coat/or some acrylic paint and sprinkle some sand in the last coat to avoid a shiny appearance.

one thing you want to keep an eye on is how it fits. unless you take the trim off the top of the tank or split your b/g into 3 pieces for installing it might not be possible to get it in the tank once it's finished... especially if your dimensions are perfect. one good thing is a brick pattern is perfect for hiding cuts into the b/g for application purposes... just cut along the seams.

for your cocofiber do a search for tightbond. that should get you on your way to a method that will stand the test of time much better than silicone.

your silicone should be fine. if it's the kitchens and baths type you might want to give it a couple weeks to cure and leach the inhibitors just in case. ge silicone comes in several colors... they're not always available when you're looking for them but they'll be there eventually.

check out the sticky on moss growing in the terra-plants section. it might give you some insight on what happened to your moss. also, it's very likely you can bring it back to life.

Before you spend money on styrofoam, check with local drug stores and maybe a wal-mart. Styrofoam is used as packing material for many products and when we unbox them we just throw it out. That is how I have gotten all of my styrofoam. Unsanded grout works beautifully at creating a rock like look to styrofoam. YOu can get it in just about any color you want and a layer of clear silicone on this would seal it nicely. I used Liquid Nails to affix the eggcrate to the back of the 20G I am building, it was stuck and permanent within 30 seconds of putting it in there.

Also I would just pick up another tank. Sometimes construction doesn't always go the way you want it and you don't want to leave your toads in a temp tank tooooooo long without being 100% sure your tank will be done soon. My 20 should have taken 3-4 days as it's a simple build but has turned into 2 weeks so far. Also then when you are complete you then have a spare tank to do things like: quarantine tank for sick animals, another terrarium/paludarium, house for another pet, bug raising for food..............tons of stuff.

... I personally would never put a layer of clear silicone on anything to seal it... It doesn't stay clear for very long and will quickly ruin the look of what you've made. it's get cloudy and somewhat blue as it ages (which it does more quickly under water).

If you have the space for it, I recommend you go buy a 20G long tank. They are a really great size for FBTs, and the footprint allows for alot of land AND water. If you do a "flaoting" landmass in a 20G long, think of how much MORE space they'll have! A 20G long is usually about $20, so that's not terribly expensive. Otherwise, I recommend getting a sterilite tub to house them in temporarily. They are easy to clean and sterilize, so they make good QT tanks for any future pets (or your current ones if one gets sick).
