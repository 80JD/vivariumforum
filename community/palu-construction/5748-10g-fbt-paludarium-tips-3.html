You'll do well adding some fresh wood to your water area as well to help keep the pH where it should be when you add new water (the tannic acid lowers the pH). I say fresh because it will lower it more quickly as the tannins begin to leach. IME, the toads prefer slightly soft water, so if the tannins lower the pH more than enough to get it to 7, it should be just fine.

I like your BG, that's going to look very nice when it goes in. I hope you don't carve it down too much! It has a very organic, tree fungus type feel to it. Can't wait to see what you do with it.

I have used Krylon fusion paints. They are the ones for plastics. They work wonderfully. If you look here: https://www.vivariumforum.com/communi...te-topper.html, you can see in the last pics what my BG looks like that I used the paint on.

It's very important when painting that you remove the outer skin on the foam, otherwise the paint won't stick well and will easily rub off. The best way to do this is with drywall sandpaper. It's like mesh with sand glued to it. I usually cut it into hand sized pieces and gently rub it over the foam to remove the skin and smooth any areas that I have carved. The foam that is directly under the skin has far smaller bubbles, so it makes the perfect surface for the paint. Buy more than you think you'll need, remember that the foam is essentially a sponge once the skin is gone. I recommend getting a couple of complimenting colors to make it look more realistic. Also, practice the spray pattern on something first to get the hang of it and see what sorts of textures and patterns you can make with the two colors. Buy a small sponge brush and a small bristle brush. You can dampen the sponge brush a little (squeeze out the excess water) to make a wash effect where you want the paint a little thinner. The bristle brush you'll use dry to add texture and blend colors. You can look up "dry brushing techniques" for more info. A rag or a small natural sponge would be good to use to add texture as well. (If you want a little paint to dip the sponge or rag into, you can spray a heavy layer onto a foam plate or something that won't absorb the paint.)

Just be sure when you buy your paints that you don't get tempted into buying anything that is not for plastics (I can't recommend anything other than the Krylon Fusion paints), other than some of the stone-look paints (they are ok because they don't have the solvents that regular spray paints have, that's to keep the bits of different colors that make it look like stone from dissolving). Stay away from anything that's not a matte finish, unless you are going to use a matte finish clearcoat. (Honestly, and clear sealer should be fine over the paint and not melt your foam, but do a test piece first, one on bare foam and one on painted foam, just to be sure). If you can't find a matte finish clearcoat or don't like the colors that come in matte, you can sprinkle a little fine sand on the last coat of paint to reduce the gloss. I don't recommend using white sand. If you do that, after the paint dries, use your bristle brush to scrub some of the excess sand away so that the BG isn't too rough.

Anyway, let me know if you have any more questions.
