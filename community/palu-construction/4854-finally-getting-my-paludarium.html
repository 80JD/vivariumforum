on the clams- make sure to test the river water where you gather them from. You said that the river is brackish, so then you would of course want to mimic that water as close as possible. In my experience, clams should be fed twice daily when housed in a tank. You can pick up a liquid feed at the aquarium section of petstores, or you can pulverize fish meal/fish food/other fishy foods and use an eye dropper to handfeed them whenever you see their filter tubes poking out of the sand.

Sand should be used instead of aquarium gravel as it is safer for them- gravel would put more pressure on their shells than sand would if a rock or other object landed in the area. Also makes it easier for them to move house.

Oh- and count how many clams you put in your tank- then count them at feeding time. Usually it is easy to find them - and even more easy to find them if you use sand.

You should also check the state's listings to make sure it is ok for these clams to be kept- for example, I believe that VA had a certain species of clam that was illegal to move from its habitat. However we also have an introduced clam species that we can take as much as we want out of the river. Not sure which one is which, but you should always check your state's listings to ensure that you arn't weakening the genetic code availability of the species.

I would also keep the clams separate from the main tank for a month or so as you have mentioned. It helps make sure all are feeding well. You'll find much satisfaction when you see them growing

beck- that is a method used for growing daphnia and other tiny critters. The clams would love the daphnia if they can suck them up, and small fish love them too. I would be careful of feeding the cultured green water though... daphnia thrive on it, but sometimes it can spark a green water bloom in your own tank. I've never had a problem with it though, but the daphnia water is just a murky light brown/green - I don't think that it is the algae green water. The water is from a pond with food added to it. There are many recipees for culturing daphnia, vinegar eels, and other such tiny fauna on the web. Usually these are cultured for raising fish fry though.
