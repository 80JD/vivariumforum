par is par, so if your 10000k bulbs have good par they should be fine... and should penetrate well. i'm sure you're aware that lower light plants will like more reds, higher light more blues, etc. but good placement should help tremendously with filtering the light appropriately.

good fogger... homemade out of an ultrasonic humidifier. check out the stickies and my how it's done thread. to find that you'll do an advanced search... search titles only and put in the search bar... favorite links.

heating can come from one source or a combination. heat lamps, under tank heaters, ceramic heat emiters, flexwatt, heat rope and aquarium heaters can all be utilized. any of them can have a dimmer installed and some options can utilize a controller (i wouldn't use one with a light since it'll turn it off and on randomly).

most amphibians don't need uvb and uva would be provided sufficiently through just about any fluorescent or full spectrum bulb. i suspect your 10000k should do just fine there.

faux rock can certainly be made into ledges.

substrate will be relative to the setup, plants, animals and the keeper. there's lots of options you just want to stay organic, not too high in ferts (any organic growing soil should be mixed with peat, cocofober, sand, etc. to cut down on the ferts for example).

carnivorous plants i would think are definitely a possibility. however the plants and animals should be compatible. you wouldn't want tiny babies in with a very large pitcher for example. keep in mind i have no experience mixing cp's and critters.

yes, a cycling period will aid in many things. it can help to detoxify any construction materials, let the plants settle and give the soil time to "cycle" almost exactly like a fish tank would. it can also buy you time to make any adjustments with temp, humidity, etc..

humidity should be relative to the animal in question. typically pretty high for amphibians. plants chosen after ward would be according to what should do well in there and not harm the animal.

to keep humidity in will require a glass top... pretty much fully covered. misting and/or fogging should be sufficient to acquire your humidity levels. at that point a fan installed... or several... will really help to keep your front glass clear. you'll want them directed at the glass and only recirculating air from within the tank... not introducing new air or exhausting your humid air. placed correctly with enough flow they can really help to pick up the water on the surfaces and in the soil and help raise the overall humidity in the enclosure too as well as provide air flow that many plants depend on and help combat nuisance molds that can be a problem in stagnant setups.

most controllers (reasonably priced) are simply on and off although you can pay the extra for something variable.

i'm skeptical about those for constant submersion but if they don't short out i don't see why one can't be used.

depending on what species you settle on i might be careful about bark chips. anything an animal can consume but can't pass can cause impaction which in turn can lead to death.

many of us usually use old computer fans but if you can find ones you can hang that are herp safe go for it. wouldn't want their little legs getting caught in the fan blades, etc.. usually any fans are in a hood, venting system, behind some mesh, etc. ... things that people put together when the necessity arises.

there are soooo many animals that are suited to a vivarium, i wouldn't know where to start. if you like cresties though you might like gargoyles.

definitely roam around the site for a bit and check out the link i gave you. if you dig around deep enough you'll be baffled at what you can find. there are more animals on this site alone than i care to remember... and you'll not only find the answers to most of your questions but most likely how to put them into action too. don't be afraid to use the search function either... it can be very useful in your endeavors.
