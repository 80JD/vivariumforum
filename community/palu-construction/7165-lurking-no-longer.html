Hello everybody,
I am new to this so please try to be gentle if I have made some monumental error in my designs. I haven't hurt anybody yet, but the end result is to hurt lots of somebodys but in a natural kind of way.
This all started with a gift from my mother.

My mother made the mistake of leaving a valuable family heirloom at my house for too long. I used to store my collection of stubby beer bottles in it but I was feeling inspired and decided that it would be better to store plants in it.
Here is the valuable particle board china cabinet.

This drawing has been met with mixed reviews. Some people find that it helps to explain what I am doing, some folks just get more confused.
The green is water flow and the orange is a glass fish tank. The three drawings on the right are the individual shelves from a top view persective. They show the stream in the top tray and the lake in the middle. The very bottom of the cabinet will house a tank to hold extra water for the pump and to catch whatever falls out the bottom.

All of the trays will have an inch or so of rock, with a layer of spaghnum peat above that and then a layer of top soil. If I get the water level just right, I should be able to water all the plants from the underground water flow without drowning or drying anything to much. This pic is the mostly complete mechanical end of things.

Lets take a trip around and see where the water is supposed to go.
This picture is both the start and the finish. Through the drainhole a 1/2 hose is going to carry water up to the very top tray. Eventually, when the water flows up into the middle tray it will waterfall here back into the sump below.

The hose will go to a nipple on the underside of the top trayand it will blast away away at a maximum of 250 GPH iside of this box:

In order to get a pump that could lift water as high as I needed to, I had to buy a huge fountain pump. I was concerned about the raging torrent factor so I built this box that I can fill with rocks or whatever to break up the flow of water and to direct it to where I want it to go. The box will be buried in dirt with flora growing on it and the water will flow out of the side of the mountain and across this tray. The train will be static and will be coming out of a tunnel at the back of the 'mountain'. and in the foreground I will use a model building or two to hide the drain.

The depth of the water in this tray will be controlled by adding a piece of pipe over the drain hole as a weir. If I want it deeper, I will just silicone a taller piece of pipe in place. That drain hole goes to a piece of 3" pipe which will allow the water to flow past the middle level and straight to the bottom of the fish tank. At the bottom of the tank I have some screen and I will cover that in a few inches of lava rock.

The water will flow up through the porous rock and overflow into the tray above it.

This tray and the tank will be siliconed together.

The screening will keep the dirt and rock from falling through and the screen will have a hole cut into it over one of the square holes to create a 'lake' and to give me access to feed the fish.

And then tier 2 eventually fills up enough to spill out over the waterfall.

and then we are back at the start. In the waterfall area I will have large stones, a fern or two. I think I am going to use some ancient cedar shakes to line the walls of this thing to give it a more rustic look than I am getting with the green paint.

I can see already that lighting is going to be a major problem. Lighting the top tier will be easy with any aquarium light. I just need to cut a hole. The middle tier and the tank are another story. My original plan had a storage area where the waterfall is now and I was going to light the fish tank from the side. The plants were to get small spotlights hidden behind the wood of the doors. I may still be able to do that but I won't know until I see how bad the humidity gets with the waterfall in there. But that fish tank is going to be really dark.

That's all I have for now and I suspect it won't look a lot different for a while. Before I get planting, I have a lot more building and testing and probably rebuilding and more testing. I am already concerned with the lack of a way to clean the underside of the screen on tier 2.

Now that the preamble is done, I am at the reason for being in a palu- forum.
My intentions for life in this cabinet are roughly as follows:
On tier#3 I will use overhead lighting to grow carnivorous plants and will have no access points for the animals. I suppose there are ways a determined critter could climb up and if it becomes a problem i will deal with it then, but it is to be considered 'movement free'.
On tier#2 and in the waterall area: unknown vegetation and possibly newt(s)/ salamander(s). I am hoping to get a land based creature or two that will swim and eat the fish in the tank below them. The 'pond' edges will be lined with moddelling clay and maybe some small round stones to protect their soft bellies from the edges of the aluminum screening. This will be under an inch or so of water and sloping up to the level of the dry ground so they should have easy ingress/egress.

fishtank: This is all mystery to me. I am not sure what fish would breed fast enough and move slow enough to feed that monster from above or if it will even come down to hunt. nor do I know what plants would be good or anything else. Access to the fishtank will be limited to whatever will fit into the lake opening so plants that require pruning probably won't be suitable.

DISCLAIMER: I fully intend to run this cabinet for a long while with just plants in it. When I get all the mechanical details nailed down and plants can survive both of my left thumbs, then I will consider adding animals. I am just posting about the animals now, in case there is something I need to consider in this construction phase that I am missing.
