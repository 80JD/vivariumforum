ge 1 or 2 should be fine. i hear windows and doors is the kind to use but i didn't even check that myself. as for the mold inhibitors they rarely leach to anything insurmountable volume wise when they're underwater imo. giving it some time to "cycle" once you're finished construction should be more than enough to keep harmful chemicals at bay with either ge silicone. biggest thing to check is to make sure it's 100% silicone!

your problem with sticking doesn't seem to be normal. also you say little pieces are breaking off. i'm thinking immediately the problem is debris. do you have an air compressor? if not get a decent vacuum, q-tips and a dry/damp cloth. use the cloth/q-tips to wipe away the debris as best as possible getting the nooks and crannies pretty good. afterward do a good vacuum on your pieces to get them debris free. that or the air compressor should help tremendously. you could also get compressed air in a can (computer air duster) if you don't have a compressor but use it sparingly... it runs out quick.

you could use rocks too but i fear getting a water tight seal would be a pain in the arse with that too. of course it's up to you if that's what you want to do. it would be interesting and a somewhat new approach.

one thing i'm thinking is why'd you cut up the g/s? i would think that sealing that back together would be much more difficult than just spraying a line down and covering it... you could silicone over it and add rocks to that too.

did you use the big gap filler?

i would get another can and start over. this time lay it thinner. do a practice run on a piece of cardboard/paper/wax paper to get a feel for how it expands.

i would think the inside would cure better if it were exposed.

you can also put on some disposable rubber gloves and form it with your hands once it's skinned over... before it's done curing. that should help if you happen to spray a little too much.

don't worry about using different materials i would think. i'm thinking you'd have to start learning all over again. if the next material fails it'd be discouraging and that's not the point of the hobby. we want it to be fun. granted there's hurdles but enjoyment and pets are the purposes.

if you can blow out the loose stuff from the inside and uncover something a little more sturdy try it... if it seems resistance is futile don't fight it.
