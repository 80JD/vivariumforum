Paladarium super build
I've been wanting to design and build a paldarium for quite some time. I've been lurking for months reading and gathering as much info as possible. I have finally entered the design phase and have a couple questions. Originally, I planned on using my 125 gal tank. I don't like the idea of hoses and wires coming over the top so I thought I would install bulkheads, but the glass is tempered and might not survive the drilling process. So I thought I would custom build an acrylic tank. My new tank dimensions will be 60"x24"x36". I plan on having a 10-12" water feature in the front leaving 20-24" of height (after substrate) for plants to grow. My first question is how important will a vent be in this size tank? I have a ceiling fan in this room that runs frequently to circulate air. Would I need something that stretches across the entire top of the tank or could I get away with a couple holes on the side and maybe use computer fans to blow fresh air in occasionally? Does the position of the vent matter (over land, over water)?

First of all...DO NOT try and drill your tempered glass, it will shatter just like a windshield into a million tiny pieces. Have you considered making a background using your 125 gal tank? You could run all your piping, water lines, and mechanical equipment behind it. Then all you would need is a drilled acrylic top to allow the lines to pass through hidden.

Kinda like this one.... http://www.aquaticquotient.com/galle...m_fullview.jpg
Im sure circulation is more important then venting. My tank has suffered without any. Most people tend to point the fans toward the front glass. It keeps the condensation to a minimum and visibility up

Chillplants, I would say that whatever tank you end up doing, plan for a vent that you can cover completely or partially- better to work it into the design plan than have to cram it in later.

Replacing the bottom glass on that 125 would be a piece of cake- have your guy cut and drill the glass to your specs and install it yourself. Failing that, you might be able to get away with drilling holes low on the back, but I don't know whether the sides would be tempered as well.

A custom acrylic tank in those dimensions would cost a fortune, and you'd have to engineer it to withstand warping due to heat and humidity- important considerations. You might be better off working with epoxy-sealed plywood and facing with glass at that point- you'll certainly save some money.

Welcome to the forum- looking forward to seeing what comes of this plan.

what animals/type of environment? tropical? where are you located... is it tropical there too? where will it be located when it's done? if it's in a house what's the environment?

the reason i ask is if you want a tropical palu at 80*F and ~80%RH in a living room with 70*F and 65%RH venting it is going to want to bring it to ~47%RH at 80*F.

before you cut holes for anything make sure you know what you're using and cut the hole according to that... obviously. bulkheads will need a certain size, vent holes will have to be according to the vent covers and circulation holes will have to be according to your hoses, contraption, etc..

i believe eddy planer lives in the tropics so venting can't hurt him too much...

look here for "is my glass tempered?" and THEN how to drill the tank... how it's done... favorite links thread ... if it's tempered, it can't be drilled of course...

of course you could use a drilled glass top also, going off what grimm posted.

aaron makes fine points as always...
