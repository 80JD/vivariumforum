A 10g Build!
Hello everyone,

My name is Ryan, and I am new to this forum. I am in the process of building a paludarium out of a spare 10g I had kicking around.

This is my first experience with this type of system. I have many many years of experience keeping marine tanks, specifically reef biotopes. Everything from nanos or large volume systems, so I am quite used to being methodical and precise with my hobbies.

I decided to go the plaudarium route because it was something totally new to me. I've spent the last 4 months reading all over the web about set-ups etc, and finally decided to take the plunge. Critiscism both good and bad is appreciated as its a good thing to learn, and I enjoy learning new things.

I have not decided what types of plants I would like to house, but I do know I would like to attempt some moss eventually. As far as livestock goes, my daughter is in love with frogs so once the system is established I would like to try an easy frog.

The tank will have a false bottom constructed from egg crate, and will try and incorporate some type of water feature into the design, I will be using "Great Stuff", sealed with silicone to add dimension to the background and to construct the water feature. I will be using coconut fiber, and husk to make the background.

Without further ado this is my build so far. (I started today)

Here is todays update!

I finished most of the skeleton for the water feature, as well as applied the rocks to the edgeing of the water area.

I did this by applying a thin layer of aquarium safe silicone to the rectangular pieces I spray-foamed last night. I then poured some leftover aquarium gravel from my last FW setup into the silicone and gently pressed it in. I left a pile on top as weight to keep some pressure on the gravel. The final result is what you see. I think it turned out pretty good, I was quite pleased with the result I got. Once the silicone setup up a little, I turned the pieces over and applied silicone to the back to comepletly seal in the foam so it does not break down
