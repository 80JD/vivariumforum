I drilled my Exo Terra and my New 29 gallon recently. I watched this video on youtube,

YouTube - Drilling an Aquarium
(Ignoring the odd sound effects and stuff while it played)

Ordered the bit from Glass-holes.com, got it 2 days later. I practiced on a 10 gallon and had no issues. I jumped right to my 29 and then to my Exo-Terra and both came out fine.

I did use a double thick layer of Duct Tape on the inside glass to hold the glass in place during punch through. If you don't have something holding against the glass when the drill is about to punch through, the weight of the drill alone will probably push it through and make chips. With the tape, the bit can finish cutting before the weight of the drill forces the glass to chip. Also as someone else mentioned it prevents the glass chip from falling to the other side of the tank and cracking it.

Just remember don't push down, let the weight of the drill do the work. Also keep the drill speeds low, and if the drill has a high and low gear set it to low so you don't have to worry about the bit spinning too fast.

Also, if you are not using a drill press, start off at an angle and with the bit spinning relatively fast, just to create a notch in the glass, and then slowly tilt the drill upright, keeping it in the notch as you do so. Remember to cut back on the speed after you make the initial notch as well.

It really surprised me how easy it was. I bought the bit with intentions of drilling just the new tank I am working on, but I wound up drilling my older Paludarium (exo-terra) to install a canister filter and now I am itching to drill my 65 gallon brackish tank .
