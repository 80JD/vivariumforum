After waiting 24 hours for the silicone to dry I again vacuumed out the tank to get the losse dirt that had fallen and I also vacuumed the background on final time.
Then it was time for the exciting stuff, time to start planting!

The bad news was that I let my plants sit in a windowsill for about a week while setting up the tank, and even though I had been watering them, the moneywart plant starting wilting. That was a real shame I actually liked it the best and it was the last one they had at Lowes. Did what I could with it and got my Mondo Grass and Japanese Spurge planted as well.
All of this was done after I finished my closing shift at work (I manage at an Applebees) around 4AM on Saturday.
For Substrate I used eco earth (the kind that comes in a brick) and a generic version labeled forest substrate. Got both of these at Petsmart. Into these I mixed some Flourite planted fish tank substrate. This makes a great pet safe fertilizer.
The next night I set up the substrate for the aquatic portion of the tank, first I put down a layer of the rest of my flourite, then I had a little bit of like 5 different fish tank gravels that I mixed together and put that down, and finially I topped it with a layer of larger fish tank gravel that had the color scheme I was looking for. I believe it was labeled course aquarium gravel or something like that. I only bought a small bag of it since it was just a topper and I didn't want any aquatic plants struggling to much to fight through the gravel.

After I filled the tank with water, I ran into a HUGE problem. I hooked up my pump to the tubing for my water feature and plugged it in, and nothing came out of the waterfall, not one drop. I had tested the pump a few days before, so the problem had to be something else.
Then I realized what happened, the waterfall tube got plugged up with great stuff! You see, I learned from a past mistake a few months ago that if you run the tubing to exit of the waterfall, the water will project itself father than you anticipate, often with great velocity based on the waterpump you are using. So for all my projects I build a ltlle chamber out of foam or a plastic bowl of something to allow the water to pool up and pour out to help control the flow of the water. Then I learned that if you only use silicone to attach the tubing inside the water chamber, as soon as you remove the pump you will probably pull the tube completely out of the water chamber and ruin your whole project (at least that is what happened to me) SO I have been using a little great stuff inside the chambers and around the bottom of the chambers to hold provide more support and leak resistance for the tubing inside the water features.
In this instance the great stuff expaned much more that I thought it would (happens) and completely clogged my water feature from the inside! ARRGH!

So I did a little surgery, I cut off the top of the chamber which was thankfully at the top of the background and shoved a coat hanger through the great stuff to allow water to flow! Sweeet, now my waterfall works.

Then more problems arose as the clat dragons head started leaking around its seal and the just fell off about 5 minutes later. SO I was having tons of difficulty at this point and went to bed ( this was after closing at work again on Sunday)

So here is a photo of one of the lids I made for this tank. HAd 2 build 2 identical lids.
To build this I used some wood planks that measured 48" long, 1 in wide, and 1/4 in thick. I built a frame from these. Then I cut out a piece of plywood to fix the frame and serve as a lid, then I attached a hinge to the back so the whole lid opens.
Used a jigsaw to cut out holes for the light fixtures, which are marineland LED fixtures I got at petsmart. They were kind of expensive but they dont emit heat and they are supposed to last for 8-10 years.
Varnished and sealed the lids, then I used silicone to attach the light fixtures and seal any gaps around them. Drilled holes in the lid for ventilation. Pretty simple to build.
