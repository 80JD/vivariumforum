Last night I had my water tested at the pet store. They said it was "hard" but could be softened with aquarium salt. I went ahead and got salt, 3 guppies, and some bacteria adder. I woke up this morning with 3 dead fish. With out having the money to go out and get a test kit for myself at this point, all i can do is speculate. I speculate that the repti-sand I used for my background and land forms caused the water to become extremely hard. I am wondering if this is the case, if the sand is causing the problem, can it be counter balanced with chemicals? or will I have to go through a long and tedious process of removing the sand from the GS, and covering it with something else?

Any type of shell (calcium) in the sand will make the water very hard, you can add peat to the filter, this will help to make the water more acidic but adding one thing to counteract another will never be as successful as rectifying the cause.
Salt will not evaporate from the water, I defiantly do not think this is a good idea!
You should be able to source "lime free" sand quite easily from any aquatic store or "play sand" (as it is called in England) as used to fill children's play pits is very often lime free river sand.
In a "Amazonian biotope" aquarium for instance, when we are looking for soft acidic water, injecting C02 would be the answer.
