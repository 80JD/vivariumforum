So I just finished applying the classic silicone/substrate layer the background for this project, and I took a few pics of this process. Now you can see why I painted the entire background brown, that way I didn't have to perfect with my layer of silicone/substrate, in fact I wanted to intentionally not cover some areas here and there and I also didn't put any substrate below where the water line will be.

To complete this step I used GE#2 brown silicone and Jungle Bed substrate. This type of substrate is really textured and has a lot of wood or coconut chips in it, so I put some in a bowl and picked put all the these chips and long stringy fibers.
To apply the silicone one should always wear gloves, but also I used a plastic spoon to spread the silicone out so my gloves didn't get as messy.

While I was at it I also siliconed some rocks to the sides of the water feature for an added touch.

Now is when this whole process starts getting good!

I attached my faux brick walls inside the tank, which are used to hide the false bottom from view, then I attached my background inside the tank.

For any silicone work that would be visible I used black DAP silicone, and for anthing not visible I used GE 1 clear silicone.

As I stated earlier, my BG warped and it was actually worse than I anticipated, I had to slowly silicone down on side at a time of each piece of the background and wait for about an hour, then Silicone the other side.
Since I had 3 BG piecs it took seriously like 6 hours to get it to attach correctly.

After this process there were still some large gaps in my background. This was due to the great stuff shrinking after expanding as well as having to cut it to fit it inside thetank. But better to work outside the tank, as mistakes are more easily corrected and I caould be sloppy with my painting work.

Anyway attached are some pics of everything attached in the tank with the great stuff applied to the cracks in the background. For fun I put my 2 aquaraium ornaments where they belong on the background.

I am still thinking about what I want to live in here.
I think newts would work out very well in this environment, but they are a real pain to feed when they are smaller, and the local pet store hasn't had any in a while. Some paddle tail newts are more agressive eaters and might do well.
FBTs would also be good for this tank, but I have been unsuccessful in finding a lid for the tank and those toads are actually really good climbers.
Another thought would be a couple smaller reeve's turtles, but then I have to think about basking lights and really watching the temps more closely.
The weakest idea I had was just some comet gold fish, since I went with a water garden theme and they look like small Koi Pond fish. But wow, these are super easy to take care of.
Basically I am still weighing the pros and cons of my creature choice for this tank, and any way I choose to go I will still have to wait a few months while all my plants and mosses establish themselves.
