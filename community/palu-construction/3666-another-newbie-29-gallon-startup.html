hello brett1138, and welcome to the forum! your plan sounds good, im having a little trouble visualizing all of it, some diagrams will help. for palnts consider Ficus pumila or a small leaved ivy, something to vine across the ground and branches, my FBTs love to hide in it. if you want an orchid, find one that will grow in your conditions. i know there are some terrestial ones that can grow in very moist soil. you may have to order from a specialist though, but price generally depends on rarity. selaginella is another low grower that might suit you. acorus species are a good marginal plant (grows in wet soil). you may want to avoid any sort of soil in your beach area as it will rot very quickly. you will also need to ensure your substrate does not become water logged. unless you have the space for them you may want to hold off on getting the plants untill the tank is ready to be planted.

Ill answer your questions as best i can:

- if the tank is entirely enclosed (i.e. no screen top), you may want to install a pc fan to help with ventilation

-if you are going to bake it soaking wood in cool water is fine, baking causes the water to boil out of the wood. depending on how much tanin the wood contains, you may need to change the water daily. when the water stays clear for a few day, its time to bake the wood.

- the placement of the fogger depands on the type you get, what part of the country/world do you live in? the fogger and mist seems a bit of overkill for FBTs in a palu.

-you shouldnt mix any species unless you have alot of husbandry experience. also FBTs are mildly toxic, enough to kill most other herps over time. i have seen them mixed with FBNs before, but i have never kept newts, so idk. the basic rule of thumb with species mixing: if you have to ask, dont do it.

im looking forward to what you come up with! good luck!
