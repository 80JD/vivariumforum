cheap mister (rain system)
ok so i'm building my first viv, this is my first post as well so please forgive me, however, first viv, trying to make everything, trying to stay cheap and trying to have all the nice things without paying the big $$$ for them.

tonight, i had such an exciting success i thought i would share.
at home depot/lowes in the plumbing section there is black 1/4" microtubing used for drip systems. I used that tubing along with the mister heads that screw into them. These all run at about 7-10 psi and i think 3??gph.
I used those and also went to a great website, called ELECTRIC GOLD-MINE (google it).
Right now at that site, they are selling a FLOJET boat pump (60psi and i think 6gpm) for 30$, a hell of a deal!!
anyways, using some simple brass pipe fittings and couplers i was able to hook up three of those misting heads and it pumped just fine!!! actually better than fine, perfect! almost the same look as some of those other really expensive misters!!

I really didn't want to hand mist my viv and some of the air stone manipulations just looked too tacky for me, as well as the tubing with a few holes poked into it. This system works awesome!! the pump is self priming and it creates a nice fine mist like you see at the grocery store in the produce! i can't wait to install it and have it run! honestly, i think three misting heads might be TOO much! i might knock it down to 2, i dunno yet, now i just have to find a nice timer to run for minutes or seconds. Right now my shortest timer runs for 15 min...

anyways, this was a great alternative I created and wanted to share with others on here, I hope you enjoyed!! leave a comment to let me know i helped you out or you like this!!

I'm thinking about adding a filter for this setup as well. On the intake for the pump, run it into the lid of a pickle jar all the way to the bottom, stuff a bunch of pump filter into the jar and then run some more tubing back out the top of the lid at the top so that the water has to pass through all of the filter material (of course using silicone to keep it air/water tight)...just an idea, any better ones?

I'm sorry, i'm really bad at posting these things, it appears i DID publish the same video twice, i was doing it all on my phone so i guess i just lost track of what i was doing.

and as far as the nozzles? i tried it with three and with two. I liked it better with two. With two it shot the water farther outwards, not by much however, both shot out pretty far. Just for the size of my tank (48" wide) i think i will only need two. I'd rather run the pump longer times or more frequently than have a lot more water spraying or a "heavier rainfall". I imagine, four misting heads can be hooked up without getting the look of water drops (not droplets) shooting out, but seriously? I unless you just have a massive massive tank, 2-3 is just fine. Also, the pump can handle 60psi. I hooked it up to my truck's battery and put my finger over the output hole and shot water just as far as say a garden hose, it's just a matter of getting the right current/volts to the pump. I imagine my 1250mA transformer is much less than that of my truck's battery. This is probably another explanation. You'll need a really serious transformer if you want to shoot many many misting heads. Just some food for thought. Overall, i think this project costed me about...

pump: 36$ (with shipping)
transformer: free (laying around house)
electric plug replacement: 3$
microtubing and nozzles: 10$?
brass fittings: 5$
70psi tubing (70psi unnecessary): 15$

total: 79$
there are some corners you can cut, I could have just gone to an auto store and bought a few feet of tubing as needed, save some money, but here i did it this way and am sharing so you or others don't have to.

mistking starter setup: 99$ before shipping and tax if applicable

i am pleased, saved a couple bucks, i've been going in steps so it isnt all at once out of my pocket too, that's nice.
