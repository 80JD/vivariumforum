Here are a few pictures of the inside of the "Fan mod" Zoo Med hood.







Outside close up



I hope that helps...

As far as I remember,(I sold my last one w/ my first build), the inside of the Exo-T hood is very similar for the 18". I do know that the plastic itself is a little different, and maybe a bit thicker on the Exo -T. I wouldn't imagine those differences would pose any issues though.

Steps:
1. Unscrew the reflective light housing/ anything in your way on the fan end.
2. measure out where you want fan, mark/score the entire area you will cut
(I would measure 1/16" under on a 2 sides. That way you can have the hood hold the fan tight before you silicone it in. It also helps w/ the amount of vibration from the fan.)
3. Drill out a starter area within scrap piece.
4. Use grinding bit to make an area to fit a saw-blade into.
5. Use open ended hand saw to cut straight across the hood.
6. Repeat steps 3-5 till you have a square.
7. Pop the fan in,(recessed into hood), and silicone all sides top and bottom in place.

Hopefully that makes sense

Joe,
I had no intention of reinstalling the light into the hood. I placed the fan on the short side of the Zoo Med hood w/ intention to not use the light. Its placed on my top w/in an area that I don't need the light requirements. I kind of planned the build out that way. I also have other hoods w/ lights in the Viv. I would imagine that the plastic from the fan would melt if a compact fluorescent was placed right near it. It might take a few fans, and a lot of brand/bulb switching to find one that wont. If you choose to... You could help the potential melt issue by, having the fan mounted on the outside of the Hood instead of inside. Then you have aesthetics to worry about as well. I would also be concerned that the air would not travel as much into the Viv. From the light-bulb in effect dispersing it. Try it out and let me know
