Well, as you may have read, I ended up having to COMPLETELY tear down the tank because the female J. splendida escaped. This turned out to be a good thing, in a way. I got the FB fixed (it was dumping substrate underneath and I didn't know it), plugged all the holes, installed the hinge (actually did that before taking everything apart, but had to redo it), and even got my lighting 100% better!

I DO need to get a new piece of plexi (might pay the extra for lexan this time), this piece isn't cut straight on the (now) top edge; I'll be having it cut at the store this time... The curved edge was the bottom of the door before installing the hinge, and it's why my tape hinge was failing. I got some more clips installed to hold the door shut and it's much tighter now, so there shouldn't be anymore feeder escapes, especially since I plugged all the holes in the topper.

The substrate comes all the way up to the tank rim now, allowing me to add in a thicker drainage layer and keep the soil from staying to wet and spoiling. I DID have to trim my beautiful schefflera down to almost nothing, but hopefully the cuttings will root well (read: hopefully I don't forget to take care of the cuttings).

I changed my lights around. I took clamp laps from other tanks and have 4 of them on top now, one with a 13w 6700K CFL bulb, and the other three with 26W 6700K CFLs. This is SO MUCH BETTER, even though it's only one extra bulb. This is because the reflectors are covering more of the tank. The hood I had on there before was actually LIMITING the light. I put it on the fish tank (it takes two standard bulbs and has a spot for a tube, but the ballast fried, so it's got two of the 26W 6700K CFLs in it, and the fish tank is SO BRIGHT NOW! WOW, what a difference, I'll post pics of it later). Because I'm getting so much better light now, the water area is really lit up! I don't think I'll have any trouble growing plants, it's brighter down there than my fish tank used to be. I added several clumps of ambulia, one clump of the moss I had on one of the rocks before (does really well completely under water, much better than being in air at all), a tiny bit of what I think is guppy grass (a little teensy piece that was caught up in some java moss), and some more java moss on the waterfall and in the back under the FB (the light comes into the back of the tank from the window. I did this on purpose to grow algae, btw, guppies like algae and algae does help keep the water parameters stable, plus I bought an otto for the tank. ). I have some more plants coming, and I'll be putting them in here for now. The fish tank is mostly full, lol (I can't wait to show you guys the growth of the plants since the last time I posted a pic of it). I'll be getting some lace java fern, a crypt or two(not identified), 3 stems of Bacopa caroliniana (two over a foot long), and some riccia, which I'm going to float (I might put that in the fish tank).

I might eventually take my schefflera out of this tank and put in something that won't get so big. Now that I have less upward space for the plant to grow tall, It's going to be difficult to keep it trimmed, especially at the rate it grows. Also, after I get the cuttings rooted, I might sell some, so PM me if interested.

The tank looks a bit bare now. I'll have to get some new things to mount to the BG or something. Sure, it's nice to see the animals more, but I really like a thickly planted tank. On a happy note, my licorice fern is starting to produce ferns again, so that's great! Soon there will be more cover on the back wall as the ferns open up. I might put some moss on the BG where the fogger outlet is, it should do well there.
