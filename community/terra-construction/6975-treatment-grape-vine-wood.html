Treatment for Grape Vine Wood?
Hi all. I've been reading here for awhile, but this is my first post. I'm still in the design phase of a 55g terrarium, and trying to soak up all your experience. I really like the tropical rain-forest look of vines, twisted wood, and bromeliads. I have yet to actually put anything together, but saw a really incredible grape vine branch at a local pet store today for $40. I would have to cut it, as it wouldn't even fit into a 55! There would be plenty left over for another, smaller terrarium later on. My concern is the often-discussed issue of mold on grape vines. I have read that air circulation helps, but not everyone agrees that is the cure. The piece I am looking at could never fit into an oven for baking, and other than the bathtub, I'm not sure how I would soak it. The pet store owner said that if I soak it first (didn't mention any baking) it would never mold. That doesn't make sense to me. I was wondering if there might be a treatment I could use on the wood to make it unpalatable to mold? Maybe paint it with vinegar? How about linseed oil? A matte polyurethane? I will probably not be adding any critters to the tank, but if I do it would just be a few anoles. I'm really more into the plants and the creation of the environment than the keeping of herps. (been there, done that) I'm new to terrariums, though. If the mold is just inevitable on grape vine, maybe I'll keep thinking about that design. Your comments are appreciated.

Jim

Vinegar was what I was thinking when I read this. A good bath in white vinegar and a soak in HOT water should help and it shouldn't harm anything.

I've had one piece in my tank for almost a year that got the typical black fuzz which turned into white fuzz and now just looks normal. It might take a few months, but if you don't mind fuzzy branches for a while, it wouldn't hurt anything to just leave it. (I'd do the vinegar route myself).

Jim,
I would look into long term updates from postings regarding grape wood in wet habitat vivariums... I know lots of people claim it has a bad rap. Imho for good reason, , at best it will shorten the lifespan of your viv. I don't get why everyone loves grape wood anyway? It's not even hard to find Swahala,(which I love by the way)! Malaysian drift wood is even better, and ghost wood even better! If price is the issue, find an old freshwater aquarium setup that has taken a dump, and get a free/cheap waterlogged piece of wood. Then you won't mind cut and chop session... Lot of info out there on how to treat wood, do some searching. I'm into seeing the 55 be sure to post her!

A lot of people do eggcrate and then LECA (Lightweight Expanded Clay Aggregate) on top of that, separated by fiberglass screen material. I have that on my current tank, but I have found that there isn't enough water build up to make the egg crate layer. I'm skipping the egg crate and just using LECA with a tube going into it so I can siphon off excess water. Unless you're adding a water feature, I'd skip the egg crate.

ive had alot of experience with grapewood. if part of the grapewood is completely submerged in water all the time, the whole peice will mold like crazy. what i did is i boiled my grapewood, but i dont know how u cood do that if its a really big peice. if ur wood is not in contact with water at all, it shood do fine. the "logic" behind boiling ur wood first is: as the wood is in contact with water, the water will begin to water log the wood. as the wood is being water logged, it will begin to mold. after the water has reached the very middle of ur wood, the water will start receiding out of it and it will EVENTUALLY stop molding. so the thing is, if u boil it first, its supposed already water log it and sort of stop the molding process.
