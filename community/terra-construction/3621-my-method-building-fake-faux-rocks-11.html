O-K! After battling the Swine Flu, or a severe cold, I am back in action... YEESH! It's 1:30am, I put on the new Blu-ray DVD of the POLICE live reunion concert and started working about 2 hours ago. Nothing better then a late night project with good music and no cell phones going on interrupting you... Looking at this build, I am going to classify it as a more advanced build... way more work then the previous ones.

I want to take the two halves of corkbark I cut and make the wood meet rock where you see glass on this background. These first few pics will show the cork bark cut in half, and placement on the tank.

I then start cuttnig and fitting my large foam block, hoping I can use both halves for most of the background... like the left side and center areas.

I already constructed my false bottom from egg crate, PVC legs, and wire ties. My friend decided he wanted a small pond somewhere, so i drew one in with marker, cut it out, and finished it off with egg crate walls. The walls help later on when you want to stick concrete on the vertical areas. It sticks better having a wall there. So I recommend taking the time to make a complete floor.. no cutting corners. The thin creek will hopefully appear as a crack in the ground later that leads to the pond. We will try to make it look less like a straight line. The less water/pond the more room you will have for plants, so don't go crazy.

Now comes the fun.....

Fitting the wood and foam together like pieces of a puzzle. This will take a lot of time cutting, and placing the pieces in and out of the tank. I'm cutting and fitting a little at a time. You can always take away, adding will be a pain in the butt. There will be concrete/Habacrete mix joining the wood and foam together in the end, but, besides the pieces fitting side by side, there is space between the glass and the skinny part of the wood that touches the glass. My goal is to have a thin area of styro behind this wood that ends up touching the glass. I will most likely end up using aquarium silicone to seal the wood and foam to the glass, and to each other. I dont want to just rely on concrete on glass here. The tighter these pieces fit together, the stronger it should be in the end.

I'm staring by holding the wood against the foam to create the first contoured edge, where the foam meets the wood. Use a marker and draw in the outline of the trees edge. Look at the back of the tank and notice there is about an inch and a half thickness on the bark.... I'm going to try and keep a thin flat styro piece this wide, since the wood is not straight, and there are gaps along the glass. (hope this makes sense-look at the side profile pic of the tree)

Now I start cutting the foam with the heat tool, trying not to cut all the way out the back of the block since I want that thin edge behind the tree. After the front view contour is cut, I go and cut the thin side profile.
