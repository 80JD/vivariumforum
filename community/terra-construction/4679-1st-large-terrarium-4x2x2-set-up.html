1st large terrarium (4'x2'x2') set up
Hi all, I have two (1'x1'x1.5') exo-terras with ferns and spiders and I wanted to up-scale.

My plan is to have a native aussie terrarium stocked with ferns (fernarium?). I want it to look like a forest floor with a stream flowing into a pool (reservoir). The original plan was to have three viewing sides, which made for a great landscaping challenge, but the missus wanted the tank somewhere else so it's now backed up against a wall with no background!

When I was browsing through the native nurseries I found a few flowering plants including 2 orchids that should survive in the terrarium (the challenge is to make them flower). Since the orchids are lithophytic I decided to add a rockery to the terrarium.

I hope to house mantids and spiders in the tank, along with native slaters and millipedes if I can find them in the garden + a crayfish in the pool.

valiant effort... the tank looks great. my first thought though... "did he strip out all the old silicone?". silicone does not stick to silicone for long. since it's not completely filled with water you may be o.k.. i'd personally think should be ok. you have been officially warned just in case though.

anyway it does look great... have you considered blacking out the back or making a background. i think it would greatly improve the look and add depth. you can paint the back of the glass on the outside, use craft paper or automotive window tint. i like the tint because it gives the look of black glass like paint would but it's easy to get off.

Questions...
I tried to set up the stream with garbage bags but it was working except for t's ungainly sight. So I getting some pond liner tomorrow at Fernworld along with a couple of little Dicksonias, kanga ferns and a bird's nest fern.

Would anyone like to share their experience and suggestions on how to make a stream with pond liner or how to hide it? Cheers if you do.

My idea is to have the edge of the pond liner tucked under a little soil/gravel so I can have the little moisture loving plants, moss and lichen living on the banks. Hopefully the plants, some twigs/bark and rocks will cover up the lining enough.
stream-idea 1.jpg
