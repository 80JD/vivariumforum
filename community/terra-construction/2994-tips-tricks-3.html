I'll follow up the lemon juice w/ a vinegar solution tip. I've started using a spray bottle of 3:1 water:vingegar for small clean-ups around the house, or in animal areas where I just need to "clean", not "sterilize." (This is great for cleaning out cricket tanks if you're breeding them.) (a) no chemicals to kill your beasties or their food (b) cheaper than any other cleaning agent (c) although your house might smell vaguely like a salad for an hour, this is not as noxious to your olfactory or tactile senses as other cleaning products. (I do generally, dislike chemically smells, and I have birds, which are sensitive to airborne fumes.)

Of course, this will not fully sterilize. It's more for the quick spot clean. If you do need to sterilize with bleach, make sure there's no vinegar solution left anywhere that you are about to bleach.

The fruit fly thing reminded me of a couple other feeder insect tricks.
If keeping feeder crickets or roaches:
Ground rabbit pellets make for excellent food (use a regular coffee grinder to crush them, otherwise its difficult for the insects to eat the pellets) instead of expensive commercial insect food.
Watering crystals sold for use with potted plants are the same as the water crystals marketed for use with crickets, only much cheaper and it comes in a larger package that lasts and lasts....

The best advice I can think to give is to NEVER underestimate tank maintenance when constructing a vivarium. Allow room for your hand to access all areas of glass, filters and powerheads should be easily reached and removed, and contemplate the likelihood of spot cleaning and turning over your soil semi-regularly. I've personally wasted a lot of time and effort on beautiful tanks that I was unable to clean properly, and forced to start over...

P.S. don't use silicone, especially anything that's not GE II, in a closed in space. It'll smell worse than a freshly used old litterbox.

If you want to use some tree branch type moss on a bare branch in your tank, sterilize with CO2, let it dry out completely, staple it to the branch, muss the area around the staples to hide them, then water it. When making a pond on the ground with great stuff....
I also use a small section of a drinking straw placed through the great stuff area before the great stuff is applied, so it acts like a little pipe so water can flow through to the false bottom easier... this way if you have a strong pump, the pond will still drain faster, and not overflow... my creek on the 47 has no pipe like this so it's a higher water level then my floor water. Usually there will be a spot in the pond that wont be visible, and you can hide the straw there. Once the great stuff is dry, cut the straw flush with scissors... check out the pics of this small 10g pond/waterfall I made, you'll see the orange straws (this is a bottom view from under the false floor). I think I actually just pushed them right through the great stuff, then chopped off the ends that were clogged.
