With the film canisters, it would depend on the plant and how much space it needs for it's roots. Some plants would be fine in something that small, and some wouldn't. I have some planters in my GS in the frog tank that I just carved from the GS and put plants in. It's a little hard to carve it the way you want though (which could have been because of the way my tank is built). You can make a planter out of anything that is the right size for the roots of the plant you want to put there. I'd say that a variety of sizes can be better than all one size. If you really feel the need for drainage, you can poke a hole in the bottom of whatever you used for a "pot". If you don't have containers that are appropriate sizes, you can fill a plastic bag with something (sand, gravel, whatever will hold it's shape when pressed on firmly) and just GS around the bag, pull it out when it's cured, and viola! instant planter, in whatever size you need. Those planter Recycled mentioned work really well too. I didn't think about asking for them at the pet store, that's a good idea.

I've found that an easy way to mount broms is to cut off the pups, leave on the long stem, and then press the woody stem into the GS until you reach the base of the plant. You can then wrap a little sphagnum around the base of the plant at the GS and use a couple of staples to hold the moss to the BG to encourage root growth.

For the most part, broms put out roots to anchor themselves, rather than to take up water and nutrients. They do all that through the center cup.

I HIGHLY suggest that you purchase your plants BEFORE you do your BG. I let mine grow for a while before I put them in the vive, and I find that it works a little better that way (for me at least). I am an idiot when it comes to plants too, but the time with them still in their pots helps me learn how to care for each plant and by learning that, where it is best suited to go in the vive. This also allows you to plan placement of your plants before you lay your GS. If you just choose random places for your planters, you might not find plants that are suited to the spots you pick, whether it be from lighting, watering, size, or traffic. Once you get the plants, you don't have to wait to lay down the GS, because you have them all there and you can decide how to arrange them. Then you'll have all the plants growing happily while you finish the tank and get it ready for planting.
