Ma77hew7's 2nd Build - Zoo Med NT-4
I used many builds I've viewed postings of or seen displays of, for inspiration into this build. I will say, the older Black Jungle builds influenced me heavily! I also want to Thank Black Jungle for great customer service, excellent products and plants, the new "Rewards" program kicks ace!

I have longed to incorporate features of many builders designs, into a Viv of my own. So hopefully here goes somthin!

This is My 2nd build ever, I'm so excited and have learned a lot through trial and error in my first build. I know there will still be issues, thankfully I have the community now to bounce ideas, and problems off of
So without further adieu...

My original intention was to use an Exo-Terra PT2607 - 18"x18"x24"(WxDxH). The problem was, I couldn't find a suitable one on Craigs. Not that I'm opposed to Zoo Med, "Naturalistic Terrariums", ha ha; but that I'm very familiar w/ Exo-T's,(I have owned 4 of them).

So anyway... back to the build.

I started with the Zoo Med NT-4, w/ a hood,(that I got a killer deal on), both were in great shape. That along with price-point, helped me get over my Exo-T addiction. I had read lots of pros for Zoo Med's, so I was happy to try one out for myself.

I drilled out one of the cord holes to fit my pump cord, behind the lid. I have seen posts on other forums where people let the little plastic shell dictate what they do in the Viv. Don't do that, drill it, cut in, saw it, I've even butane torched my way around those lids, they are pretty forgiving,(both Exo-T's and Zoo Med's), and stronger than they look or seem...



I then started by silicone spreading on the sides,(not too concerned with my walls view of the Viv, hence no silicone on the back).



Then I placed the underwater heater,($13 Hydor Mini Submersible Heater,from a lps). I have had great success with Hydor's for Viv's where I only have 1-3" of water and not much space, or depth for bulky tube style heaters.



Sorry for the book, I'm excited let me know if I'm too verbose...

Now on to the water features... Yes I said features, this small container is going to have a lot going on! I'm super into it! My original plan was to have a multifunctional drip wall/ waterfall feature built in. Run by one internal workhorse pump,(used for outdoor ponds), running a 3/8” tubing into the dripper manifold – Who would then send H2O to 8 other ¼” lines!



The best part,(pun intended), is that the dripper manifold has adjustable flow! So I can control each of the eight lines output depending on its desired function, plants nearby, etc. I might be a little over zealous about this thing, but it's functionality has been my saving grace many a times in this build thus far!

I wanted to conceal the pump,(which I purchased from a local garden center $13), from view obviously. So I set out for the perfect starting point to a cave for my little H2O loving friend.

Now I don't know about you guys but once I have it in my head about a specific Viv product, and I think its going to be perfect. I will end up trying to make it that way until I either destroy it, or make it work...



That being said... I finally found my cave of choice, Habascape Cave $8 off Amazon, (did not find a distributor who worked w/ lps's in CO). They look great, and are easy to drill/cut/grind. I'm tired. I'll post more later Thanks!

I then proceeded with a wet run of the pump. Monitoring the coco background, and replacing the coco filled water many times over the next few weeks - in areas where the water flow would soon take me back a few steps... 1 step forward, 2 steps back. At the same time it was fun to play around with the manifolds flow adjustment to each hose, and was also great for placement of plants to come. You can see all the husk in the water, even after a thorough vacuum session on the background.



I was getting exited to see more, so I prematurely put the 24" Monkey Ladder's,(also from Black Jungle), into the setup...



I started to find out after the rock and bio-filtration media was pushed aside, my Habascape cave,(even though at this point was full of holes on most sides and plenty on top), was still buoyant That along with my choice of coco background started me on a "working out the bugs phase", that I'm still in...

I was able to find another cave that was actually easier to cut/drill/grind than the Habascape, and more importantly was not buoyant. Exo-Terra XL cave,(that I used about half of), and was able to grind away the rear of the cave to meet with my background for a tighter fit and less conspicuous for sure. I also preferred the warmer tones from that specific cave to the lack of choice,(at least from the seller on amazon), with the Habascape. Being that I received a gray/black one...



Quick note, I love this stuff! It's on the Pricey side,($28 at my local garden supply store), but I've used it a lot in pond style uses and in my last Viv. Has anyone else used it in Viv's for 1year +?
