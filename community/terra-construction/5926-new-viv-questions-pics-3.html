Andry, I hate to say it, but I think you should start over...

I wish I had been online more often lately, I could have pointed out some things to you before you finished when you could have fixed them earlier.

The metal screen is totally inappropriate. I'm sure we have stressed over and over again that you MUST use fiberglass screen, as metal screen will rust. When it rusts, it will begin to degrade and eventually fall apart. when it starts falling apart, it will fall down into your water, and it will allow your substrate to fall in as well. This can happen faster than you might think... Is there a reason you chose to go with metal screen?

The height of your FB is going to leave your soil very wet most of the time. There should be an air gap between the bottom of the FB and the top of the water to prevent the soil from wicking to the saturation point. This gap should be at least 1/2", preferably 1".

I don't understand why you put down a layer of bark, THEN leca. What was your reason for this? the leca is used as a drainage layer, and as such should be the BOTTOM layer, not the middle. Each layer should be separated by a layer of FIBERGLASS screen. So, you have the bottom of the tank, water, air, eggcrate, scree, leca, screen, soil mix (you can layer or mix this however you want, in your case I would do soil then bark).

I haven't watched your video (my internet connection doesn't like videos much), but from the context above I think that your drain is leaking, correct? Did you just screw a fitting into it, or did you seal the fitting? With a bottom drain, it is necessary for you to seal the fitting for the drain. You should put a bead of silicone down, press the fitting into it, then put a bed around the fitting and smooth it into the seam with a finger.

Did you cover your filter box with a plant?

If you are getting lots of substrate in your pump, then you need to build a better pump housing. dart's idea of a removable box within a box is a good idea. Regaurdless, a box covered in FIBERGLASS mesh is a good start. You can sew the mesh onto the box with fishing line or NYLON thread (NO COTTON!!!) to be sure it will not gape and allow sustrate into the housing. You can cover this with nylon stockings, but the stocking will clog fairly quickly. A solution to this is to create a staged filter outside of the stocking, such as LECA (since you have it already), filter sponge or something like PLASTIC kitchen scrubbies, FIBERGLASS screen, stocking, FIBERGLASS screen, eggcrate, pump (that's layers of filtration from outside to inside).

With all of the things that you NEED to fix, it would be easier to pull everything out than try to retrofit everything where it is.

It's very important to solve one problem before charging ahead and finishing a project. If there were things going wrong, why didn't you get them fixed before continuing to the next step? Now you have more work than you would have had if you'd finished each step before moving on. I'm not scolding or trying to upset you, I am just pointing this out to you to be sure that you see it. You aren't a failure at this, you just need to work step by step next time.
