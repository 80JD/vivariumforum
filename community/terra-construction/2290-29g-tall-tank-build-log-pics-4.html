Quote:
Originally Posted by Shana's_mom
I don't recommend putting a sticky under-tank heater on the glass behind the GS foam BG. I did this, thinking it would be OK since it wouldn't come in contact with water. Not only did the heat not get through the BG but it ended up cracking my glass. (It happened in less than a week.) I guess the BG acted like an insulator and kept the heat on the glass. It was an Exo Terra glass tank.

By the way, I am using the Exo Terra Compact Top Canopy. (My new tank is 45 x 45 x 60 cm) I have 2 of them and they each hold two bulbs. I have one fitted with two Sun Glo incandescent bulbs and the other fitted with two Moon Glo incandescent bulbs. I leave the Moon Glo on 24 hours. My tank gets to 30C in the day and 20C at night. It is home to my daughter's two adorable White's Green Tree Frogs.

Love your set-up and write-up. Your frogs should be very happy.
water - metal - rock... something may have touched your glass causing uneven heat... or it may have been too close to the water. putting a heat mat behind gs is no different than under sand substrate.

did you use a desert mat in a tropical viv and then mist or fog??? was the heat mat faulty? there is an underlying issue here we are not seeing that is for sure. i have been using some sort of heat mat as long as i can remember. i have used them on fish tanks, tropicals, and deserts and never had any such issue. trust me the issue here is not using a heat mat, rather the heat mat you used (whether faulty or not) or something you overlooked.

remember heat melts glass if high enough... it's cold or rather cooling that cracks it. if your mat was faulty then heat could easily be concentrated in one small area making it much hotter than it should get causing rapid cooling in surrounding areas or when powered down. definitely not the addition of insulation making this happen.

last but not least... if the wall you place your viv against is a cool spot in your house it's likely that there is a draft the mat may have been working against causing a perpetual heating/cooling which is pretty likely to produce these results. it is common knowledge in the general hobby of putting animals in glass houses that drafty or cool areas of the house are suggested against. that alone could very well have been all you needed to avoid the whole cracking glass scenario.

now i know i might be coming off as pointing a finger but to take away one of our most IMPORTANT tools over 1 very unlikely mishap would imo be ridiculous. like i said there is an underlying cause and you might want to get your money back for your uth as it is very likely it is faulty in your case... not usually likely... but in your case i'd want my money back... and a couple freebies .
