With FBTs in a small tank like that, you are going to want no more than two in the tank. In order to maximize space, I think I would go with a ledge system, with parts that jut over the water and little islands that come out rather than going with part land part water. This will allow you to have almost the whole bottom of the tank water, but still have alot of land (they do come out of the water, they need ample space to explore out of the water, they even climb, they just do all that at night when no one is paying them any attention, lol). You don't want the water to be more than 3 inches deep, so you may not be able to use a standard filter. You can have the water running down the BG and across/through different types of surfaces to help filter it.

I don't have much time today, or I'd give you some more advice... I've had my Marvin for years now (he's getting old, lol, but still going strong!), in a variety of setups, and I think I have come to some conclusions on what they prefer in a vive setup.

A simple rain system will be a must and will be the best way to show your students their most natural behavior. A rain system is best, rather than a misting system, it's the drops that make them think it is breeding time.

Hi SHF!
I kept a pair of fbt's a couple years ago before I discovered this forum. I went with a fairly low tech set-up at the time.Was also a 10 gallon tank,with a 50/50 land/ water ratio.The water was about 6" deep,used slate to build up the land side.Filtration was a fluval #1 submersible,worked really well.I originally thought I had a pair due to all the mating,quite comical I must say.However discovered that both toads were in fact male, not that there's anything wrong with that! They do a lot of "hugging & barking" . I made the mistake of setting up their tank in my bedroom,but had to move due to the constant "chatter".The colors are quite variable as well. I chose the bright green variety,with orange tummies and finger tips.For a school project I agree these are a good choice.Kids also love to watch them hunt down the crickets! Hope that helps.
