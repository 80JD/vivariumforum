And you thought we were done painting....

Alright, I want to make this look as real as possible, and if moss or algae doesn't grow on my waterfall, no problem, it will already be there. The paint I'm using is Phthalocyanine Green, and Black (non toxic acrylic by Liquitex). Water it down for this step or it will look too fake. I'm washing on some dark green in areas where water would splash but not constantly run over the rock. From the top to bottom of the waterfall, and around the base where it meets the creek. On some rocks I will go a little heavier on the paint mixture. With using less black, you'll get a bluer green in areas. Being watered down allows you to get that blended in look, like the algae is lightly covering the rocks.

Next step is the area where water runs over the rock. I've looked at several pics of rainforest waterfalls, and have even seen them in person. Where the water has contact with the rock, it turns a dark brownish black color. I don't know if it's from mud or algae, but it looks black. So I'm making a dark muddy wash using the Burnt Sienna, and Black paint. Keep in mine I test this stuff out on a small area first. When it dries and I like it, then I continue. I hit the areas where water would run down the waterfall, and even continued the dark color into the pond. The waterfall looked just fine without the green and brown steps, but check out the finished look. When the real water runs over this, it should look pretty neat.

I'm going to house orange and black leucomelas, three of them. I'm currently housing a Dendrobates galactonotus with them, he's small, black and yellow. They are all getting along, I know it's not recommended, but this is only temporary. I also have blue azureus (in another tank), they will be in my next 55 gallon project!

I use Liquitex Acrylics. They come in tubes, and small plastic bottles. They are pretty much at any hobby store. Actually, you can find the green styro, white styro, and the heat tool there as well. The tool ran around $20 or so. I cured the small waterfall in the 10g for nearly 3 weeks in water and vinegar. I changed the water every three days. The rock was stuck to the glass so well, I just has to fill the tank with water to submerge it. I did that in a bath tub. Let me know if you start a thread, I'd like to see your pics! I just saw today that some guys use tile grout instead of cement. Then they used a non toxic sealer... no soaking was necessary with that stuff, so maybe look into it. The only bag of Ultraflex I found was 50lbs or so.. so there is LOTS of left over material especially if your only doing a ten gallon... so maybe the grout is better? Look on Youtube for the guy that does the rock tutorials with the grout for lizards... I saw him today after I posted my vids. His doesn't seem to get the finer details though like mine did. ?

Here's some shots of whats happening up top. Some of the white foam is temporary. I need to plug it up so they cant squeeze through to the overflow. You can see the pump and tubing running down. I made the nozzel out of plumbing and aquarium fittings. I wanted it to fan out so it wasn't too strong flowing out. I'm fitting the plexiglass tops. You'll notice I left some of the blue plastic over the overflow area, just to keep light from shining down and promoting any unwanted algae. And yes, I had to put the frogs in, I couldn't take it anymore!!! They are running all over the place, they seem to love it. We'll see how they like the waterfall in a bit. I ended up plugging two of the cracks in the rock a little because the water was traveling too far over my wall. I just used a little Habacrete. And remember the yellow guy is only in there temporarily.
