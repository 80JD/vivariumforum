Work slowly and patiently. Get a really sharp blade, I used an Olfa blade, but a razor blade would probably work better. Then just work the blade through the silicone carefully. Make sure not to put too much pressure on the glass or you will do what I did and crack the side glass. I built the screen using materials I bought at the HD, to make window screens. You just have to cut the aluminum sides to the right length then add the screen using a spline tool. Once the screen was made I cut the glass and siliconed both pieces back in glass first then the screen. I hope this helps.

I did take the whole pane out. I peeled the bottom black trim back, I didn't take it all the way off. If I were to do it again I probably would just remove it completely. It wasn't necessary to remove the top trim on my aquarium. I cut the glass myself. It was a poor cut but it didn't matter as I put the cut end at the bottom inside the black trim.

As to the foam I used, it was the black can Great Stuff. I used about two cans on the whole build. I say about because I used one can that had been used for minor household repairs first and one can must have had a propellant leak as it didn't work the second day I went to use it.

A couple things I found with expanding foam. Firstly the name is accurate, it expands a lot so start small and get a feel for it. Don't worry though if you use too much as it can be cut back. As I stated earlier in this thread I used an Olfa knife and a Dremel tool to shape most of my foam. Second it is really sticky and messy so I prefered to let it dry before trying to shape or mold it.

typically messing with the power that goes to a water pump is a bad thing. it can throw off the timing between electrical pulses coming from the wall and push or pull the pump motor to one side or another. the effects are not usually noticed until it's too late and the pump is already degraded.

a better solution would be to try and find a small ball valve or gate valve that can be plumbed into your hose after the pump. you could slow the flow that way without throwing off or seriously degrading your pump over time. the trick is finding one that fits your size hose. even a cut off/disconnect could be used for this as they are usually just 2 ball valves with a disconnect between them.

do not plumb a valve in to the intake side, it can create a suction that kicks up air and degrades the pump much faster than using a dimmer/rheostat.
