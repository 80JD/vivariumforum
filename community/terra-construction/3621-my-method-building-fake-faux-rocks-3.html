ok, here are some pics of the new build. It is a 47g tall tank. It will have a false bottom, with a rock waterfall across the whole back of the tank. I am using Habacrete this time. I'm using my same method with the blocks of styrofoam as the foundation of the faux rocks. I feel you get much more control over detail this way rather then spraying great stuff everywhere. It's time consuming, but fun.

I started out with a large block of green styrofoam that you would use to stick silk flowers in.... you can find it at any hobby lobby place. I then cut the shape I wanted using the hot knife. First just a basic shape, then making more details as I went on. This set up will have an overflow type hole that runs behind the rock wall. This way I can access my pump, heater, and water easily. The 4" PVC tube will be the support for the hole. I used a template to decide what shape I wanted the back waterfall to be, then traced it on my foam block. Then the cutting starts.

Using the hot knife tool, you can get pretty creative with the detail of your rocks. If I did this again, I would avoid tiny cracks, and make most of them larger, since the Habacrete mix isn't easy to fill in those small cracks.

I mixed up some Habacrete and started covering the foam today. I made a very runny batch first to get into the cracks, then thickened it up later. After a few thin coats, it was covering nicely. Using a butter knife, I kept the cracks from filling in too much, and kept sculpting the rock formations. I'd mix it in a plastic cup in small batches. This stuff dries fast, so you dont want a large amount drying up in a bucket while you work.

If the sculpted areas looked too much like concrete, I would smooth them out with a wet paint brush. Keep cutting in the cracks with the butter knife before the coats get too dry.
