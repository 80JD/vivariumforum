The foaming is REALLY starting to come along now. I've done some work on the left side, and I decided to male it look like a tree growing down over a rock. It's turning out pretty good so far! I really like being able to take my time and work on this without rushing to get the critter inside, lol...

I've got the right side pretty much finished. All I have left to do is to decide where to cut it to make the top removable. I'll figure out how to disguise the seam once I start covering the foam. One of the things I like about using GS is that any time before it's covered, you can cut a door in something and be able to just pull it out whenever and stick it back in TIGHT! so that animals can't move it. I'm hoping to be able to preserve that quality in this build without compromising aesthetics.

I may end up covering the foam almost entirely in crete instead of the binder method, but we'll see when it comes to it what ends up being easier to do.

One tip i have for those building with GS, when you have to do multiple layers, letting them cure in between, save the pieces you cut off when you carve in between layers. You can use these pieces to build and bridge gaps as you continue to build. You can use the GS sort of like mortar, and the cured foam like bricks. It's not exactly the same, but it works! For example, In the picture of the left side I'm working on above, you can see a place where a root comes down over a gap, I used a piece of cured scrap foam to bridge the gap and make a continuous piece without having to do multiple layers.

Also, when you are working in layers, carving or sanding the skin from the GS before adding the second layer of foam helps the GS adhere better. There are places where I didn't do that, and it's fairly easy to peel the second layer of foam from the first.

Comments welcome, of course.
