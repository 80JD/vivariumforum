I don't see why you couldn't build it more than that tall- perhaps those specs were for the whole thing to be filled with water and used as an aquarium?

Glass is easy- there are a ton of window/glass stores around where I live, at least. They'll cut whatever you want to size, and most places that specialize in the material will drill holes and everything for you. If you don't need to consider the weight of water pressure on it, you can use quite thin glass without a problem- tempered glass only keeps it from breaking into splinters when it does eventually break, I believe. and thicker glass tends to get heavy.

Here's a link to a build design that's just glass, but might give you some ideas as to how to vent the front: http://forum.theorchidsource.com/ubb...arium_Ste.html

I really think you should pursue this- a homemade tank would be far more rewarding than working within the confines of a store-bought tank. Good luck.

I think a lot of people end up using a marine epoxy like West Marine brand. I looked into less toxic/more environmentally friendly alternatives for water sealing, but wasn't able to come up with anything that's as effective as fiberglass or epoxy. I think the epoxy might be a bit easier to work with, but I'm not entirely sure.

You'll definitely want to seal the whole interior if you're intending to keep it humid as opposed to just the bottom- it'd be a shame to put that much work into it just to have it rot away on you.

I'm not too sure about those vinyl tracks- I think the space seperating the two panes of glass would only be as wide as the bit between them on the track itself, right? It's something to consider. You might be able to put a bit of rubber squeegee-ish type material on one of the panes or something, but it'd be less than nice aesthetically, perhaps.

I think this build'll be a piece of cake for you- keep us posted.

Gorilla Glue will work.

It is polyurethane based glue.

Tips
Wipe the area to be glue lightly with a damp rag before applying the glue. It helps activate the glue and makes it stck better.

Definately use clamps. As the glue expands as it cures. Even with 1 1/2 Inch finish Nails. To much glue can cause it to expand and warp the seams..Just a Visual problem

Ceramic Backer board is not the thick Cement Board. Both Can be Used. But as you stated the Cement Board does get rather Heavy. If you use either one do not seal it. Why, Because you will not get good adhesion with any mortar or adhesive you attempt to adhere with. There is no need to seal it because both are already waterproof.
