Yeah, its a bit of "light" reading but burning PVC pipe releases toxic compounds including hydrogen chloride gas and dioxins which present a slightly different, even elevated danger to a person than you would see in the manufacturing process.

Now, having started to read a bit more into this, there is a real issue with "dioxin residue" which requires warning lables for human products, so I would imagine that issue is magnified for our herp friends. I'm not the expert in this field but it seems that dioxin residue is not something you can just clean off the burnt pvc, it even leaches through normal pvc, but burning increases the amount of dioxin created.

Its an interesting read and the related articles make me wonder...

some people will take things more seriously than others. for example; i'm on another forum where right now a guy is considering cleaning out USED truck gas tanks and making fish tanks out of them... and selling them to un-suspecting folks through craigslist.

as well there are concerns that many folks overlook. how many folks bake wood and without thinking open the oven door with their faces over it? it's a sure way to get burned because steam sticks well to the skin long before it cools.

dry ice... it's a great way to flood a tank with co2 and kill your bugs without chemicals that will stay in the tank as residuals and kill future inhabitants. most folks see that idea and think it's the greatest thing since sliced bread. i wonder sometimes how many of those folks stop to realize that it's so cold that touching it might just cause a person to lose a finger.

there are stupid and/or uneducated people everywhere. as well there are folks who are so intelligent that common sense just never crosses their cerebral cortex... their entire life... smart people do the dumbest things often.

it's good to have the information there... or a direction to go to find it... just in case. not that i'm scared of making my own wood... i just think the real thing looks better, imho. but when people are scorching plastic it would be a good thing for them to know what the risks are.

it doesn't stop every manufacturer from producing baby bottles containing bisphenal a (bpa) but aware consumers realize they'll eventually probably microwave a bottle full of milk and choose to search out alternatives that don't contain bpa.

either way, this is a great alternative, imo since it'll last practically forever and can look very real. some folks will choose to do it and others will choose not to. there's 2 sides of the coin and i, personally don't think anyone has to justify their stance on the matter until herps/fish/whatever start dropping like flies. i've seen no reports of such happenings so, imo the biggest threat is to the person doing the work, really.

however... if burnt enough this might just cause an issue for some folks... especially in completely enclosed systems where no air exchange normally occurs such as tropical enclosures. who knows... but what i'm saying is use your best judgment. to each his own...

i think i understand... and don't exactly disagree with your stance, tazely... nor joeg's stance... and if submarinr feels better getting more answers on the matter... that's great too. maybe he'll come back with scientific evidence clearly stating one way or another on the toxicity/safety of this method. i'd appreciate it personally.

either way... this is really starting to get off-topic. i'm leaving it here for now because it is pertinent to the original topic... but if it goes any further i'm going to have to move it to it's own thread, i fear.

crazyairborne... please keep in mind that in no way is any of this supposed to be a downer or bash... this is great work and very innovative... i'm sure many of us (including me) really appreciate you sharing your work/experiences. thank you.
