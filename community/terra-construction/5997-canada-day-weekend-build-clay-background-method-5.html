"Thats why sodium bentonite based substrates are now used often"

I don't get what you are saying - don't you mean "calcium bentonite" rather than sodium bentonite?

Is there any data that supports the theory that calcium bentonite is going to add nutritional calcium or is this a "it sounds correct so it must be true", thing ?

Please don't get me wrong, I not trying to rain on anyone's parade, I just trying to get to the actual science, because everything I hear ( on this and other forums ) is that plants don't necessarily like being planted in a heavy clay, and line plants many types of micro-fauna are unable to burrow through a heavy clay. From the stand point of many years of gardening, while clay is great for holding water - it's also very poor at allowing air to reach roots - thus they often smother , and this is why you amend clay garden soils with organic matter ( mixing it in ) to lighten the soil, making it easier for roots to penetrate and for both water to drain and air to reach the roots.

I was doing some research on clay and calcium to see if a calcium based clay does add nutritional calcium, and while I couldn't find any support for calcium bentonite, I did find this little blurb on Wiki, about NASA using a type of red clay to provide nutritional calcium to astronauts:
The effects of weightlessness on human body were studied by NASA back in the 1960s. Experiments demonstrated that weightlessness leads to a rapid bone depletion, so various remedies were sought to counter that. A number of pharmaceutical companies were asked to develop calcium supplements, but apparently none of them were as effective as clay. The special clay that was used in this case was Terramin, a reddish clay found in California. Dr. Benjamin Ershoff of the California Polytechnic Institute demonstrated that the consumption of clay counters the effects of weightlessness. He reported that "the calcium in clay ...is absorbed more efficiently ... [clay] contains some factor or factors other than calcium which promotes improved calcium utilization and/or bone formation." He added, "Little or no benefit was noted when calcium alone was added to the diet"
( Medicinal clay - Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia )
Doing some further checking I found that Terramin clay ( or stuff claiming to
be Terramin ) is currently in demand in some areas of the health food industry and as such is available on-line - even at Amazon.com ......


Now that being said, my gardening background tells me that clay in moderation and mixed with organic matter ( not just having the organic mater on top ) is more biologically active ( and thus more productive ) and as such is also more likely to get on and be eaten by the micro fauna, and a mixed substrate is also going to allow more critters to be able to burrow through it - which is part of what what we want, isn't it?



It really seams to me that searching this forum and Dendroboard, that there was a real quick change from using clay granules mixed in the substrate to an almost pure clay "slab" as substrate and then another quick change to using another almost pure clay "slab" as the background material - and while I can accept ( and plan on using ) some clay in the substrate, the speed of the change make me wonder if the science is sound, or if it isn't just another fad that people are using to try and make their tank as successful as someone else's tank ( who happened to use lot's of clay ).


I'm not saying that trying to have a awesome tank is a bad thing, but in some ways, what I am hearing and seeing on the forums, is that it appears that people are deciding that they are going to use clay just because someone else did - and in some cases, it really doesn't matter what type of clay it is, or what is in it, or where it came from, they are going to use it because it's what they have and everyone else is using clay ( this is what I'm talking about when I say that the subject has the earmarks of a fad ) - they don't know why they should or should not be using clay ( or even what type should not be used ).

Interesting arguments, Greg. I think one of the reasons it may become a "fad" is the ease of construction, price, and quick setup time for the BG.

I feel I must ask though, unless one uses the wrong clay, or someone points out some sort of hazzard that these clay BGs might pose, couldn't it be more beneficial that a BG made of an expanded petroleum product? Or even better than something like concrete, which can burn the skin of delicate amphibians if not cured properly? Also, while plant roots may not easily penetrate a clay substrate, and it may smother the roots, isn't it easier for some climbing plants to attach to a clay BG than one made of foam that is covered in silicone, or a BG made of concrete? Fad or not, the benefits outweigh some of your arguments, IMO. For example, while folks on other sites are using clay as the substrate as well, no one on THIS site is doing that (or at least not talking about it). Grimm used the clay for his BG and then used a soil mix for the substrate. The folks that are planning to or already trying this are also only using it for the BG, using it as a substrate hasn't even been discussed (until now, lol). I think as an all natural, cheap, and easy method to construct a BG, it has alot of things in it's favor. The thing I see as possibly being a problem is the clay drying out and possibly breaking down, or being damaged by being CONSTANTLY wet (if used for a waterfall, for example). That's why I'm hoping that Grimm will do a test with it. If it can't be allowed to dry out or be used for waterfalls, that limits the types of enclosures it can be used in. Remember, on this site not everyone is housing darts. Many of us house varieties of herps, including dessert species. What I want to see is a BG that is made in a similar fashion that can be used in ALL environments.

Honestly, I am personally ALWAYS on the lookout for cheaper, easier, faster, nicer looking BG techniques that are SAFE for our pets. The more we can reduce the money we spend to set up tanks and keep from spending money at the big box pet stores that abuse the herps they sell, the better!
