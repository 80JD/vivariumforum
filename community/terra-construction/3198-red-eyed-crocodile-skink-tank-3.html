For the "pegs" to be sure the enclosure is held in place, you could use some bolt style locks, latches, or hasps (either a regular one, or the ones with the flat hooks, which I think might be best if you go with a hasp). That way it would hold it securely, and you would be able to just unlock it when you need to move it.

Here's some links for what I'm talking about:
Bolt
Latch
Hasp

You might not need quite so much ventilation, but that's a good place for it. You wont lose as much humidity from the front as you will from the top. I would recommend that you go with a square opening rather than round. Reason being, it will be easier to cover it if you are getting too much ventilation. Since you are building it yourself, you're only limited by your imagination. You could even build a sliding cover into the design in case you need to cover the vents, that way you can easily do it with a slide of the wrist, and you can control how much you cover.

I LOVE your idea of supports around the tank to keep the weight off the glass. Great idea. You may have saved me some heartache there. Mine wont be all wood since I'm doing GS on screen in a frame, but it will still get heavy with the branches and stuff going into the background.

Rather than cutting a hole in the top and covering it with screen for the UV, you should just mount a UV strip inside the top. The bulb doesn't get hot enough to burn, they are safer than the CFL type, and you wont be blocking a great deal of your UV with screen (the finer the screen, the more UV it blocks, a fine mesh, like we commonly use for screen tops, actually blocks most of the UV). If you are worried about the moisture messing up the strip, you could probably waterproof it somehow (I haven't thought that one through yet, but I suppose you could coat the assembly in silicone... You could even probably cover it with peat moss if you wanted too, I don't think they get hot enough to catch it on fire). With a tank that tall, it will be very important that your UV source isn't diminished by screen. Once the dragon was 6 inches away from the top of the tank, he'd never get any UV. Mounting it inside, you can control placement and avoid having to attach it to the top. Using a silver reflector (like foil tape or even aluminum foil) will help the UV penetrate farther. Make sure you have at least one basking spot where the top of the branch is no less that 10" from the bulb (8 is best, they say the dragon should be able to get within 6 inches of the bulb to get the best UV exposure with a tube bulb, 8" allows for the height of the dragon on top of the branch).

If you want to save on weight and wood and having to worry about waterproofing it, you can do what I'm planning and build a frame to attach screen to and then lay GS on the screen. you can then cover the outside with thin MDF or anything else you want to. That will make the enclosure much less heavy, plus you can play with the design a little and make places to plant plants in the BG and all of that. The thing that takes the longest is planning it and covering it (and waiting for it to cure, lol). Anywhere you want to mount a heavier branch, you can add a cross piece to your frame and screw the branch into the frame for added stability. As for easy cleaning, one of the points about having a vive is that you don't have to clean it constantly. You are trying to build an ecosystem, animal creates waste, plants use waste, etc. It was a little weird for me at first, but I got used to it. I love not having to clean my vives very often. Maintenance involves changing water (I don't have a FB in the CWD tank), pulling out excess leaf litter, and trimming plants. The animal will be happier if you don't have to disturb their environment to clean it all the time ( I usually do mine once a month or so, other than water changes).

I was thinking that you could also just set the eggcrate on top of the screen. It will make it easier to use as a regular tank again in the future as well. Are you going to cover the eggcrate in additional screening?

For the doors, if you have trouble finding hardware to make sliding doors (I haven't found anything yet, but I may not be looking for the right thing), you can make hinged doors fairly easily. Take a look at a wooden picture frame to see how to make the frame and install the glass. Heck, if you've got some extra picture frames that are the right size, you can go ahead and just hinge those, lol. It's really pretty easy and the finished product looks better, IMO, and makes it easier to get into the tank (you can open the whole front, instead of having a piece of glass in the way). I don't think you'll like having that piece of wood in the middle of the tank, so I'd just go ahead and do the doors all the way down to the frame around the glass tank, or put in a pane of glass there instead (you can use the same picture frame type deal if you want to). I'd personally go with between 8 and 10" for the piece of wood across the top of the front, so it doesn't cut down on viewing too much.

You can line any wood you use on the inside with pond liner to waterproof it (I'd use an adhesive to attach it, maybe a spray adhesive? anyone have any better ideas on that one?).

lol, that's about all I have for now!

Glass will block ALL UV.

Quote:
UVB explosure from a heat lamp above it
UVB bulbs don't generally produce heat (or not enough to even factor into your heating). The only ones that do are mercury vapor bulbs (MVBs).

It is safe to mount a strip inside the enclosure. It will not get hot enough to burn the animal. I have the UV light mounted inside my beardie tank about 6 inches (could be less) from his basking site. He could easily touch it, but I know it wont hurt him because I can touch it and it is only a little warm.

Heat lamps will have to be mounted outside (or you could use heat mats, which will also have to be mounted outside). Don't set them on top of glass, it can be disastrous (check the thread "I am so pissed." in Off-Topic). I'm not sure how much ventilation would be needed to keep it from cracking the glass, and I doubt I'll experiment with it again, lol. Heating your water will help heat the enclosure as well, so a small fish tank heater will be a good addition (I recently bought one for a 10-15G that is actually adjustable for about 15 bucks). If you use heat mats instead of heat bulbs (where they wont come in contact with any water and aren't mounted on wood) you can just use fluorescents for you lighting, which can all be mounted inside. One for UV and two regular ones for brighter light would work well, and the fixture would be cheaper if you build it yourself (not very difficult to wire a ballast, from what I understand). Then you can just make the top out of wood. The more of your tank that is made out of wood, the better you will be able to maintain your temps. If you want to use heat mats and you don't have anywhere to mount them that isn't on wood, you can attach them to the back of a tile and mount the tile inside the enclosure. The ceramic will disperse the heat throughout the tile, heating the enclosure without creating a hot spot where the animal will be burned. You can also buy some flexwatt, which is cheaper and easier to customize. I'd go with two or three smaller heat mats over one larger one and space them throughout the vive to get your temp gradient (warmer at the top near the UV basking spot and cooler near the water).
