Last summer I got a fish tank similar in size from a friend who, like yours, kept it outside for a few years. The tank was full of spiderwebs, tree leaves and all sorts of grime dried onto the bottom sections of glass. After testing for leaks, I'd put in a solution of 50/50 water and white vinegar, and let it sit a few hours. I drained the tank, wiped away whatever came off with paper towel alone, then used the vinegar to soak more paper towel and got the rest of the tough spots. Next time though, I think I might try the half lemon scrub. Just to compare.

UPDATE

so i finally got my piece of glass today (they quoted me 3-4 days, took 7). anyway, i did one more cleaning of the existing glass first with just a multi-purpose cleaner, then with rubbing alcohol where the new silicone will go to make sure there was no left over residue from anything. then, siliconed it in place. i ran into one minor problem. as i have never siliconed glass together before, i didn't know how large a bead to run to ensure that i get full coverage while not getting a lot of excess silicone oozing out of the seems. as advice to any other's trying their hand, i would go ahead and use enough to make sure that you get full coverage, then go back and wipe away the excess as i undershot it and only got about 90% coverage.







so now i must wait the 24 hours for it to cure, then will run a large bead of silicone along the inside of the joints, smooth it out with a wet finger, then the construction of the faux rock wall/hardscape begins.

so long as you're turning it you don't have to worry about that bottom but you can never use that tank to hold a full load of water again with the glass on that way. it would have to go inside the perimeter glass to hold water again.

a good idea... take an old credit card and a 2 liter bottle cap. put the cap on the card touching 2 edges and trace a line around the cap to the edges and cut along that line. then use that corner you made to press and smooth the silicone in place.

total necessity, just in case... cut away all the new silicone once it's dry from the inside. silicone does not stick to dry silicone and you'll need as much bonding surface clear as you can get on the glass for a re-seal.

another tip i've seen people be successful with... take blue painters tape and put it 1/4" from the corner all the way around, apply the silicone, let it sit, cut the silicone along the edge of the tape and remove the tape for straighter lines.

finally an UPDATE:

firstly i'd like to apologize ahead of time for the poor quality pics. they were taken at night in my basement. ok, so basically since the last time i posted i ran the bead of silicone around the inside corner of the new piece of glass installed on the back and smoothed it with my finger. then cut some screen and siliconed that in place over the air flow vents. let that all cure for 24 hours.





then started cutting/shaping/carving styrofoam for the faux rock wall backround.






my girlfriend had this sweet little tool that carves styrofoam. it has a plastic handle with a thin metal rod that extends out maybe 6" that gets really hot. it basically just melts the styrofoam, but it is very versatile. you can see it in the bottom, right of the pic above (she says she got it at hobby lobby for about $30 if anyone is interested). the small hole in the bottom, left of the backround is for wiring access for either a hidden temp probe, or flexwatt if i decide to use that for belly heat rather than a UTH, or both. a pic of it from the back of the tank:



*found a close up of the styrofoam carver:

another small ledge in front of the far left vent/arch (there are actually ledges all over from top to bottom):



anyway, here it is pretty much done:

i don't know if you can tell from the pic above, but the very bottom edge of the faux backround is very uniform and straight with no jutting edges. this is so i can try my concrete underlayment/grout substrate idea i mentioned a few posts ago.
on the top, between the two arches of the left and middle vents is the spoiled boy's name who will inhabit this terrerarium:
