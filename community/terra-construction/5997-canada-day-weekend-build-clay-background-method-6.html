Greg, I meant Calcium Bentonite substrates...My bad...And Edited

Also, I don’t mean to say that the substrate is solely made from clay...I believe the approximate amount is roughly 25-30% bentonite. I read that people let a large flat piece of clay dry up, and then they break it up and mix it into the substrate so it can degrade over time. As you can see from Reclycled's link, this fad has been going on for some time and there seems to be quite a lot of research put into it. I definitely don’t know much about it, and haven’t even gotten 1/4 of the way through that thread. But the reason the switch to clay based substrates is happening, is because dart enthusiasts are trying to mimic the natural environment from where they are naturally occuring. Clay based substrates may not be optimal for plant growth, but they help with sustaining substrate micro fauna and to replicate the natural environment. Because of this, many tanks don’t even have an abundance of plants rooted in the substrate. They are typically mounted epiphytically and then the substrate is solely covered in leaf litter. If you were to look at a jungle floor, it would look very similar to this and this is what everyone is trying to replicate. I’m not a fan of this “style” and don’t plan of setting up any of my vivs in this manner, but I can see why everyone is doing it. With my current and future tanks, I’ll be doing a mixture of the old and new ways of thinking. I’ll have sections of substrate covered in moss for aesthetics, and other sections covered with multiple layers of leaf litter to provide breeding ground for micro fauna. I think meeting in the middle will satisfy my own need for an aesthetically pleasing viv, and the darts needs at the same time.

We could probably go on about this for a long time, but I think it’s best to make another substrate thread if it is to continue since this is just a simple build thread

On that note, I have made very little progress on my tank....I’m still trying to make the grapevine water tight so that it doesn’t leak into my substrate area. It is really driving me up the wall haha. I picked up a fairly descent sized chunk of java moss last night, I am still planning on checking out some broms from a fellow frogger in town, and I am about to send a letter down to Florida so it can be sent back up to me full of magnolia leaves. Hopefully I can have it fully planted within 1-2 weeks. I’ll keep you guys posted on any visual progress as it happens!

And one more thing....I have a pyramid of clay sitting inside my viv. I’ll be letting it cure for another 1.5 days, then I will have water running over it to test how well this kitty litter clay mixture can withstand being constantly wet.

K quick update...

So apparently no one at the pet store, or 3 of the forums I am a member of mentioned that Grapevine is NOT recommended for vivaria. I’ve had the tank running the entire week, with regular misting and it already has 2 different types of mold blooming all over it. I boiled and baked it before adding it to the viv; it’s just that the wood does terribly when constantly moist.

So this weekend I will be exchanging that piece with a piece of Mopani, then I will be adding more vines, moss, and newly acquired broms into the viv.

I am also doing a wetness durability test using a large clump of clay that I let sit/cure inside my viv over the past 3 days. So far it is holding it's shape to a constant flow, however it has flaked a little bit and the outside has become very soft. Also, it seems that some of the peat is washing off of the clay and exposing some of the clay pellets that did not mix 100%. I'll be keeping it going overnight to see how well the rest holds up. As of right now, I do not suggest using this in constant contact with water.

I'll keep everyone updated as it comes along this weekend.
