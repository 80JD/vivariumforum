Pictures! Pictures! Pictures!

The below four pictures are of the stream bed covered in GreatStuff (GREAT STUFF? Pond & Stone). One thing I learned is that it expands way more than you might think. I did a trial run first on some left over Egg Crate and observed how much the foam expanded. However, when I applied it to the actual stream it seems like it faked me out on the test and expanded much more. I may need to cut some of it back to widen the stream a bit. Please let me know if any of you have cut it before, or have ideas on how to cut it while maintaining a natural look.











This picture is just me playing around with cutting the foam for the back wall. I wanted to experiment with different ways of cutting it before I moved on to the actual wall. I highly suggest anyone trying this method does the same.



Now for the real wall:

The first thing I did was to draw a general idea of how I wanted the top of the block to look in sharpie on the foam. I then traced out the general shape of the waterfall as it ran down the face of the block. I decided to tapper the waterfall so that it was widest at the top and gradually became more narrow as it reached the bottom. I also made sure that the waterfall started deep in the foam and slanted gentle outward as it went down to ensure that the water would run down the wall and not simply fly off. I cut the water fall out in small two inch pieces and then went back in to shape the edges and add more detail. One thing to be careful of is when you start cutting at the top where there is the least distance between the back of the water fall and the back of the foam is that you do not cut all the way through the foam. I almost did on several occasions bu got lucky.




















This is what I have so far. It takes a lot of time so be prepared to work on it over the course of several days. Also, if you are using a hot knife or other tool which melts through the foam make sure you are working in a place with good ventilation.

Let me know what you all think, and any questions you might have.
