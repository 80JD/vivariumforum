When using silicone, tape up the area around what you are siliconing with masking tape (or painters tape, whatever you want to call it) and dip your fingers in soapy water before smearing it so it won't stick to your fingers. You have to keep dipping your fingers in the soapy water to keep it off. As soon as you're done smearing, pull the tape up, it will leave a nice straight edge.

I got a tip regarding old terrariums:

If the bottom sheet of glass is cracked and silicone repair isn't an option, build a frame of wood in which you fasten pond liner or waterproff tarp cloth. Simple, cheap and effective way to waterproof your terrarium. Just make sure that the wooden planks used are pretty high, so that you can get in substrate of the required depth.

Another tip on substrates in terrariums is to have a layer of Ex-clay pellets in the bottom of the tank. Don't know if the material is popular in the US but here in Sweden it is commonplace, can be cheaply bought at a HD equivalent. What it does is prevent the substrate from sitting in water and rotting, something that you don't want to happen. If you are incorporating a falce bottom into your terrarium this isn't neccesary, but if you aren't i definetly reccomend it.
