janelc, where are you from that it is illegal to take (or even own?!?) milk crates? Some of the gas stations and stores back in SC (where I'm from) actually just throw them away. It is stealing if you don't ASK for them first, since they keep them in the same place to be picked up if they are recycling them (or sending back to the distributor) as they do if they are throwing them out. I have worked in gas stations that kept them, gas stations that sent them back, and gas stations that threw them out. I used to have quite a few of them, (they aren't JUST used for milk) but I only have a couple now due to giving them away.

As far as using the milk crates for the FB, I don't see why you wouldn't be able to do it. You could leave it square and cut it down shorter, just use a stronger mesh than you would if you were using the eggcrate louvers, since the holes are larger. I think you would actually have a sturdier construction with the milk crate, since you would have no need to add legs (unless you wanted to for extra support, which I doubt you'd need; I weigh over 250lbs, and I can stand on one with no problem), and the joints in the milk crate would be prefabricated. You would not be able to customize the shape as easily, but if you are going for a square land area and the milk crate fits into your tank the way you need it to, go for it! Run it for a week or so after you build it, BEFORE you add any animals just to make sure it is working out how you want it to. If you don't like it, or you have problems, just redo it with the eggcrate. This hobby is all about experimentation, as long as you are willing to redo it if it goes wrong, don't let anyone stop you from trying new things (as long as what you want to do is appropriate for the herps you are keeping). Take a look at my frog tank build if you want to see what I mean (that was HIGHLY experimental! lol).

I guess the best advice I can give is: be prepared to fail, and prepared to share your failure with others. You wont always fail, but you learn more from your own failure than you do from other people's instruction.

It's against the law to have a "dairy" stamped milk crate in your possession. In the state of California, it is a felony. People steal truckloads of milk crates and turn them into recyclers for regrinding of the plastic. The ground up plastic is shipped to China or Korea. I have known someone who went to prison for this. Dairy companies lose millions and millions of dollars in stolen milk crates every year. It is illegal in all states, but each state sets thier own fines and potential prison terms. Crazy, but true!

Those are fair sized tanks and are really worth putting the effort into because IMHO you will really enjoy them when they are complete. If you ask me about what to keep I'd say go for frogs. Frog care is basically the same as fish care except they will be the coolest "fish" ever.

Whatever you decide to do my advice would be to make up your mind on what kind of animal you'd want to keep first. I'd suggest doing the research on the frog you want so you will know what kind of setup (ie: materials) you'll need to build your viv. This is really important because you need to have a budget. With a tanks those sizes it can get pricey. I have similarly sized tanks and I set them up with small submersible filters at first. I found that it was alot of work to clean the filters every two weeks so what I did was drill the tanks. I drilled the bottom of both my 45g and 55g, then drained them to a sump underneath the stand. Trust me when I say it's a lot easier to drill out before you start than with an already established tank. It was worth it though, because now everything is pretty much automatic. I only open the tank to throw crickets or other food in.

For a pump you'd need to have an idea of the volume of water you intend to put in there. Just off the top of my head I'd say go to your local pet store that sells salt water fish and ask for pump for a saltwater tank. I'm assuming you want to use the pump to recirculate water from a water area in the tank to the top of a waterfall?
