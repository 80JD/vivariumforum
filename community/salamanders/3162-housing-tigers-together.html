48x12x18 should be enough for 3 Tiger salamanders of the same species.

However, I do not reccommend 3 animals of any kind housed together, as breeding pairs are normal to occur and males will, yes, WILL fight for female attention. This being said, if you plan on housing 3 Salamanders together, get 1 male and 2 females.. Fighting may still occur, I would just watch them.

Do not mix species unless you are aware of the consequences. These salamanders are aggressive when it comes to hunting for food and will bite eachother's limbs, so I would stick with the same species.

As for Tiger salamander set ups, just use Google. No need in being lazy asking other people to do the research work you should put in yourself.


Cage temperature: 65-70F, should not rise above 75F or drop below 60F (For Tiger Salamanders and all other Salamanders), Larva should be kept at the same Temperatures, but with only about 6inches of water
Humidity Levels: 70% and higher, not to extend 80% for air flow (Be sure there is no stagnant air, possibly installing a fan for circulation)
Feeding: Crickets every other day (Or Earthworms), 5-6 a feeding, twice a week for an adult Salamander (Do not leave extra feeding items in the Aquarium)
Substrate extras to Avoid: Perlite, vermiculite, small pieces of bark, or gravel, and be sure to check the substrate PH level, that it is not too low! (6.5)
*Note: Do not get any Silicone items that contain “Mold Inhibitor” and a 5% bleach solution is okay to clean enclosures if it is completely washed off.

We keep four tiger salamanders together with no problems at the museum I intern at. There is about 6sqft floor space in their enclosure so they have plenty of room, but 90% of the time they hang out in a little pack a couple inches away from each other. they also tend to sleep together. They have all been housed together for over five years. they are fed separately so there is no competition for food. oh and there are 2 males and 2 females. I say go for it from my experience they have proven to be very social animals that do very well together.
