As I understand it, the orange and yellow colors can come from the same population. In other words, you can have two yellow parents who could produce some orange offspring and visa versa. I'm no geneticist, so I don't know if it's a recessive trait or what; however, there are different Leuc morphs that you don't want to mix. All dart frogs are from Central and South America. There might be poisonous frogs from New Guinea, but not what we typically call dart frogs.

The standard Leuc morph is pretty common and inexpensive and, as I said, can be yellow or orange. There is a 'British Guyana' morph that is more expensive and has a different, more banded, pattern. This morph also varies in background color from yellow to orange. Most people advise not to mix these with the standards, while others say that they are not from different wild populations and were artificially created by selective breeding. Anyway, it's fine to put Leucs together that have orange and yellow.

You might have to buy frogs from two different sources if you want to start out with some orange and some yellow. But, for example, Patrick Nabors at Saurian Enterprises sometimes sells sexed pairs and he provided photos of the pair he is offering. Out of the same group of frogs, he'll get orange and yellow. Unfortunately, he told me recently that his Leucs aren't cooperating because I was trying to get a sexed pair. But, the point is that you could find a breeder that has both or you might have to contact more than one breeder to get yellow and orange frogs. It just depends on what they have in stock. Also, their site photo might show the yellow variety, but what they actually have in stock might be yellow and/or orange.

The red pigment in their skin that causes the yellow to look more orange can be affected by diet. I've seen pictures of Leucs that are intensely and unnaturally orange because of dietary supplements containing red pigment.

So, if they get no red pigment in their diet, I suppose the orange could fade to yellow. However, the reverse might also be true. If you over-supplement with pigments, your yellow frogs might start turning orange.

This is simply information I have gained from reading and not from personal experience, so take it with a grain of salt and do some researching to confirm it.

I know that I was wrong about the British Guyana coming in both colors. I didn't realize they did not come in a yellow form.
