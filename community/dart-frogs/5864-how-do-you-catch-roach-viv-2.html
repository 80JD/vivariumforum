not trying to either.
small critters in tight places without chemicals in general is not very fun. i'd hate to be in your shoes attempting to work around thumbnails.

i think here the issue is to build a better trap... that's safe. how do you think they'd fare trapped in with the roach? do you think you could properly identify the roach species? do you think the roach could get in where the frogs can't?

roaches definitely like carrots and oranges. you can use that against them. if that worries you with your frogs you can blend up other fruits/veggies with them to a paste and use that.

some roaches can climb smooth plastic. if your guy can't and he's small enough a 20oz bottle might be fine. if he's a little bigger you'll need a wider bottle so it doesn't just reach to the outside and pull out. if it's a climber you'll need something like ky jelly, vaseline, etc. to make the surface inside too slick for it to escape.

if you can swing it without soaking it you could probably use egg cartons stacked on top of each other to provide security for it like a home it would seek out naturally and just check it every now and then replacing anything that's too wet. when you lift up the egg carton, if it's in there it probably won't want to make a jump for it. it should hold on for dear life actually. that should make it easier to carry away. the texture on egg cartons is perfect for them to hold on to unlike tissue rolls where there's a chance it could fall out. you could probably use foam pieces of egg carton on the top and bottom to avoid extra saturation from viv conditions/misters/etc. too. as long as it can reach the paper portion it'll probably find it an inviting place. a little food in there surely would help your chances i'd think.
