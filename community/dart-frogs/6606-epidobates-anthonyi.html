beta carotene (which is processed into vitamin a) can be toxic even at levels used in regular vitamin supplements if used too often for herps. search vitamin a toxicity or carotenemia for more information on that.

repashy foods suggests that no more than 6% of the total supplement diet should consist of superpig. as well repashy supplements contain up to 3% by volume already... so with the right supplements you should not need more than another 3% total. cgd for example already contains the aforementioned 3%..

beings superpig consists of only color enhancers i seriously doubt it's necessary for a healthy herp. ingredients consist of Carotenoids, Astaxanthin and xanthophyll which are simply red, orange and yellow color enhancers.

full description:
"The ultimate pigment enhancing blend from the finest, and most concentrated natural sources! If you want to bring out the color in your reptile, amphibian, or fish, give this a try!. Spirulina pacifica algae for full spectrum Beta Carotene (Carotenoids) NatuRose® brand of Haematococcus algae for Red and Orange Pigments (Astaxanthin) Marigold Extract for natural yellow pigmenters (xanthophyll) SuperPig contains equal amounts of these finest pigment enhancing products. No fillers, just a pure blend! "

quoted from: Repashy Ventures - Retail Store - Superfoods :: Products :: Supplements :: Pigment Supplements :: SuperPig

betta carotene is a vitamin (or is at least processed into one once ingested)... vitamin a... so it is a vitamin mix... but one that if overused can cause toxicity. hence the term "most concentrated".

i, personally would be using rep-cal herptivite once in a while and rep-cal calcium pretty often (maybe every other feed)... maybe adding superpig once monthly... or mixing it with my herptivite at 3% by weight. should be more than enough to bring out any color without risking toxicity.

Speaking of interesting studies - I borrowed "Poison Frogs" by Lotters, Jungfer, Henkel, and Schmidt to refresh my memory on husbandry and found this interesting bit about the toxins of E. Anthonyi (which up until recently were classified as tricolor)

"This frog (as E. tricolor) attracted international attention when it was discovered to contain an alkaloid that was later named epibatidine. The history of the discovery of this substance goes back to 1977 when two American scientists collected this species in Ecuador and detected an alkaloid fraction with an opiate-like action in its skin. This had one of the scientists return to Ecuador in the following year to collect more specimens. He returned with some 750 of them from which about 60 mg of alkaloids could be extracted. This amount then yielded about 750 (ug) of the sought after alkaloid fraction. However, it then took until the late 1980s for analysis equipment and methods to become advanced and refined enough to map the chemical structure of the substance; in fact it was not before 1992 that this process was eventually completed. Epidbatidine turned out to have an extreme pain-killing action, and is today produced synthetically. Owing to its toxicity it was never used as medication, but rather in neuronal cell research. Meanwhile the molecule has been modified artificially to that it is less toxic than the original epibatidine, but still remains many times more effective than morphine. Its great advantage over morphine is that the substance, called ABT-594, is apparently not addictive. This caused a global stir, but also led to political arguments. If the US based pharmaceutical company Abbot decides to introduce the painkiller to the market within the next years, its market potential is estimated to be in the region of $40 billion. This is a volume that Ecuador, understandably, wants to be a part of. After all, it is argued, it is a genetic resource of that country, protected by convention on biodiversity and the UN resolution 391 of 1996. Observers reckon that chances are slim for Ecuador to be sharing the spoils for this reason, though, because of the US not having ratified the convention on biodiversity. Even the argument that the 750 frogs collected in 1977 were exported from Ecuador illegally is of judicial concern because the ministry that was entrusted with affairs of this nature at the time today has no relevant records anymore."

Interesting stuff!
