You would have to get pinhead crickets, they can be difficult to source in small quantities. You would have to get small quantities unless you are feeding something that needs larger ones because they will grow too quickly for the darts to eat them all. If you cant provide a steady supply of flies you are in trouble. Regardless of how big the dart looks it eats tiny food. You might as well dismiss any preconceptions you have about feeding frogs, because 98 % of the time it will be to big for darts. You can use a variety of foods,(in fact its recommended) but all of the alternatives will be more work than fruit flies. Bean Beatles, termites and isopods along with pin head crickets are standard alternatives used to supplement pdf's diet.

I don't know how many times I have driven to pick up Pin heads and find that stores idea of a pinhead is a 1/4 cricket. for perspective your looking for feeders that are in most cases 1/16 - 1/8 inch Max.

Oh, I am all for variety! It is the spice of life! My auratus trio eat plenty of melanogaster ff's, hydei ff's, pinhead crickets and newborn roaches (blatta lateralis). And I'm sure they pick off some of the springtails living in their tank too But the fruit flies compose the bulk of their diet by far, and they are very healthy and breed like rabbits!

Now for stinky fruit fly cultures... As previously stated, it's a matter of starting with a healthy colony and using a good media to culture them. I've been using the medium from Josh's frogs for a couple years now and I highly recommend it. It doesn't stink, and produces lots of robust flies quickly. I've tried MANY others, both at home and at work, and it seems to be the best pre-made mix by far. And if you use canning jars and plastic crafting mesh (instead of flimsy plastic containers and excelsior) you can re-use them indefinitely and the only thing you'll have to buy again is the media! (unless your cultures crash... which can happen... but that's another story... and it doesn't happen very often.... but I digress...)
