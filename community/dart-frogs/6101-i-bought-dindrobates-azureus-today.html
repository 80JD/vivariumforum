Dendrobates arent exactly a "spur of the moment" purchase to be made. Typically a home should already be built, and let establish for a few months before a single frog is bought. The temps, humidity, and microfauna need to be established also. It is also recommended that several colonies of ff's or springtails should be kept 2-3 months prior to ensure there will always be a constant food source since ff's are harder to purchase in stores.

Do you have a viv ready? Does it have the essential items needed in a dart tank? (leaf litter, plants, microfauna, hides, ect...) A picture of the enclosure would be the best thing to post so I can give you advice on what you should add/remove.

Luckily for you it is a faily hardy species and one of the easier species of darts to care for....In the mean time...Here you go

The fruit flies are a good food source. However some people typically have springtails and woodlice that are introduced into the substrate. They help with tank maintenance and act as additional snacks for your frog. They are especially important when trying to feed froglets since they require much smaller food. If a good layer of decomposing leaf litter is present, the micro fauna will be almost self sustaining. Given that you only have one froglet, it is safe to say their populations would bloom.

I’m fairly certain keeping your froglet in quarantine isn’t necessary unless you are worried about contaminating your enclosure, or more importantly, if you are planning to purchase additional azureus froglets. Typically they are kept in simple, easy to clean enclosures in case they carry disease or parasites. If a froglet dies from unknown causes, the tank can then be torn down and have its contents replaced with minimal effort and cost. Quarantine tanks are similar to grow-out tanks for froglets. They have micro fauna rich soil, tons of leaf litter at various stages of decomposition, and a few hides. Live plants aren’t usually added, however cutting are sometimes used to spruce the tank up a bit.

I would forgo the quarantine if you see the frog actively eating. You do however need to start working on the fruit flies, You will be making a new one a minimum of every two weeks. I would also discourage the pin head crickets, these frogs eat tiny food items even as adults. My azureus morph out almost as big as a dime, so this guy is very young and young frogs are not very hardy. READ THE CARE SHEETS.

The reason I discourage pin heads is that they are a unreliable feeder in most cases, day old crickets are fine but they grow quickly. In most cases they are also difficult to find reliably. There have also been posts about those pin heads hiding in the tanks and eating plants and even nibbling on frogs from time to time. I suggest you get some experience with culturing fruit flies as they will be generally the most reliable food source. I would use the pin heads as a supplemental feeder for variation in its diet.
