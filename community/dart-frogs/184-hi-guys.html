Hey,

Welcome to Vivarium Forums
Okay, you can grow all sorts of tropical plants in PDF(Poison Dart Frog) enclosures. Some common vines include species of Pilea, Philodendron, Dischidea, etc... There are many.
Moss, I myself grow what might be Java Moss, and tropical moss. Both do well under PDF conditions. The trick with moss is lighting.
Humidity, PDF's like 80-100% humidity! Anything less will put your froggy in danger.
Temps, most PDF's like it between 65 and 80. D. pumilio, among a few others I believe, like it between 65-85ish.
Now, I believe that the best begginner arboreal PDF is D. pumilio. The drawback to that is that they are expensive. Then again, they are probably the least expensive of the arboreal species, and the largest of them.
Arboreal species include, D. pumilio, D. intermedius, D. fantasticus, D. imitator, D. reticulatus, and D. variabilis.
Keeping these species you need to grow bromiliads in your vivarium, which these PDF's use as homes and breeding sites.
Now for the most important aspect of keeping PDF's. You must learn how to culture your own food for them. They eat small fruit flies, and springtails. This is the staple diet of all PDF's. They will not eat crickets unless you get true pinheads, and or you have a really big terrestrial PDF like Phyllobates terribilis, or D. galactonotus.
So, your best bet is to build the viv, plant it. Seed it with springtails and other microfauna, learn how to keep and maintain a steady stream of fruit fly cultures, and then choose the best PDF you like.
Really, once you are comfortable that you can provide the correct food, and temps, and home for whatever species you want, you can get any PDF.

very nice planted tank. what are the dimensions of it? it looks huge!!

i think Tindomul has your Qs answered the same way i would. i would only add D. ventrimaculatus to the good beginner thumbnails. they are a little more reclcusive than some of the other species, but are proven hardy and fairly easy to breed. they use vertical and horzintal space.

for a little larger frog the D. leucomelas could fit your bill. they are an attractive bold species that are often reccomended as one of the best beginner PDFs of any size. they are readily available, fairly cheap (in PDF terms ), hardy eaters, relatively easy to breed, and they like to use vertical space as well.

also, depending on how your tank is desigend, you could make a very tall tank accesible for all frogs. in our small tanks (compared to nature) i doubt we create anything taller than the most terrestial frogs venture up into in the wild. of course you wouldnt want to put a really big terrestial frog in there and expect them to be able to climb to the top on small elafed vines, but a terraced layout with legdes, and thick leafed strong plants, could get even tincs to the top happily. depth front to back helps tremendously in achieving this. thing abot that if you end up building your own tank. every inch you can get front to back will be a big help in decorating the tank.

definatly take the advice to start culturing food items ahead of time. its one of the most challenging aspects of keeping these little gems.

good luck!!
