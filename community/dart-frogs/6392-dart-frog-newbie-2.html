Hi Judy S,

As far as which frogs to start with I would recommend leucomelas. They are not only colorful, they are hardy and can withstand environmental changes easily. The males have a nice call and they are quite bold meaning they will be out most of the time. They can also be kept in groups depending on the size of the viv. Definetly a great beginner frog.

As far as the temp/humidity the viv I have my Leucs in does not have a false bottom rather pea gravel with mosses on top (by the way, a 20lb bag of pea gravel from your local garden store will cost you about 5 bucks, the same stone is sold at aquarium shops for 20 times as much). I have a pond area hidden in the back of the tank with an ultrasonic fogger to help add humidity. In addition to this I have a couple of computer type fans that not only help with air circulation but does a nice job of lowering the overall temp. Both fogger and fans are on timers that cycle on multiple times throughout the day. Without getting into expensive vivarium programable control systems this is alot cheaper. The only drawback is it will take some experimenting with the timing prior to introducing your frogs. It is also important to know that although the ultrasonic foggers are promoted as "cool misting" systems they will heat the water if left on too long.

Hope this helps.

I have a fogger that operates outside the vivarium and transfers the fog to the tank via a hose. The brand escapes me at the moment.

Great frogs to start with are any of the Dendrobates Tinctorius (tincs)morphs. I started with the blue morph. You could also try one of the D. Auratus morphs or D. Leucomelas (leucs). You have to be careful with tincs, though because the females, in particular, are very territorial and will fight and eat one another's eggs. The dominant female can so intimidate the other that it can die from hunger and depressed immune system. They are best kept in pairs. I have a pair of Auratus in a 29-gal tank and they are doing great. I understand that they aren't quite as territorial as tincs, but it is still best to keep a pair. Of the three, leucs seem to be the best suited to keep in groups if you have a larger vivarium.

I love my blue tincs (azureus). They are bold and out in the open most of the day. They also breed readily.
