magnolia leaves are great.. a pretty good sterilization method-

1) make sure you're collecting from an area where pesticides aren't used.
2) rinse
3) let the leaves dry out (not just from the rinse... let them get brown and crunchy)
4) rinse again
5) place in a pot of boiling water for 3 minutes or so
6) dry 'em, and you're good to go.

Make sure you let them dry fully, this takes care of chytrid, and the boiling will take care of most of the bacterial baddies. There are very few things that can live through both of these processes.. this is the same method that many dart frog supply vendors use with their leaves.

I struggled with the perfect substrate for my first and only dart frog vivarium. I decided to do the following (I'm no expert but it has been working for me):
1. about 4 parts coco fiber
2. about 1 part non fertilized top soil
3. crunch up a couple large hand fulls of dried oak tree leaves (yes, the ones from the yard)
That's it! Not sure how everybody else on this forum feels but it has worked for me. I'm growing: Java Moss, Baby Tears, Prayer Plant, Heart Leaf Philodendrom and Oak Leaf Creeping Fig. ( Those are pretty easy plants to grow)
Springtails are keeping themselves alive and abundant in the vivarium as a supplemental food source for dendrobate dart frogs.
I'm also only using 1 T-8 flourescent (sp) tube light (another no-no. I plan to switch to a double T-5 fixture in the future but the single T-8 is keeping everything looking green without too much stretching. I want to switch to a double T-5 in hopes of bringing out the color in 3 neo bromeliads growing epithetically (again, probably spelled wrong) on wood in the viv)
I would suggest using a false bottom with egg crate ( flourscent light diffuser - bought at Menards) and fiber glass screen (bought from Menards). I used 2" PVC pipe to lift the false bottom from the bottom of the 29 gal glass aquarium. I like the 2" lift because I can hand mist the tank at least once a day and only have to remove the water from the bottom about once every 2 months (the water is great fertilizer for other house plants).
I know I'm getting a little long winded, especially coming from a "New Guy" but it has been working for me. Hope this helps
