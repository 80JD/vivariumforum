if you're talking about a diy spraybar intake...

since the intake is through the pvc i'm saying wrap that with course media held on by your stocking instead of having media inside of it. more surface area (circumference) = better flow. intead of restricting it you'll be more like dampening/baffling it on the outside. don't worry if the media isn't right up against the holes either cause that can cause it to clog faster.

if not you can always build a pump box out of media also.

you may need more holes in your pvc, idk. i'm assuming when you say 90* you mean a slow curve, not a bend? that would be better on the ouput imo. i guess if it's necessary you could make due.

the danger in breaking glass is held within a sudden stop of movement and a sudden shrinkage. if we can do our best to reduce those things from happening we can keep glass from breaking. now, you can't keep that from happening if there's gaps, the heat necessary isn't dispersed properly over the right distance, etc.. if you ever look at ambient heat, it pulsates... the hotter, the larger and faster. the reason being is because hotter things move faster. this can be air, electrons, atoms, flames... whatever.

so, we have to use what we know works to dissipate heat appropriately for the temperature swing we need to invoke. with a coffee pot i would use hot tap water in the pot so as to not cool down the pot too quickly and unevenly. once the pot is overflowing i slowly turn the temperature down on the water giving it a nice even ambient gradient to work with. in our cases we need that glass hot... sometimes there's no way around it so this train of thought comes more from a frying pan. we need either a material that's worthy of transfer heat well and even, air pockets to reduce contact so as to reduce the speed at which changes occur or a combination of both. one thing to take into concern is that you wind up with something stable too.

sooo, i like flexwatt for this and i'll use it as an example. say i was keeping the beardie in the flatback hex. i'd cover 3/4 of the bottom with flexwatt with silicone zig zags from end to end to get most of the floor between 75-90* with a small area without. this would take 2 panels back to back at different temps (hot one below the heat lamp). i'd tape them end to end with foil tape and foil tape all the way around it with tape all the way to the end of the glass on the hot side to make a perfect air pocket where air couldn't escape with enough tape to hold ambient heat so the glass isn't radiating/fluctuating so much.

since the silicone doesn't transfer heat well all the heat will come through air which will move freely and quickly even itself out. the fact that it closed keeps it from siphoning cold air for the quick shrink. the foil radiates the heat and covers the other side of the glass from cold air keeping the quick fluctuations almost purely inside the tape.

a side benefit from this whole situation is since heat rises and we're not losing any it's more efficient also. we're getting more than 50% of the heat output of the pad applied. that's more than i can say for a lick em' stick em' store bought model that might shatter you're glass when the glue starts dieing.

i'm breaking this down for you so you can use it better. it surely could be applied to other materials and i'm sure you have something in mind already.
