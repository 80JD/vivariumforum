there are viable options but i suspect they'd all be more work than just scooping out the crickets. for example: one could use just about any freshwater shrimp, crab or crayfish, etc.. the crays are usually too slow for many fish but there are lots of exceptions to the rule and eventually the sneaky crayfish is likely to get most, if not all of them anyway. crabs like thai micros would likely not finish the meal before the water is fouled leaving you to do water changes often. larger crabs... same fate as the crays but most likely brackish anyway. shrimp... same consequences as the thai's. i simply think that even though 40 gallons is a decent size tank for fish to have a nice community in there to have something that's going to sufficiently munch on crickets you'd need something at least twice the size to accommodate larger, more boisterous, active and aggressive fish that could deal with such a creature. in essence you'd actually be better off getting your fish to do such work since the crickets would hit the top first and most likely float or slowly sink. that would ensure that they don't rot slowly at the bottom of the water section unnoticed. at that point you'd have a choice of less fish (not exactly community possibly), a bigger setup or another option. i think the best option is to keep a net by the food or tank itself.

Originally Posted by jbreczuk

The problem is, more than likely ( depending on the particular species of catfish ), you already have predators that will eat anything smaller than themselves ( and sometimes not even much smaller ) - cories are about the only catfish species that I would trust to be relatively harmless to most other species.

Crayfish and crab's will even munch on your terrestrials, and at times when your terrestrials are sleeping, it doesn't matter how slow crays and crabs are.

The only shrimp that would be safe with your other inhabitants, are to small to do much about crickets ( unless they are pin-head sized ).

Have you looked into butterfly fish? They will definitely eat crickets that fall into the water, though you may have to change up the fish you plan to keep. (I'm pretty sure they harass -- if not eat -- small tetras, but it's been ages since I kept one, so it might be worth further research.)

Don't underestimate the tetras, either. While they won't eat a whole live cricket at once, once it drowns, they'll pick at it if they're hungry, and they may even be able to keep the tank clean for you, especially if you have some loaches/cats or other bottom feeders that will help with the sinking debris. Schools of kuhli loaches can be quite active and fun to watch.
