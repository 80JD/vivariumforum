There's lots you can do on a limited budget, and you can make a tank that basically takes care of itself. I have a ten gallon with two or three guppies and my rough skinned newt, and I rarely even feed her (she eats the snails and whatnot). All I have to do in that tank is trim the grass that grows from the top.

The amount of money you spend depends on your creativity when choosing supplies as well as how well you hunt our deals and freebies.

Wood is one of those things that can generally be had for free, you just have to take the time to make it suitable for your tank (sterilization, etc).
If your dad has eggcrate left over, you can always use that so you don't have to buy any yourself.
Waterfall pumps are much less expensive than herp (or even fish) specific ones, so definitely price those. Of course, if you have a filter or pump laying around somewhere, you can always make that work as well.
If you want to carve styrofoam to make rocks and whatnot, it can be had for free from a plethora of places. Try retail stores first (you might even be able to find open cell foam you can use for filtering your water or planting moss), especially the ones that carry china and electronics.
As far as plants go, the cheapest route is to make a native paludarium, because then you can just mimic what's outside and gather your plants. Otherwise, buy your plants small and let them grow into the tank. Many tropical sp. can be found in 2" or 4" pots for a dollar or two.

As for animals, you need to decide that before you start building the tank. Believe me, it's the best way to go.The first thing you decide is the climate you want to replicate, then pick an animal that will work, THEN start designing and building.
