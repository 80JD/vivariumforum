New here, already have questions
First, I just wanted to say I've spent hours on this site, looking at every thread I could find, and I want to extend my feelings of astonishment at the true works of art that have been created. I can only hope to one day be able to create my own enclosure with a fraction of the realism as can be seen in almost every example on these pages. And on that note, I have a few questions as to how to construct a paludarium -

First, I'm very interested in creating an enclosure designed to have a central "island" as the land mass, and I was trying to determine the best way to go about constructing it. My first idea is to take nature's course, and just pile a number of large rocks and pieces of wood together in an aesthetically-pleasing manner, while also creating an area around the top that could be filled with substrate and planted. My concern though is that this type of natural construction wouldn't allow for a solid enough foundation to hold the substrate and plants. Am I correct in this feeling?

My next thought as to how to construct this center island would be to use the oft-referred GS foam, with smaller rocks and wood pieces implanted in it. This would allow for a much better platform for substrate, but I have two main concerns with this method; The first being that it will be an "unnatural" construction (mostly my own personal apprehension to having a tank that appears artificial), and that the GS might not be water-proof and begin to mold or rot after a time being in the water. If anyone could adress this concern, I might be more inclined to take this route.

My last concern has to do with the inhabitants themselves. I would like nothing more than to maintain a small group of dart frogs in this island tank, but I am worried that the depth of the water might pose hazardous to the animals should one decide to venture a bit too close to shore. Obviously the island will be constructed with several points of easy access (branches gently sloping into water, plants in contact, etc.) as to allow easy access to the animals, but would dart frogs even be able to sustain swimming to be able to reach these points? The last thing I want is to put an animal in a position where it would not have the best chance at survival.

Any and all help is greatly appreciated, thank you in advance!

Yeah I agree. Non of the posted enclosure would be suitable for any darts. They are all paludariums, and not intended to house land based critters. Plus most of the food would be lost due to drowing, instead of inside your frog's tummy.

Typically a dart enclosure will have only a small water feature, with multiple points of entry/exit. Some of the more experienced dart keepers even have said that if they were to rebuild their vivs, that the water feature would only have 1 side exposed to glass (meaning the 3 other sides could be used as exits) Take a look at this video from one of the more experience dart keepers on Dendroboards....At the 50 second mark you can see how a dart will continue to swim up against the glass after it falls in.

Yes that would be better. Keep in mind of the natural setting where darts are found though. Thick layer of leaf litter, lots of plants, and plenty of microfauna. Only for a certain few dart species do they actually benefit from a running water feature, or feature at all. I believe A. Bassleri is one of the species that does.

Also keep in mind that lots of microfauna is a good way to vary a dart's diet, all while acting as your viv’s full time "maid" service. To help keep microfauna thriving, lots of substrate and leaf litter would be needed. Hard to do in a palu, even if the water is shallow as you suggested. I water feature is definitely a good thing to have, plus its esthetically pleasing, just keep the size in moderation. Even inch taken up by the feature, decreases the usable space of the frogs.
