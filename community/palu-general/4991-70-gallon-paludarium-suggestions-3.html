my answer to #:
1... just for show really... it adds depth and the wall behind it doesn't detract from the overall look afterward. a dark background, different color paint or tint, or a sheet of dark paper that covers it all would work. if you go with a cover of sorts instead of tint or paint i suggest using some cooking oil or vaseline to hold it up... just smear it on the glass and press the b/g cover on then smooth out the bubbles.

2... remember the cork would just be for hiding pvc, not for support

3... the turtle would eat the fish, snails and shrimp. that's the idea behind letting them breed for a while and getting cheaper aquatic pets that could easily be found on many fish forums in good health. besides... if you get a musk turtle it'll likely be just bigger than your thumb at first so it'll take a bit to wipe out 4 breeding colonies. when it does i'd just get more. they'll make wonderful treats for him anyways.

red cherries should go for about $1 ea... guppies... close to the same... mts... anywhere from 10/$1 to free with another order... ramshorns... about the same

i'd want about 20 rcs, 20 mts, 20 ramshorns and 3-5 FEMALE guppies
4... yes, the big one

5... sand would look better and accentuate the little bit of light you'll get down there making it brighter. pool filter sand is great. regular colored play sand is fine but will need severe cleaning first... when my local home depot has it back in for the season i'm getting a few bags for my tanks though.

6... rhizome... the leafs grow up out of it... the roots grow down out of it... it looks like a little green turd in the middle of the plant

7-9... i'm not too good with plants but... cryptocorynes, swords, vals, mosses, anubias, coral moss, java ferns, etc.

12... i'm not too good with plants, lol

15... compact fluorescent lamps... spiral replacement bulbs... ge makes a daylight one that's pretty good most people can find at walmart.

16... 20



i think peat should definitely be used at the top since at least 1/2 the land will basically be bog area. i could be completely wrong though.

i don't think a disco ball would help the plants. if you want one and can pull it off go for it.

Quote:

5. The only sand I have found is the white sand used in the saltwater tanks. What are some good kinds of sand ? Why is sand better ?
Quote:
Originally Posted by beckdg
5... sand would look better and accentuate the little bit of light you'll get down there making it brighter. pool filter sand is great. regular colored play sand is fine but will need severe cleaning first... when my local home depot has it back in for the season i'm getting a few bags for my tanks though.
The sand I just put in my fish tank is the same I use for my beardie and it looks just wonderful in the aquarium. It should be wasy to find as it is a national brand and is inexpensive. It's the Quikrete Medium Sand, available at Home Depot. It comes in a brown paper package and has green trim. The sand is white, and would actually help reflect the light in the bottom of the tank, making better use of what you have available. This said is washed, kiln dried, and screened. It's inert, so no worries about it screwing up the tank parameters. This stuff is clean enough that it barely clouds the water even if you stir it up considerably (I missed the plate a few times and stirred it up quite a lot, but it never clouded the water more than a slight milkiness). It seems like it was less than $10 for 100lbs of it, a totally great deal. I always thought that dark substrate would look better, but the white looks AMAZING. I have a thread with some crappy pics of the tank in the Off-Topic forum if you want to see how the substrate looks.

Now, an idea for lighting the underside of the land portion... Have you seen those new under counter LED light strips? They have some that are VERY small. My suggestion would be to get either one long enough to go across the tank, or enough of them to link together to go across, then make a black housing for them (so they won't look so bad) and mount them to the front of the tank right under the water line. This way the light will shine into the tank without shining in your eyes when you look at it, and it will open your options for plants under the land portion. Also, the LED lights are very efficient, so there will be little power consumption. Also, if you wanted to maximize the light output, you could mount a narrow mirror under the false bottom to reflect more light. Placement would depend on the shape of the beam of light produced by the LEDs.
