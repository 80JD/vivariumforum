I don't have alot of time ATM to address everything, but i will say that the best way to get rid of a film it to skim it off the water with a cup (hold the cup under the water and allow a small stream to go into the cup, siphoning off the film), this can be from leaching expanding foam, protein buildup, even the substrate (sometimes tiny tiny particles settle on top of the water and are too small to sink, causing them to float on the surface and clump together, causing a skim). generally, changing the water will not fix the problem, because as the water level drops, the film will stick to the glass and cage decorations, rather than coming out in the water.

Ok, I guess I DO have time, lol...

1. From what I understand, fungi don't take in CO2 and put out oxygen like plants do, they take in oxygen and put out CO2 like animals do, so I don't think limited oxygen would be the issue with the mold. However, stagnant air may well be. The mold is "eating" the top decaying layer from the wood. If you add a fan to circulate air, this will help get rid of the mold (at least what's on top that you can see). The springtails will get rid of what's left.

2. springtails are great, you WANT them in there. They are a necessary part of the ecosystem.

3. I addressed this in my last post. Skim as much of it off as you can, then spray down the sides of the glass and the edges of the wood and anywhere else the film can get stuck, the skim it again. DO this until you don't see any more of it. It's tedious and can take a long time, many rounds of flushing and skimming, but it's the best way to do it. I spent a good two hours at least doing it yesterday in my palu... What a pain, literally... I had to stand on a step stool the entire time because the tank is on my kitchen counter.
