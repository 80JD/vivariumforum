Curiosity with idea
So, I've been fascinated with all types of vivariums ever since I did a project in grade school years ago with a paludarium inside of a 3 liter bottle.. Anyway, I've been dreaming of the "perfect" paludarium ever since. I've always wanted a fully closed one, but can only do that if I use shrimp in the water, lol, but that's no fun...

So, I've been planning on this one for a while, it will be semi-closed, so, closed, with a lid or opening so I can go in and fix/clean/feed.

I wanted it in a long case, such as a 30 gallon or more and in different layers. Most bottom layer will be a mix of rocks and carbon filter rocks and the top will be a mix of sand and Eco Earth coconut fiber substrate with a little bit of moss on top.

There will be a water fall in the corner emptying into a small creek that goes down into a pool of water on the opposite side of the case. The water will travel under the rock/carbon rock mix on the bottom and go back into the waterfall.

In the pool of water will be a small air wand blowing oxygen into the water to keep oxygen in the water and the tank to help the water fall keep the water from going stale.

Now, I know this may sound nice to certain people and you may be asking why I would use those materials, such as the carbon and substrate.. well, I want to put hermit crabs in it.

Now, I KNOW that hermit crabs are messy. They like to tip things over all the time, dig, etc, so the paludarium may not look so beautiful for too longer, but that's why I was going to make it sub-closed since it seems sorta obvious.

However, my main reason for this post was... I wanted to ask what other people thought of, not only my idea for the paludarium, but if I should add hermies or not.

I already know how to take care of hermies since my girlfriend has three. She doesn't take care of them that well so I'm always giving them water, feeding them, cleaning up their cage and misting it because she leaves the substrate dry. We don't have to worry too much about humidity because of where we live. It's always humid here, lol. She's also in a room that was converted from a garage, so it's usually warm and humid anyway.

So what does everybody think? Sorry if I typed too much, lol, just wanted to get my point through and all that jazz.

then here's what i would do to make your idea work...

have you seen the "european vivs" (the case style) where the f/b is built right into the glass or plexi? something similar comes to mind. my guys dig at least 6" to the bottom and then i can hear their little claws/legs tapping at the bottom glass trying to go further. because of that and the fact that they tore through fiberglass screening in a couple days and had my leca at the top of my soil i would not attempt to layer my soil. i'm pretty sure that idea will prove useless fast and who wants to put in all that effort and then do it over next week.

sooo... i'd build my pump box to go straight up to the lid and give plenty of space for me to get in there. then (assuming your case will be glass) i'd fit a piece of glass around that and silicone it to the surrounding walls leaning slightly towards your pond for run off purposes. if you still want some carbon at this point you can put a little in a filter media bag and drop it in your pump box where the water will flow through it, it can be replaced and it'll be out of view.

i would not try to layer my soil at all... so coco fiber will do just fine. i'd personally get it at a hydroponics store. you'll get more for your money and there's a chance it'll be better quality too.

your waterfall can be built right into your pump box if you'd like and your stream could easily go from there without an issue. i might be inclined to make a ledge all the way around the pump box to act as a reservoir and catch all the water to feed the stream. a dripper manifold could be split up with 5 hoses on one side/4 on the other/etc. to give you the flow you want yet still keep the splashing to a minimum so as to not soak your soil.

i see no reason we can't have plants with our hermies... we'd just have to be careful in selection and give plenty of time for them to take root. for instance a strong creeping vine would probably do good creeping up a b/g and some sturdy epiphytes such as some anubias bartieri grown terrestrially could be attached to wood used for a hide or climbing surface. anything "potted" i don't think would work for too long though for obvious reasons.

at any rate, i don't want to get too far ahead of myself unless i know you're understanding me. sorry for the long post, but you started it.
