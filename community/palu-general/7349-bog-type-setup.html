If I understand you correctly, you want to study aggression in the animals. Do you mean aggression between individuals of the same species (i.e., intraspecific aggression) or aggression between individuals of different species (i.e., interspecifc aggression)?

Either way, you'll have to do some research to see what animals would be appropriate for your study. Some species are naturally more aggressive than others, so if you picked one that was non-aggressive, you wouldn't get much action. (Siamese fighting fishes are famous for their aggression, and a lot of research has been done with them for this reason.)

Also, you'll have to try to figure out what causes the aggression, for instance, competition for space (territoriality), competition for food, competition for mates, etc. If you know what causes the aggression, then you can manipulate it as an experimental variable. For example, if it is competition for food, you could see how one vs. two food sources affected it, if at all.

Additionally, you'll want a way to quantify the amount of aggression. What if they do it at night, when you can't see them? (Makes me think you shouldn't work with nocturnal species.)

It seems like it could be an interesting project, and you could learn a lot from it and impress your teacher and friends.

Martin
