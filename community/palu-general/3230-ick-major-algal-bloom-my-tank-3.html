kry... NEVER stir that substrate in that tank... anaerobic areas stirred up can cause big issues also... your snails keep it from being anaerobic... it's their job... unless you have larger than normal gravel... if so just do your gravel vac and move any "decorations" every couple months... besides pulling out more than the vac will will take out food for the plants roots... new bulb maybe... add co2 maybe... dose flourish excel with a pipette directly onto algae maybe... very small doses of hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) with a pipette directly onto algae maybe... add root tabs and micros and k2so4 to equal out your nitrate intake so the plants can fight back and win some of the battle maybe... if your tap water is not balanced water changes could fuel the fire... not a bad idea, but it's always possible it could make it worse... i am thinking bright green threads or no... what furnishings do you have... lava rock can create algae pretty bad... you can reduce your light cycle... i have seen where people have had to reduce their light cycle to 6 hrs a day to beat algae... i have also seen tanks that were gorgeous and kept lit 14hrs a day... the 3 day blackout sometimes helps

if you have issues with under your decorations going bad you will need to "stir it up a little" but enough that anything decides to go elsewhere other than into your siphon can cause a bad morning the next day... so this is to be taken very delicately if this situation ever arises... also be very careful with h2o2 (peroxide) or excel... small overdoses can cause massive wipeouts

What I ended up doing was a three day black out. By the end of the three days the algae was 99% gone (I don't mind a LITTLE, it was just getting ridiculus) and my baby mystery snails and my baby ramshorn snails had tripled in size, lol. I was leaving the lights on for about 14 hours a day, and I cut that down to 9 and leave them totally off one day a week and it has been staying under control. I noticed some clinging to the cabomba that was directly under the light and pulled it off last night, but other than that the tank has been doing great.

The only type of algae I have seen was the thread-like stuff and it was on the tops of the cabomba and on the glass. I have trumpet snails in the substrate (They would be great for your tank with the sand, Frogg!) so I didn't really want to stir up the substrate anyway. So I didn't
