Self Sustaining System
Hi everyone.

I am interested in experimenting with the intention of creating a self sustaining paludarium.

I have studied the construction process and am confident that I can handle the construction of the palu.

I would like this to be able to work in a 50 gallon or smaller glass tank.

A small amount of maintenance from me would be, in my opinion, still be acceptable for the experiment to be considered a success. For the most part, I would just want it to exist on its own.

Ideally, the palu would contain plants and animals that reproduce at a rate that is such that the next species up the food chain can survive off of them. That being said, here are my initial thoughts.

Some species of insect would need to be present in the palu. Perhaps something along the lines of a fruitfly. Low maintenance and reproduce quickly. These would be preyed upon by some fish. I was thinking about trying guppies. I have raised guppies in the past and they are very low maintenance. Depending on the tank setup, they can "self-regulate" their own population or it can grow fairly quickly. The guppies would provide food for some sort of predator, perhaps a turtle or tarantula (I think some species eat fish??). I would also like to consider having some sort of land creatures in the palu as well. Maybe some frogs/toads or geckos. These could provide food for another predator (tarantula?).

I have not been able to think up some creature that would be able to control plant growth.


I know that the limiting factor in this experiment will be figuring out how to balance everything.

Im hoping that some people here that have more experience with these types of plants and animals can offer me some advice on my project.

Id like to get a more carefully developed plan together before I attempt this and Im hoping the people on this forum can help me get to the point of actually attempting it.

i think tom is offering good insight although i'm sure there's a possibility that you could get at least close to the results you're looking for. i am quite confident that you'll have to have a tremendous amount of patience and be fine with repetative failures to accomplish your end product.

spiders throw a huge amount of slings. even considering them you'd probably need a viv measured by fractions of an acre instead of inches imo. with them involved in the food chain i'd think your project just went from measuring price by dollars/thousands straight to millions. not necessarily just for the spiders but for accomodations and stocking. feeding hundreds to thousands of slings (the natural way) is going to add up to tons of complications if you want the feeder species to survive.

regardless what the bottom line is i think the best place to start would be to choose a local and work specifically with that.

good luck... you'll need it!

If its not "sealed" ie you let in air, its MUCH more forgiving.

I currently have a sealed aquatic 2.5 liter tank thats been running for 5-6 years no food, air or any additional resources, just add light. No joke, i know a guy who had one for 18 years though.. freaking unreal!

For what your talking about dont think "I will never add or take anything from the system. The best attitude to take is "see how long it will go." if its a palu it will be tricker but shouldn't be too bad I wouldnt be surprised if you could get one going maybe a year without doing anything? It will look like hell at the end let me tell you...
