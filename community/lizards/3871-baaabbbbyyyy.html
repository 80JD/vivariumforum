if you have an extra uth laying around you can put one under it with a towel on top to keep it from getting too hot. BE VERY CAREFUL WITH THAT THOUGH. a corded lamp dimmer should help keep that at a low enough temp so you don't start a fire. i would put it on some egg crate or something with ventilation holes for safety also. right now i have 2 land hermit crabs in a critter keeper 1 gallon. i know it's small, but it's temporary. i keep them between 70f-75f by keeping the enclosure about 6" away from the outside of my clamp lamp on top of pj's cage. the clamp lamp in question holds a 100 watt ceramic heat emitter. it works well. critter keepers also tend to have holes (slits) in the lids. if you were able to cover that in a way that's safe that should help. the first thing that comes to mind is aluminum foil over about 2/3 of the top. might also be a good idea to use a rope heater (like a substrate heater) around the lower part of the cage. you can use old rags or kitchen towels to shield the plastic from the heat so the little guy don't get burned. for a more permanent solution you can put it on some wax paper and spray gs around it and wrap a rope heater in that while it's drying. if it is fairly new and has no scratches it might not be too hard to remove when you're done also. idk about getting your rope heater out though. another solution is to put the critter keeper on a metal wire shelf and place a clamp lamp underneath.

i hope this sparks a thought at least.

you can also break up one of the various "worms" or mealies into smaller pieces for him to eat for now. not much nutrition, but it should fatten him up a bit... fat babies are healthy babies imo.

if you have a compost bin or some old fruit, collect some FFs with a net (if you use old fruit, put it somewhere warm... you might attract some with some beer or wine or even fruit juice as well). you can also collect some springtails from your garden soil or potted plants, some tiny rollie pollies, tiny worms (they'll be white or clear, you can attract them with something actively fermenting), tiny snail (preferably baby snails rather than ones that stay little, as their shells will be thinner), baby slugs (can sometimes be found hanging out on mama slug. lizards LOVE slugs, so you may want to culture some), drain flies (if you ever see any) would be the perfect size... basically anything you can find that is smaller than it's head, as long as you don't use pestisides. with crawlies that small, you shouldn't have to worry too much about whether you neighbors use pestisides, you'd have to worry more about the fliers. if you are worried about potential parasites, you can feed the worm, slugs, snails, and rollie pollies yellow cornmeal until they poop yellow (or you can see yellow throgh the worms). IMO, even though your feeders will be in tomorrow, you should still catch some of these critters and culture them, since it offers a more varried diet and the lizards would be eating them in the wild. you can feed them to the adults too. you might also consider some feeder roaches, as the babies are very small and there is very little risk of them living outside of thier container. just don't grab any ants.
