Aren't leos usually kept on sand? I plan to keep my bearded dragon on sand when he is a bit older. Not calci-sand though, from what I read it makes them want to eat it because they enjoy the flavor (and they lick stuff alot). I'l be using sifted, washed play sand (I made sure what I bought was not treated with a mold inhibitor or anything).

This has seemed to turn into an argument about calci-sand and what type of heating is best... I think Nighsliv was just showing the new babies. While I understand that calci-sand has been shown over and over to cause impaction because the herps eat it (and I'm talking on purpose, since they flavor the stuff, like fruit, I believe), are we 100% that is even calci-sand? Is it Nighsliv? I'm not saying it isn't, but I AM saying that it has been assumed to be. Fine grained, spherical sand grains are far less likely to cause impaction that regular square grains. Sure, there is more of a risk with this type of substrate than with newspaper or tiles, but there is risk with any substrate that can be consumed. There is even risk of impaction with the foods we feed our herps. Impaction is always a risk, but with proper hydration, supplementation, and monitoring, it can be avoided. Now, on to the bulbs. Herpmad, what exactly is your reasoning behind discouraging the use of a black light bulb for heat? I use black bulbs for nighttime heat in all of my enclosures that require it, other than my BP tank, since I cannot get a 25W black bulb (he has a heat mat, so I only need to boost the temps). I choose this OVER red bulbs. The wattage is higher, allowing me to more easily reach the temps I need at night, and the black light cannot be seen by the herps either (black light is actually purple, btw). Even if it is somewhat visible (which is possible with the mix of red and blue light), the blue light produced will just mimic moonlight, while the red part will not be visible. I sincerely doubt that it is pitch black dark at night where these lizards come from. They probably benefit from a tiny bit of light at night that mimics moonlight. IMO, a heat mat is only necessary for animals that will not bask under a light. The light warms the substrate producing ample belly heat (this can be maintained better by placing a flat rock under the light source) that is more like what would be found in nature. Of course, with a heat mat, you can provide unvarying belly heat 24/7. I guess it really depends on the preference of the keeper, since both are sufficient for most lizards (that will bask under a light).

Ok, I think I've had my say...

While the bulb is black, the light it produces is ALWAYS purple. This is the same with an incandescent style light and a tube light. I know leo's don't bask under the light the same way a beardie does, lol, that would be hard for them to learn when the sun isn't out when they are basking... I totally agree with the cost issues associated with heat mats, especially when you buy all of the necessary equipment (like a thermostat). That's why I use bulbs, they are cheap to buy, cheap to replace, and easy to regulate (whether buy using a dimmer or changing the wattage of the bulb). The only tank I have a heat mat on is my BP, and that will be changing as soon as I get the enclosures built that I am planning. I'll have him over the beardie tank, and the heat from the beardie's bulbs will heat the bottom of his tank. So see, you can even make a box to go under a leo's tank that houses bulbs to get heat from below without the expense of a heat mat.
