Um, as for the silicone application try and use one hand with a glove to smeer it on, and use your other hand to squeez the tube, aswell as put the co-co onto it. I usually work in about 4x4" sections to avoid the silicone skinning over. Also make sure that the co-co is 100% bone dry.

I honestly dont think I'll be doing the silicone/co-co method again though. Ive noticed is has slowly started to flake off and fall into my water section (from the waterfall). It isnt a huge deal since a lot of it is still intact but if it continues to degrade I may have to redo it.

As for the potted plants, I had bought them without thinking about where I was gonna have them planted. I love pitcher plants so when I saw them I needed to buy them! When I redo this tank I may end up putting one of them in the land area, and put the other in my 2nd viv.

You should of seen how bright the colours were when I woke him up during the day to put him in the viv...INSANE! The blue was super dark yet bright, and there was no green colour anywhere past the yellow stripes on his side. Next time I need to wake him during the day, I'll be sure to snap a quick pic before he goes back to sleep.

The odd thing is that the owner said he only fed him 3-4 crickets every 2-3 days...I'll be tossing in a bunch of gutted and dusted crickets every 2 days to make sure he stays this chubby and healthy.

Yeah it definately does stay clear...I get comments all the time on the other forum I'm on asking how I keep my water so clean. My filter, my shimp/snails, and my natural waterfall filtering all help, but the main reason is that there are no tannins from the soil getting into my water.

Because of my leaking barrier I syphon out the land area every 5-7 days, then fill the water section up to the top again. It is a pain but at least it ensures fresh water being added to the tank every week. I do syphon out waste and fallen co-co, clean out my filter spong, and add new carbon every month or so, but thats about it

Edit: The fine sand helps a lot aswell. It is consistent in colour, and all unwanted particles stay ontop of its surface making them easily syphoned out.
