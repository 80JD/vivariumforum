i forget who said it (username, i remember who, just can't remember the username) but whenever i think of short t8/t12 bulbs i'm always reminded of (i want to say his real name is paul) when he mentioned attending a lecture and the lecturer said 6" at either end of the bulb produces less light and to put your higher light plants near the middle. my first thought is that say you had a 2' bulb... 6" at either end is 1'... half the bulb. if you have a shop light (any length) that's been in use for some time you can look at the bulb and substantiate that claim by the blackened ends yourself. i don't know if that accounts more for the quality/brightness of the lights than just the fact that their more in demand but it's definitely a big factor IMO.

that's it... i think it was pavel.

using short shop light bulbs bothered me before then but more so since. that's for sure.

i think that might account for a shorter use time also since it'd be directly related to heat and heat would be directly related to usable life. i may be over-thinking that one though.
