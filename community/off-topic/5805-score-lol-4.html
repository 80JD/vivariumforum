your nitrates are fine... about where you want them if you're adding co2 soon anyway.
your gh should be fine too. a little high in the case you want some liverworts maybe but i don't think directly in the water it'd matter much... not sure on that one. it's lack of that that would contribute to osmotic shock in fish or plants in most cases since many soft water species should adapt fine usually.

***angels might be a different story... i know practically nothing about them***

kh is fine... before you add co2... afterward it could be stripped pretty good and result in unpredictable ph swings. a daily swing is fine but going too low can hinder your beneficial bacteria (from i think dissolving oxygen) and potentially kill off some of your colony.
iron, phosphate and potassium are not always essential. if you're dosing low/sparingly/pps pro or experiencing algae/melt/issues/etc. you might want to know them. or just iron if you suspect it's high from the tap and have experienced deaths in your tank.
for kh i'd add a 1/2tsp to every 5 gallons of w/c water and add it slow like acclimating new fish... cause that's what you'll be doing, acclimating them to the higher kh/ph/tds/alkalinity. once your up close to or over 5dkh you can start adding co2 and monitoring kh to see how it dissolves and adjust your w/c mix or schedule accordingly.

right now your ph should be low with those numbers. that holds almost all your available ammo as ammonium which is less toxic to fish. as you raise that number more of it becomes full on ammonia so change your kh slowly over time or wait till you're not reading any ammo on your test kit. i'm sure the hobby kit you have measures total nh3/nh4 as one category and there are calculators that will tell you what percentage of that is ammonia according to your ph online if you're really into doing the technical side of math. your ammo/trite levels at this point are concerns regardless. you don't want them going any higher if you can avoid it.

one thing i would do is run tests on your tap water too. doing water changes is usually the best way to rid your tank of harmful chemicals whether natural to a tank or not but knowing if they're already in the water can be the difference between poisoning the tank while trying to help or not. for instance adding tap water with .5 ppm ammo is not going to be better than .25 ammo and .25 nitrite since the tank would be indicating it's doing it's job at that point.

definitely test the tap for ammo and trite and if you don't find any do water changes daily. i'd start with 50% at this point and try to do enough to keep both ammo and trite <.25 at all times. if you need to you can dose the entire tank with prime to detoxify it for short periods to get you through.

if i missed something, let me know.
