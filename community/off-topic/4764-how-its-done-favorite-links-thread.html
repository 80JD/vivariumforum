i will start by saying that tom barr once told me "a clever monkey would dose both" meaning the substrate and the water column. i concur.

cycle products for the most part are JUNK!

aquarium water is a great fertilizer. i have a dead lawn and bush that have been resurrected to prove it.

EDIT: fertilizers for terrestrials are much different than those for aquatics. the levels are not balanced correctly which could prove dangerous in ways varying from excessive algae to deaths in your vivarium or aquarium. off the top of my head the first thing that comes to mind is urea as a source of nitrogen/ammonia. this and other compounds found in terrestrial mixes are potentially extremely hazardous in several ways that are not conducive to a natural environment.

for ferts in a planted tank there are a few methods... the "natural planted tank" (npt or the walstad method), pps pro, ei, etc....
the one thing they ALL have in common is for overall health and algae control you want enough of each fert to keep the plants healthy but not so much as to harm your inhabitants.

for ferts i would not pay for bottles of water. for example what you'd find at your lfs. you'll get a year or 2 worth for the same price as a month or so if you go with dry ferts.

a few of my favorite places for ferts...
www.aquariumfertilizer.com ... best prices, service and selection... everything you'd need except baking soda. also the only place to get a source of gh (magnesium and calcium... barr's gh booster... great if you use r/o and necessary for plant health)
Fertilizers For The Planted Tank | Ferts second best but shipments don't always come timely and price is just a little higher.
Micro & Macro | Aquarium Fertilizer | Green Leaf Aquariums good starter pack but most expensive of the 3 and skimps on quantities to lower price... after shipping it's more expensive for less though.

James' Planted Tank Web Page this is an overall overview type site/blog. imo it's the best little no-frills no junk source for great information. there's a lot there in a little area. calculators, explanations, tricks, algae overview that's excellent. no bull... straight forward... no need to wade through the crap!

best forum on the net (in english) for plants/aquariums, etc. ...
Aquarium Plants and the Barr Report - Aquatic Plants. membership is reasonable ???and lifetime??? and gives you access to articles and other features. it's run by tom barr and maintained by gregg watson. there's very little that could be done to get such information in abundance to compare. note: you can use the forum without a subscription.

best root tab recipe i've found on the net. saves a whole bunch of money and works like a charm! DIY - Root Tabs - Aquaria Central

dosing... keep target ranges in mind!
Estimative Index Dosing Guide - Fertilizing - Aquatic Plant Central
Dosing Dry Fertilizers
EI light: for those less techy folks - Aquarium Plants - Barr Report
Welcome To The UK Aquatic Plant Society - The Estimative Index (EI)

EDIT: i start at half ei with my tanks. i find since i run low light (most likely would be the case in a palu) that i can lower one nutrient at a time slowly until i eliminate what i don't need. then from there i can lower my doses and have a little more freedom on maintenance and save a buck doing it. i work in 2-3 week periods between each change and never change more than one thing. i keep an eye on both flora and fauna during each change. everyone must be considered!
