Ethics of Collecting Wild Plants and Animals
Hello Everybody,

This can be a touchy subject, so let's keep it civil.

I'm an avid gardener and orchid grower, and new to vives. In orchid circles, collecting wild plants is kind of taboo. Over collection has impacted many species, and this has put a stigma on any collection. But collecting wild plants is how we have saved some species, and it's how there are plants for me to grow now.

I'm trying to get a feel for how the Herp world views this topic. Is collecting native Herps more accepted? Is it totally unaccepted? I imagine that many Herps have felt similar collection pressure, and I am curious what some experienced vive gurus think about the topic.

If you are removing native species, one individual removed from a population (if it's a female) means no offspring, no next generation. That's how I think of it. Native vertebrates that are rare should never be removed from their habitat unless they are sick, and even then it's questionable unless you are a wildlife rehabber.

I realize this is an old post but I want to throw my 2 cents worth in. Like many of you I have raided the fields and forests for plants and wildlife eversince I was young. Alot changed when I got into highschool between Agriculture,Horticulture and Biology classes I got a better understanding of how things 'mesh' together and what is good and bad for the eco system. That is when I became much more selective on what I took out, left behind or destroyed.

For example if it was anything that was very common that is not invasive I would take it. If it was something invasive it would be destroyed, if it needed to be I reported to the wildlife dept. ,some fish, mollusks, and plants had to be reported so they could try to figure how much futher they traveled,if it is in a new area and so on. If it was an animal that was protected I would contact the wildlife dept. to see if I was allowed to assist the survivability. I done that a few times with Alligator snapper nests by making a cage to go around and over the nests to protect the eggs from predators until they hatched.
