Amphibian Rule Change--Lacey Act
DOI/FWS RIN: 1018-AX05 Publication ID: Spring 2010
Title: ¤Injurious Wildlife Evaluation; Amphibian Trade and Chytrid Fungus
Abstract: We are reviewing a petition to add all traded live amphibians or their eggs to our list of injurious wildlife under the Lacey Act unless certified as free of Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis (chytrid fungus). The importation and introduction of live amphibians infected with chytrid fungus into U.S. Natural ecosystems may pose a threat to the interests of U.S. Agriculture, fisheries, and commerce, as well as to the welfare and survival of wildlife and wildlife resources. For live amphibians or their eggs infected with chytrid fungus, an injurious wildlife listing would prohibit the importation into, or transportation between, States, the District of Columbia, the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, or any U.S. Territory or possession by any means, without a permit. Permits may be issued for scientific, medical, educational, or zoological purposes.

This is going through under the guise of controlling chytrid, which is is a legitimate concern. The problem will be determining exactly how animals can be certified as chytrid-free.

What is stated here is that animals that are certified to be free of chytrid can be freely traded--those that are not will be subject to Lacey Act injurious species restrictions.

Just thought I'd point out this bit so that it's a little easier to read:

Quote:
For live amphibians or their eggs infected with chytrid fungus, an injurious wildlife listing would prohibit the importation into, or transportation between,... or possession by any means, without a permit. Permits may be issued for scientific, medical, educational, or zoological purposes.
Note that the permit requirement is only for animals infected with the fungus, not for clean animals. Permits would be required for those needing to study the fungus and that sort of thing, I imagine. The permit issue wouldn't affect hobbyists, unless they wanted to house infected amphibs (doubtful).

Personally, as long as they keep it worded that way, I wouldn't have a problem with it. It will promote captive breeding of clean animals and help reduce the chytrid problem. It would also leave more of these animals in their native habitats so that there is a larger population to overcome the threat of the fungus.

It would however, increase the price of imported specimens, since there will have to be some (likely expensive) test run on each animal before it can enter the country.


That said, maybe if they would give the FUNGUS an injurious wildlife listing, with a requirement that all imported amphibians be tested before entering the country, rather than the amphibs themselves, this would turn out better in the end if passed. Otherwise this gives them a toe hold in the future to ban the animals outright.
