Cricket Food Recipes
There are many recipes out there, but they all seem to contain some basic ingredients. I thought i might be helpful to have this in our new feeder section.

Basic Ingredients:

- High protein dog food, meat must be the primary ingredient

- Powdered milk

- Fish food, without ethoxyquin

- Vitamin supplements, dusting powders work well, as do human multivitamins


Other Ingredients:

- Italian seasoning

- Parsley, dried

- Basil, dried

- Vegetable noodles

- Oatmeal

- Whole grain cereals

- Dried seaweed

- Peanuts

- Grits
Amounts used vary, but definitely use dog food as the main ingredient. if any one wants to post their recipe, please do. I will add any new ingredients as they are posted.

I had bad results with the Flukers cricket food from the pet store. Lots of deaths. I'll be mixing up some new gutload powder in the next few days, I'll post whatever I come up with. I'll try to take exact measurements or at least ratios. We have a food dehydrator now, so I may dehydrate some greens and whatnot and then powder it and add it in.

Fish food is definitely good to feed them, and they LOVE it. If you can, try to find one without ethoxyquin. It's a harmful preservative. The goldfish food that I have doesn't have it in it, but they seem to prefer the tropical fish food over the gold fish food. I keep a dish of fish food in there for my tiny baby crickets.

As for the dog food, if you buy a really high quality dog food for your cricket feed, you don't have to buy a whole bag. Most of the high quality foods come in trial sized bags for just a couple of dollars, and it will be plenty for your recipe. You should check some of the online dog food stores and the websites of the manufacturers too, as they often give away free samples, sometimes with free shipping as well. You might be able to find coupons online for free bags that size too. Sometimes it doesn't look like they are eating it at all, but they really are, they just take tiny tiny bites. They seem to like it best if it is in a shallow dish with a single layer of the dog food (if you are feeding just the dog food and not a ground up powder containing dog food).

If you can get it, some stores that sell bulk spices sell calcium powder. You can substitute the calcium powder for the powdered milk.

Dusting powders definitely work well for vitamin supplementation in the gut load, but you can also get/use people vitamins as well. Centrum is a good one. Just grind the pills into a powder, sift out any chunks, and add to the gutload. It generally comes out cheaper in the long run that way.

It should be noted as well, if using dog food for the bulk of the gutload, it is recommended to refrigerate what you aren't currently using to prolong the shelf life.

IMO, the best dog food to add is Orijen. It's got a high protein content, no grains, and is made from human grade all natural meats. I use it for my dog. Another good one I use for my dog is By Nature. Blue Buffalo also makes a food that is comparable to Orijen, but it has one grain in it.
