Dermestid Beetles- WTF????
i went down to the feed store today to pick up crickets, typically they have robust healthy crickets. as usual i examine the bugs before leaving and notice small hairy larvae in the bag with the crickets. i ask the store clerk, and he says thats it a bug that comes in with the crickets some times... no kidding... since he was no help i asked for the name of the company that he oredred them from.

I called the breeder and found out that they are dermestid beetles, of which some species are used to clean skulls!! he couldnt tell me which type they were but he was pretty sure they came in with the cricket food. then i asked the big question, do they sell the carnivorous species? They used to, but not any more, and he couldn't tell me why. i have my suspicions...

my other concern was that they are hairy and the little hairs may become lodged in the animals mouth or intestines, potentialy harming them.

Anyway, i decided not to risk infecting my tanks with carnivorous beetles. at least i can keep the crickets away from my pets with a dish of food. i would hate to wake up to find a perfectly cleaned salamander skeleton.

does anyone have experience with these bugs? are they safe to use as feeders?

in case you are curious this is sorta what they look like Dermestid Beetle
the larve looked most like the ones in the pic, compared to the other results i got from google, and those ARE the carnivorous ones

They are most likely Dermestes maculatus. Many crickets breeders keep them in their colonies to clean up the dead crickets. It extremely unlikely that they will attack and kill an animal, they are carrion eaters. Hence their use for cleaning skulls. Also, they are more likely to just die in your tank than become a breeding colony, mostly due to a lack of food (carrion).
I've never specifically used them as feeders, but I am sure I have inadvertently fed out the occasional larvae along with the crickets. I don't believe they would be harmful if consumed. Your salamanders would consume even stranger and potentially 'harmful' bugs in the wild!
